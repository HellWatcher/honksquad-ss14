tree, and — for the 447 units assessed so far — what it does to a round.
A row says a fork *has* the feature; only the shortlist says anything about taking it.

assessments trying to refute them and corrected the graft shape of 70,

the path data: it indexes the directories a fork *claims*, so it cannot see upstream files

**[Cosmic cult](FORK-CATALOG/antag.md#cosmic-cult) — deltav**

*structural · drop-in · high confidence · 3 forks carry it*

A team of converted crew quietly drains "entropy" from living people (a touch that steals a bit of their health over time), carries it back to an invisible Monument hidden somewhere on the station, and feeds it in to grow the Monument through three stages. Each stage unlocks new powers the cult buys individually — freezing someone in place, blasting doors open, going invisible as a floating spirit, becoming immune to vacuum, healing near the Monument — plus chalk glyphs that convert bystanders standing on them, turn spacesuits into cult armour, turn knives into cult weapons, and turn a portable scrubber into a spire that eats the room's air. Cultists who find and absorb the rifts that start appearing become permanently empowered and can trap a borg in a shell that hatches into a huge Entropic Colossus, or overwrite the station AI's laws to serve the cult; the crew fights back with mindshields, the chaplain's bible and a censer that deconverts, and if the cult finishes the final ritual at the fully-powered Monument the station is destroyed and the round ends.

- **Take** — It is a complete, fully documented round type with its own escalation curve, counterplay and losing condition — a whole alternate way for a shift to go, not another uplink antag.
- **Skip** — It is roughly 300 files of bespoke systems (monument, glyphs, transmutation, influences, astral forms, colossus mob, AI lawset corruption, borg capture) that hooks mindshields, bibles, the AI upload console and borg chassis, so it is a permanent maintenance surface that will fight every upstream merge touching those systems.
- **Touches** — antagonist, all-players, security, epistemics, science, chaplain, silicon, command, ghost-roles
- **Verified** — supported; assessed as needs upstream edits, corrected by the verifier. The gameplay description (entropy siphon, three-stage Monument, purchasable influences, glyph conversion/transmutation, deconversion via censer/bible/mindshield, Colossus mob, AI-law/borg corruption, station-destroying final ritual) is directly confirmed by the guidebook XML and prototype/system files I fetched myself. But the graft_shape verdict is overclaimed: a full recursive tree search of the DeltaV repo (299 cosmic/cult-tagged paths) found zero files outside _DV namespace directories, and reading the actual C# (DeconversionSystem.cs, CosmicFragmentationSystem.cs) shows the 'hooks' into mindshields/bible/borg/AI-laws are done entirely via SubscribeLocalEvent from the fork's own self-contained files consuming stable, already-existing upstream components/events — no upstream file was edited to register or wire in the feature (registration itself, e.g. game_presets.yml, is also under _DV). That is much closer to the seed's original 'drop-in' than the assessor's downgrade suggests, with the caveat that it is a large, deeply-coupled maintenance surface if those upstream APIs ever change shape.
  - unsupported: "needs-upstream-edits" as the graft_shape: the repo tree (299 files matched, all under _DV directories; zero non-_DV files mention "cosmic" anywhere in the repo) shows the feature is 100% self-contained with no upstream files edited to wire it in, contradicting the 'fork-patches-upstream' reading entirely
  - unsupported: The framing that it 'hooks mindshields, bibles, the AI upload console and borg chassis' implies edits to those systems; in fact the actual C# (DeconversionSystem.cs, CosmicFragmentationSystem.cs) shows it only subscribes to pre-existing upstream events/components (MindShieldComponent, BibleComponent, BorgChassisComponent, SiliconLawUpdaterComponent, AILawUpdatedEvent) from its own _DV files — the upstream files themselves (MindShieldSystem.cs, BibleSystem.cs) contain zero references to CosmicCult and are completely unmodified

**[Alternate round types](FORK-CATALOG/antag.md#alternate-round-types) — serbia-strong**

*structural · drop-in · medium confidence · 1 forks carry it*

Dead players and observers can open an "Arena Lobby" window listing the fights currently running, then create or join one: you get pulled into a small randomly-picked arena map with a randomly-assigned kit, wait out a countdown while barriers hold you apart, and then fight until one side is left standing or the five-minute timer calls a draw, with winners announced over an arena channel. Lobby slots are sorted into duel, creative, social and misc categories and can be one-on-one or small teams, so it works as a parallel side activity for people who are out of the round rather than something the station notices. Separately, the fork ships whole-round faction war events on their own maps, where NT, USSP and Iron Squad crews fly out on faction shuttles, plant and pull each other's flags on capture pedestals over a fifteen-second struggle, earn points for every stretch of time they hold a pedestal, and score a big lump for blowing up an enemy shuttle's console, alongside a handful of themed event maps (a snow holiday village, an orbital village, a cooking event, a rift map).

- **Take** — It solves the two hardest problems at once: dead players get a real, repeatable thing to do instead of orbiting, and the faction-war half gives you a complete team-versus-team event round (capture points, faction shuttles, scoring) you can run on a slow night.
- **Skip** — Every SS220 file here carries a custom EULA/CLA header with a hosting restriction rather than the usual MIT/AGPL, so grafting it into an AGPL fork is a licensing problem before it is an engineering one; on top of that the faction war is only as good as its bespoke maps and shuttles, which you would have to maintain and rebalance yourself.
- **Touches** — ghost-roles, all-players, antagonist, security, admins-and-event-hosts
- **Verified** — supported; assessed as needs upstream edits, corrected by the verifier. Gameplay is unusually well-supported: arena.ftl confirms the lobby verb/window, duel/creative/social/misc categories, countdown, and draw/timeout text; ArenaRuleComponent.cs confirms MaxFightDuration=300s (exactly "five-minute timer") and barrier/countdown fields; ArenaFightBarrierComponent and the ghost-role locale ("has no connection to the station... any interaction outside the arena is prohibited") back the "side activity, station doesn't notice" framing; arena_gamerule.yml + Arenas/*.yml (2v2, 3v3, single maps) confirm "one-on-one or small teams". FractWar checks out equally precisely: EventCapturePointComponent.cs has FlagManipulationDuration=15s exactly matching "fifteen-second struggle" and RetentionTimeForWinPoint=15s for the hold-points mechanic; ShuttleConsolePointsComponent/System confirms PointsOnDestroy=500 for "a big lump" on console kill; the tree shows dedicated NT.yml/USSP.yml/IronSquad.yml faction shuttles and ai_factions.yml with EbentUssp/EbentNanoTrasen/EbentIronSquad, confirming the three-faction claim even though none of the 5 cited evidence files show the faction names directly. All four named theme maps (GorgosiaSnowHoliday.yml, GoladraOrbVillage.yml, CookingEventMap.yml, RiftEventMap.yml) exist verbatim. One overclaim outside the gameplay text: skip_because says "Every SS220 file here carries a custom EULA/CLA header" but Content.Shared/SS220/FractWar/ShuttleConsolePointsComponent.cs (one of the 5 cited files) has no such header at all — it starts directly with a using statement. On graft_shape: I found the FractWar round type registered as a `gamePreset` entirely inside the fork's own Resources/Prototypes/SS220/game_presets.yml, with zero matches for "arena"/"fractwar" in the upstream Resources/Prototypes/game_presets.yml — i.e. the fork did not patch that upstream file in place. Every file I found for this feature (Content.Server/Shared/Client/SS220/{Arena,FractWar,EventCapturePoint}, Resources/{Locale,Prototypes,Maps}/*/ss220/...) lives under the fork's own SS220 namespace, and SS14 prototypes/systems are reflection-discovered rather than centrally registered, so there's no positive evidence integration would even require upstream edits. This contradicts the claim's downgrade to needs-upstream-edits; the seed's drop-in call holds up better than the claim being tested.

**[Objective and uplink store rework](FORK-CATALOG/antag.md#antag-objectives-and-uplink-rework) — stellar-station**

*loop-change · drop-in · medium confidence · 9 forks carry it*

Stellar Station replaces the usual static "your objectives" text list with live goal cards. Each goal is its own entity carrying a name, a description, an icon drawn from an objectives sprite sheet, and a progress value from 0 to 1, and the card redraws the moment the server updates it: a filled bar that shifts colour as it climbs (red-to-green style progress tinting), with a second bar beside it. So an antagonist watches their objective fill in during the round rather than finding out at round end whether they made it.

Goals sit in containers owned by a mind, and a mind can also observe containers it does not own, which is how a team sees a shared board: everyone in the group watches the same goals tick up as any member makes progress. Goals attach automatically when a game rule picks someone as an antagonist.

The shipped content is the Cosmic Cult. Cultists get three goals rolled per round with randomised targets: convert somewhere between 8 and 20 people, siphon 90 to 120 entropy, and reach the fourth and final stage of the monument. There is also a "PERSIST" goal handed to the Colossus, which is satisfied simply by surviving to the end of all things. One small extra: prayer books are registered as a steal target for thieves.

- **Take** — It turns objectives into live, icon-and-progress-bar cards that a whole antag team can watch fill up mid-round, instead of a text list you only reconcile at round end, and the goal types (numeric counter, target-survives, target-self-mind, warp-point target) are generic enough to hang any antag's objectives off.
- **Skip** — The only goals actually shipped are Cosmic Cult ones, so grafting the framework alone gives you an empty progress panel until you write goal prototypes for your own antags; and the file list contains no UI controller that puts the goal cards on screen, which means the display is almost certainly wired in through an upstream menu file you would have to patch and maintain.
- **Touches** — antagonist, all-players
- **Needs** — [cosmic-cult](FORK-CATALOG/antag.md#cosmic-cult)
- **Verified** — supported; assessed as needs upstream edits, corrected by the verifier. All 9 cited evidence files check out (StellarGoalComponent has Progress 0-1 and an Icon; StellarGoalContainerObserverComponent + StellarGoalsSystem.ObserveContainer let a mind watch containers it doesn't own; StellarAntagGoalsSystem attaches goals on AfterAntagEntitySelectedEvent; goals.yml has the exact 8-20 conversion / 90-120 entropy / stage-4 finality / PERSIST-Colossus goals; the prayer-book steal-target locale string exists), and I independently confirmed the \"shared team board\" claim via CosmicCultRuleSystem.cs (one shared SpawnContainer(\"Cosmic Cult\"), every cultist mind calls ObserveContainer on it) and the red-to-green tint via the stock ProgressColorSystem's hue lerp. But the claim's central reason for calling this needs-upstream-edits — \"no UI controller in the file list, so it must be wired through an upstream menu\" — is contradicted by the repo itself: Content.Stellar.Client/Character/StellarCharacterWindow.xaml.cs + StellarCharacterUIController.cs are fork-owned, self-contained, and already display the goal cards by overriding the stock character-menu keybind, with no upstream files patched. The catalogue's own file list for this unit is simply missing that controller.
  - unsupported: "the file list contains no UI controller that puts the goal cards on screen, which means the display is almost certainly wired in through an upstream menu file you would have to patch and maintain" — false: Content.Stellar.Client/Character/StellarCharacterWindow.xaml.cs and StellarCharacterUIController.cs (both fork-owned, not in the unit's evidence list) instantiate StellarGoalControl directly and rebind the stock OpenCharacterMenu key to a self-contained Stellar character window; no upstream file is touched.
  - unsupported: "with a second bar beside it" — the xaml shows PrimaryProgressBar and SecondaryProgressBar as overlapping children of the same PanelContainer (different margins/heights, Secondary starts Visible="False"), not two bars positioned side by side; reads more like a translucent base layer plus a hidden flash/delta overlay than a visible second bar next to the first.

**[Role slot management and join queue](FORK-CATALOG/jobs.md#role-slots-and-join-queue) — cosmatic-drift**

*loop-change · drop-in · medium confidence · 6 forks carry it*

Command gets a "Job Slots Console" they can walk up to and use mid-round: a searchable list of every job on the station, each row showing the job's icon, its name and how many slots are still open, with plus and minus buttons to open another seat or close one off. So the Head of Personnel can add a second engineer slot when the department is drowning, or shut the last security slot so no more latejoiners can take it. Certain jobs are locked and just show a "this job's slots cannot be adjusted" tooltip, and using the console without the right ID access gets you a rejection buzz instead. Separately, on the join side the server can reserve seats for whitelisted players: once the general (non-whitelist) slots are all taken, an unwhitelisted player trying to connect is told the open slots are currently full and to try rejoining later.

- **Take** — Gives command a real in-world lever over station staffing instead of leaving the latejoin roster fixed at round start, and the whitelist-slot condition lets a busy server keep seats for its regulars without hard-closing the door.
- **Skip** — The unit ships no prototypes at all, so the console entity, its machine board and its placement in an office have to be authored and mapped in yourself before a player ever sees it, and handing HoP the power to close job slots invites round-souring griefing that you then have to write policy for.

**[Mail delivery](FORK-CATALOG/cargo.md#mail-delivery) — starlight**

*loop-change · drop-in · medium confidence · 4 forks carry it*

Starlight's addition to mail is a handheld tracker the courier carries. Tap it on a sealed parcel and it reads the addressee off the label, then locks onto that crewmember through the station's suit-sensor network, showing their name and live position in a small window for about a minute before going on a half-minute cooldown. If the sensor net is down, the recipient is off-sensors or dead-quiet, or somebody already tore the parcel open, the readout says so instead of pointing anywhere, so a courier can tell "they are hiding" apart from "this package is already spoiled". Examining the device tells you who it is currently hunting. The fork also writes a set of junk-mail letters that turn up in the post: Syndicate recruitment rants about NanoTrasen's bad guns and squishy commanders, a "take what you want" pitch egging the reader into stealing, and an ASCII-art Soviet enlistment flyer, each with a signature line at the bottom.

- **Take** — It fixes the dull part of mail delivery, wandering the station hunting for one named person, by turning the courier's job into a short tracked chase with a cooldown, and it leans on the suit-sensor net that already exists.
- **Skip** — The unit contains no prototype at all, so nothing in these files actually puts the tracker in anyone's hands, and the whole thing is dead weight on a server where crew routinely switch their suit sensors off; the spam letters are also self-antag bait aimed straight at new players.
- **Touches** — cargo, service, all-players
- **Verified** — supported; assessed as needs upstream edits, corrected by the verifier. The gameplay description matches the code closely and precisely: MailCompanionComponent.TrackingDuration = 1 minute and CooldownDuration = 30 seconds match "about a minute" / "half-minute cooldown" exactly; OnAfterInteract fires ScanDelivery when tapped on a DeliveryComponent target, matching "tap it on a sealed parcel"; TryResolveDeliveryTarget matches the recipient name against SuitSensorStatus entries from the DeviceNetwork suit-sensor feed, matching "locks onto that crewmember through the station's suit-sensor network"; the MailCompanionStatus enum (RecipientUnavailable, TrackingUnavailable, SensorsOff, DeliveryOpened, DeliveryAlreadyOpened) with distinct popup text for each state matches the claim's "the readout says so instead of pointing anywhere" and the "hiding vs already spoiled" distinction; OnExamined pushing the RecipientName matches "examining the device tells you who it is currently hunting." The three spam-letter descriptions (Syndicate guns/commanders rant, "take what you want" theft pitch, ASCII Soviet enlistment flyer) all appear near-verbatim in delivery-spam.ftl, each ending in a signature line as claimed. However, the graft_shape verdict of "needs-upstream-edits" is not supported by the evidence: every fork file cited lives under a _Starlight namespace (Content.Server/_Starlight, Content.Shared/_Starlight, Locale/_Starlight), none patches an upstream directory, and the system's dependencies (Content.Shared.Delivery, Content.Shared.Medical.SuitSensor, Content.Shared.DeviceNetwork) are stock upstream systems the mail-delivery unit itself says ship in core — normal reliance on existing upstream code, not evidence of needing to edit upstream files. The claim's own skip_because ("no prototype at all") is a real gap, but a missing item/entity prototype is an authoring gap the fork would fill with its own new prototype file, not proof the feature requires touching upstream files. Nothing in the evidence shows or implies an upstream file needs editing to wire this in.

**[Baseline midround antagonists](FORK-CATALOG/antag.md#upstream-midround-antags) — serbia-strong**

*loop-change · drop-in · medium confidence · 1 forks carry it*

**[Reagent and reaction expansion](FORK-CATALOG/chemistry.md#reagent-and-reaction-expansion) — cosmatic-drift**

*activity · drop-in · high confidence · 13 forks carry it*

This fork's chemistry additions are built around psychiatric and pain medicine as roleplay props. Each condition gets a mild, standard and strong drug: antidepressants (Serenitol, Neurozenium, Blissifylovene), anti-anxiety meds (Calmafluxine, Tranquinase, Equilibrazine), painkillers (Stubantazine, Soretizone, Agonolexyne), plus an antipsychotic that quiets hallucinations, an anti-amnesia drug, and a shelved stimulant. While one is in your blood you get occasional private messages telling you how you feel ("The world seems a bit brighter", "You forgot what pain feels like"), and a fade message when it wears off. Take too much and the messages turn on you: paranoid shouting, "Run! RUN! HIDE!", or drowsiness you cannot shake. Drinking on your meds makes your body absorb more of them, so alcohol pushes you into overdose, and stronger antidepressants purge weaker ones out of your bloodstream. Heavy use leaves Addictine behind, which nags you for another fix and neutralises charcoal so you cannot simply flush it out. Separately there is a drug-cooking ladder from Mindleaf sap: brew it with water for a relaxing tea, bake it into an euphoric powder, then acid-refine it up through rust, bake and grit, each step more potent and more toxic, with smuggler packets and wrapped packages to craft for moving the product. Medical also gets forearm crutches and single-use TTE medcases colour-coded by injury (burn, radiation, poison, asphyxiation, brute, bleed) that pop out the right supplies for crew who are not doctors.

- **Take** — It is the rare chemistry expansion aimed at roleplay rather than combat: tiered psych meds with self-narrating flow text, alcohol and cross-medication interactions, withdrawal that resists charcoal, and a full drug-cooking and smuggling ladder from a single plant sap.
- **Skip** — The mental-health drugs only produce text popups and small poison ticks, so their whole value depends on a playerbase that wants to roleplay conditions; on a lower-RP server they are twelve reagents nobody prints, and someone has to maintain the interaction web (each drug's thresholds, ethanol rules and purge rules are hand-tuned per reagent).
- **Touches** — medical, chemistry, antagonist, all-players
- **Verified** — supported. I re-fetched medicine.yml, narcotics.yml, the reactions/crafting files, both .ftl locale files, medcase.yml, crutches.yml and ResetParacusia.cs: every named reagent, the three quoted popup strings, the ethanol/purge/Addictine-vs-Charcoal mechanics, the Mindleaf tea-Breakout-Rust-Bake-Grit ladder with smuggler/wrap constructions, and the six colour-coded SpawnItemsOnUse medcases are all present as written. All files sit under _CD namespaces and reference only upstream reagents/APIs, so drop-in is confirmed; the one caveat is that MindleafSap comes from a Mindleaf plant defined in _CD/Hydroponics files outside this unit (depends_on is empty), a fork-owned, trivially copyable botany prototype a graft of the narcotics half must also carry.

**[Species racial abilities](FORK-CATALOG/species.md#species-racial-abilities) — cosmatic-drift**

*activity · drop-in · medium confidence · 12 forks carry it*

Rodentia characters get an "Open cheek storage" action button that opens a tiny 3x1 pouch inside their own mouth, so a rat person can pocket a couple of small items with nothing visible in their hands or bag. It is deliberately leaky: anyone who examines them reads a yellow line saying the person has something in their mouth, and the contents spill onto the floor the moment they get knocked down, disarmed, or take more than a token amount of damage. Living creatures cannot be stuffed in there, and the fork's code also checks whether the mouth is occupied so a full mouth gets in the way of ordinary mouth use.

- **Take** — It is a complete, small smuggling mechanic rather than a free extra pocket: the examine tell plus the drop-on-knockdown/disarm/damage rules give security a real counterplay, so it can be dropped in without becoming the obvious best place to hide contraband.
- **Skip** — It is one action and one 3-slot container, so the whole payoff is a niche flavour pocket for a single species; you also inherit a hand-rolled storage-in-a-container pattern that has to be re-checked against upstream storage and damage events on every sync.
- **Touches** — all-players, security, antagonist

**[Chemical plumbing network](FORK-CATALOG/chemistry.md#chemical-plumbing) — starlight**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 0 forks carry it*

Chemists get a build-out toy: a hand-held Rapid Plumbing Device places hidden floor ducts (on three separate layers, with straight, bend, T, fourway and manifold pieces) and snaps together a chain of fluid machines, so reagents flow between them on their own instead of being ferried by hand in beakers. You pour into an input tank or a sink, then route the fluid through filters that split named reagents onto two outlets, reactors that pull set quantities and heat them until a reaction fires, synthesizers that conjure basic reagents like iron, oxygen and sugar straight out of battery power, buffer and 2000u storage tanks, a smart dispenser that fills a labeled jug automatically, and a pill press that stamps pills or patches at whatever dosage you set (including blending two feeds at a chosen ratio). Floor drains suck up spilled puddles into the network, a disposal unit quietly vaporises whatever you do not want, connectors show red for inlet and blue for outlet and look visibly jagged when you have misaligned a machine, and a plunger flushes anything stuck onto the floor.

- **Take** — It turns chemistry from repetitive beaker-shuffling into a buildable factory the chemist can design, debug and expand over a round, which is the single biggest depth upgrade available to that role and something upstream has no answer for.
- **Skip** — It is a large new node-network subsystem with roughly a dozen machines, six client UIs and its own construction tool, and its own guidebook advertises hooks into upstream ChemMaster, chemical dispenser and reagent grinder, so it will not sit quietly in a fork namespace and will need re-reconciling against upstream chemistry changes forever.
- **Touches** — science, medical, engineering, service
- **Verified** — supported. The two guidebook pages read almost line-for-line match the gameplay claim: RPLD tool building three-layer ducts (straight/bend/T/fourway/manifold), red=inlet/blue=outlet connector coloring with jagged-when-disconnected visuals, plumbing input/sink/drain, 2000u tank and 200u buffer, filter splitting up to 4 named reagents into filtered/passthrough outlets, reactor pulling set quantities and heating to trigger reactions, synthesizer generating iron/oxygen/sugar/etc from battery power, smart dispenser with labeled-jug auto-fill, pill press with configurable dosage and two ratio-mixing inlets for pills/patches, disposal that vaporizes reagents, and a plunger to flush buffers. Nothing in the gameplay text goes beyond what these files show. **[Cortical borer](FORK-CATALOG/antag.md#cortical-borer) — monolith**

*activity · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 1 forks carry it*

A dead player can come back as a tiny brain worm that crawls into a living crewmate's head (blocked by any helmet, and by sugar in the victim's stomach). Once inside it rides along invisibly, listens to everything the host hears, reads their vitals like a health analyzer, and spends a slowly regenerating pool of "chemicals" from a menu to dose them straight into the bloodstream, anything from bicaridine and painkillers to mute toxin, ephedrine, blinding acid or knockout sleep drugs. Every few minutes it can seize the host's body outright for 40 seconds and walk them around, and while in control it can spend a big chunk of its chemical pool to make the host vomit up an egg that another ghost can take over as a new borer. Hosts get freed by drinking sugar to suppress it and then having brain surgery to pull the worm out; borers share a private hive chat with each other.

- **Take** — It is a complete, self-driving parasite role with real counterplay already designed in (helmets block entry, sugar suppresses, brain surgery removes), so it gives ghosts something to do and gives medical a new patient problem without touching the round's win conditions.
- **Skip** — The prototypes lean on several non-upstream systems Monolith already has (a universal-language speaker tag, a collective-mind hive channel, thermal vision, a custom surgery step and a radio-overheard hook), so a graft means either bringing those along or stripping features out, and the role itself hands one player quiet control over another player's body, which needs admin attention and a playtime gate (Monolith uses 50 hours) to not be miserable.
- **Touches** — ghost-roles, antagonist, medical, all-players
- **Needs** — [language-system](FORK-CATALOG/roleplay.md#language-system), [surgery-and-limb-wounds](FORK-CATALOG/medical.md#surgery-and-limb-wounds)
- **Verified** — supported. **[Disease and virology](FORK-CATALOG/medical.md#disease-and-virology) — backmen**

*activity · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 0 forks carry it*

People catch named illnesses like space cold, space flu, bird flew, plague and OwOnavirus, and start sneezing, coughing, vomiting, dropping items or taking slow damage while the sickness spreads to anyone nearby who is not wearing a mask. A medic swabs a patient's mouth, feeds the swab into a diagnoser machine, and gets a printed report naming the disease, whether it is contagious, how resistant it is to spaceacillin, and exactly how to cure it: drink so many units of a specific chemical, rest in bed for a while, run a fever or chill yourself, or just wait it out. Once a disease has been identified it can be loaded into a vaccinator that spends biomass to print vaccines, so the crew can be inoculated before the outbreak reaches them; the nastier strains escalate in stages, with things like the robovirus eventually turning its victim into a cyborg and the xeno strain twisting the body into something else entirely.

- **Take** — It hands medical a genuine second job loop (swab, diagnose, brew the cure, mass-vaccinate) that runs on its own clock and creates crew-wide urgency without needing an antagonist.
- **Skip** — It is a large content system whose infection, cure, effect and diagnosis pieces all have to land together, and it reaches into rotting/miasma plus mob and mask prototypes to work, so it is a long-lived maintenance surface rather than a file you drop in and forget.
- **Touches** — medical, science, all-players, custom-station-events
- **Verified** — supported.
