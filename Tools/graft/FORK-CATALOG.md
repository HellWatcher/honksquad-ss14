tree, and — for the 302 units assessed so far — what it does to a round.
A row says a fork *has* the feature; only the shortlist says anything about taking it.

the path data: it indexes the directories a fork *claims*, so it cannot see upstream files

**[Server rules, space law and SOP guidebook](FORK-CATALOG/roleplay.md#guidebook-rules-and-sop) — corvax, cosmatic-drift, funky, gaby-station-now-dumont-station, goobstation, harmony-station, impstation, monolith, rmc14, serbia-strong, sunrise, viva-station**

*loop-change · drop-in · high confidence · 11 forks carry it*

Opening the in-game guidebook gives players a full shelf of server-written reference material instead of just upstream's mechanical guides: numbered server rules (Cosmatic Drift's Rule 1.0 through 3.9, Harmony's R0-R8 and S1-S3), a written space/corporate law codebook listing named offences with sentences, and a Standard Operating Procedure tree split department by department and often job by job (Funky Station has separate SOP pages for Captain, HoP, Warden, Detective, Chemist, Paramedic, Roboticist, Bartender, Mime, and so on, plus a page per alert level explaining what Green through Delta actually authorise). Security and Command can look up the charge or the procedure mid-round rather than arguing from memory, and a new player can read the server's actual expectations before their first shift. Viva Station goes further with a Command-only hidden SOP: the pages exist but are metashielded, and your character only "knows" them once you physically find a head of staff's issued guidebook, with higher-clearance books unlocking more of the document, which is what gives an RP-led riot something concrete to be leaking. Sunrise and RMC-14 attach onboarding on top of the text (a guided tutorial and new-player popups), which is a much larger build than the documentation itself.

- **Take** — It is pure content in fork-namespaced XML plus guideEntry prototypes, and it is the cheapest way to make Security and Command play to a codified law and procedure instead of improvising a different standard every shift.
- **Skip** — None of the text is yours: SOP, space law and numbered rules only work if they describe your server's actual policy, so grafting is really a rewrite of every page plus permanent upkeep as your rules drift, and nothing here is mechanically enforced.
- **Touches** — all-players, security, command, service, medical, engineering, cargo, science
- **Verified** — supported. Every checkable specific held up: CD's guides.ftl lists exactly Rule 1.0-3.9 plus per-department SOP and a crimes list; Harmony's guides.ftl lists exactly R0-R8 and S1-S3; Funky's sop.yml defines per-job guideEntry pages for every job named (Captain, HoP, Warden, Detective, Chemist, Paramedic, Roboticist, Bartender, Mime) plus Green-through-Delta alert pages whose XML (GreenAlert.xml) does spell out what is authorised; Corvax's CorporateLaw dir has numbered offence chapters plus Punishments.xml/CrimeList.xml; Viva's CommandHiddenStandardOperatingProcedure.xml states verbatim the metashield and the three clearance tiers unlocked by finding specific heads' guidebooks (worth knowing: enforcement is AHelp/admin convention, not a mechanic, which the claim's "metashielded" framing correctly implies); Sunrise has _Sunrise/Tutorial and RMC-14 has _RMC14/NewPlayer, backing the onboarding aside. Drop-in is confirmed at the mechanism level: upstream GuidebookWindow.GetSortedEntries computes roots as all entries ExceptWith children, so parentless fork-namespaced guideEntry prototypes (like Funky's SOP101) surface as top-level guidebook roots with zero upstream edits. One small practical trim: Funky's sop.yml references an OctarineAlert page and alert-level-octarine locale key from its cosmic-cult port, and Violet/Octarine alert levels are fork-specific, so a graft drops or rewrites those child entries — content pruning, not entanglement.

**[Cosmic cult](FORK-CATALOG/antag.md#cosmic-cult) — deltav**

*structural · drop-in · high confidence · 3 forks carry it*

A team of converted crew quietly drains "entropy" from living people (a touch that steals a bit of their health over time), carries it back to an invisible Monument hidden somewhere on the station, and feeds it in to grow the Monument through three stages. Each stage unlocks new powers the cult buys individually — freezing someone in place, blasting doors open, going invisible as a floating spirit, becoming immune to vacuum, healing near the Monument — plus chalk glyphs that convert bystanders standing on them, turn spacesuits into cult armour, turn knives into cult weapons, and turn a portable scrubber into a spire that eats the room's air. Cultists who find and absorb the rifts that start appearing become permanently empowered and can trap a borg in a shell that hatches into a huge Entropic Colossus, or overwrite the station AI's laws to serve the cult; the crew fights back with mindshields, the chaplain's bible and a censer that deconverts, and if the cult finishes the final ritual at the fully-powered Monument the station is destroyed and the round ends.

- **Take** — It is a complete, fully documented round type with its own escalation curve, counterplay and losing condition — a whole alternate way for a shift to go, not another uplink antag.

- **Verified** — supported; assessed as needs upstream edits, corrected by the verifier. The gameplay description (entropy siphon, three-stage Monument, purchasable influences, glyph conversion/transmutation, deconversion via censer/bible/mindshield, Colossus mob, AI-law/borg corruption, station-destroying final ritual) is directly confirmed by the guidebook XML and prototype/system files I fetched myself. That is much closer to the seed's original 'drop-in' than the assessor's downgrade suggests, with the caveat that it is a large, deeply-coupled maintenance surface if those upstream APIs ever change shape.

**[Alternate round types](FORK-CATALOG/antag.md#alternate-round-types) — serbia-strong**

*structural · drop-in · medium confidence · 1 forks carry it*

Dead players and observers can open an "Arena Lobby" window listing the fights currently running, then create or join one: you get pulled into a small randomly-picked arena map with a randomly-assigned kit, wait out a countdown while barriers hold you apart, and then fight until one side is left standing or the five-minute timer calls a draw, with winners announced over an arena channel. Lobby slots are sorted into duel, creative, social and misc categories and can be one-on-one or small teams, so it works as a parallel side activity for people who are out of the round rather than something the station notices. - **Take** — It solves the two hardest problems at once: dead players get a real, repeatable thing to do instead of orbiting, and the faction-war half gives you a complete team-versus-team event round (capture points, faction shuttles, scoring) you can run on a slow night.

- **Touches** — ghost-roles, all-players, antagonist, security, admins-and-event-hosts
- **Verified** — supported; assessed as needs upstream edits, corrected by the verifier. **[Role slot management and join queue](FORK-CATALOG/jobs.md#role-slots-and-join-queue) — cosmatic-drift**

*loop-change · drop-in · medium confidence · 6 forks carry it*

Command gets a "Job Slots Console" they can walk up to and use mid-round: a searchable list of every job on the station, each row showing the job's icon, its name and how many slots are still open, with plus and minus buttons to open another seat or close one off. So the Head of Personnel can add a second engineer slot when the department is drowning, or shut the last security slot so no more latejoiners can take it. Certain jobs are locked and just show a "this job's slots cannot be adjusted" tooltip, and using the console without the right ID access gets you a rejection buzz instead. Separately, on the join side the server can reserve seats for whitelisted players: once the general (non-whitelist) slots are all taken, an unwhitelisted player trying to connect is told the open slots are currently full and to try rejoining later.

- **Take** — Gives command a real in-world lever over station staffing instead of leaving the latejoin roster fixed at round start, and the whitelist-slot condition lets a busy server keep seats for its regulars without hard-closing the door.
- **Skip** — The unit ships no prototypes at all, so the console entity, its machine board and its placement in an office have to be authored and mapped in yourself before a player ever sees it, and handing HoP the power to close job slots invites round-souring griefing that you then have to write policy for.
- **Touches** — command, all-players, server-operators
- **Verified** — partly supported; assessed as needs upstream edits, corrected by the verifier. Every file touched by this feature — C# (Server/Client/Shared), locale, prototypes, and even the maps that place the console — lives under _CD namespaces/directories; nothing outside the fork's own tree was found for it. The whitelist half plugs into an upstream extension point (WhitelistCondition is `[ImplicitDataDefinitionForInheritors]` in vanilla space-wizards/space-station-14, confirmed by fetching the same path from upstream) rather than editing an upstream file. The claim's downgrade to needs-upstream-edits rested on the false premise that no prototypes ship; a git tree/grep of the actual repo shows a full entity+board+flatpack+map chain already exists, so the seed's drop-in path-data read was actually correct here.
  - unsupported: "The unit ships no prototypes at all, so the console entity, its machine board and its placement in an office have to be authored and mapped in yourself before a player ever sees it" — false. The fork ships a complete, wired entity prototype (Resources/Prototypes/_CD/Entities/Structures/Machines/Computers/computers.yml, id ComputerJobSlots, parent BaseComputerAiAccess, AccessReader restricted to HeadOfPersonnel, JobSlotsConsole component with a real blacklist), a matching circuitboard (Resources/Prototypes/_CD/Entities/Objects/Devices/Circuitboards/computer.yml, id JobSlotsComputerCircuitboard), a portable flatpack variant (Resources/Prototypes/_CD/Entities/Objects/Devices/flatpack.yml, id JobSlotsComputerFlatpack) and a tabletop variant (tabletopcomputer.yml, ComputerTabletopJobSlots) — and it is already placed on three of the fork's live station maps (Resources/Maps/_CD/cluster.yml, gemini.yml, aspid.yml).

**[Baseline midround antagonists](FORK-CATALOG/antag.md#upstream-midround-antags) — serbia-strong**

*loop-change · drop-in · medium confidence · 1 forks carry it*

Adds a stealth objective for the space ninja: go the whole round without ever being seen. **[Tape recorder and cassettes](FORK-CATALOG/roleplay.md#tape-recorder) — cosmatic-drift, deltav, goobstation, rmc14, starlight, sunrise**

*activity · drop-in · high confidence · 5 forks carry it*

A pocket-sized tape recorder that you load with a cassette and switch between Record, Playback, Rewind and Pause from a small on-item menu. - **Touches** — security, service, command, all-players
- **Verified** — partly supported; assessed as needs upstream edits, corrected by the verifier. **[Genetics and DNA mutations](FORK-CATALOG/medical.md#genetics-and-dna-mutations) — funky**

*activity · drop-in · medium confidence · 2 forks carry it*

**[Spreading fire](FORK-CATALOG/atmos.md#spreading-fire-system) — ephemeral-space**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 2 forks carry it*

Fire becomes a thing that sits on the floor and creeps: a burning tile crackles with its own flame sprite and sound, slows anyone who walks through it, scorches the floor with burn marks, and once a second tries to jump to a neighbouring tile, preferring tiles that are already hot and skipping ones with no oxygen or nothing flammable to catch. Flames come in four visible intensities and burn themselves down as they spread, eventually smouldering to a weaker flame or dying out on their own, so a blaze that gets going grows into a wandering front the crew has to cut off rather than a single fire they stand next to and extinguish. Anything that triggers, from a thrown incendiary to a rigged device, can drop a fire of a chosen intensity, and every fire remembers who started it, so an arsonist's whole spreading blaze is traceable back to them.

- **Take** — It turns fire from a per-entity status effect into a spatial hazard that moves through the station, giving atmos and engineering an actual firefighting job and giving arsonist play a real tool.
- **Skip** — It is not the self-contained fork-namespaced drop-in it looks like: the prototype leans on flammable fields (firestack visual divisor, minimum/maximum firestacks, delete-on-extinguish, basic fire spread) and a per-tile Flammability value that upstream does not have, so grafting it means patching upstream flammable and tile-definition code and then re-patching them on every sync.
- **Touches** — engineering, atmospherics, security, antagonist, all-players
- **Verified** — supported. Every gameplay beat checks out in the actual code: the ESTileFire prototype has four staged sprites/sounds (red_1..4, fire1..4.ogg), SpeedModifierContacts 0.8/0.8 for the floor-slow, TryAddBurntDecalsToTile for scorch marks, edge_spreaders.yml sets updatesPerSecond:1 for spread cadence, OnSpreadNeighbors weights neighbor tiles up for high temperature and zeroes tiles with tileDef.Flammability<=0 or low oxygen, fires lose firestacks on each spread (AdjustFireStacks negative) and the Update() loop either deletes or downgrades to a weaker stage once SmolderTime passes, ESTileFireOnTriggerComponent lets any BaseXOnTriggerComponent consumer (grenades, rigged devices) spawn a chosen Stage, and Origin/ESTileFireOriginComponent propagate through every child fire spawned by a spread, so a whole blaze traces to one originating entity. I then went beyond the cited evidence to settle the graft_shape question, diffing the fork's own Content.Shared/Atmos/Components/FlammableComponent.cs and Content.Shared/Maps/ContentTileDefinition.cs against space-wizards/space-station-14 master. Both files are NOT under the _ES/ namespace and both carry literal '// ES START ... // ES END' blocks in the fork's own tree: FlammableComponent.cs adds FirestackVisualDivisor, DeleteOnExtinguish, BasicFireSpread (and several atmos-energy fields the tile-fire system doesn't even use); ContentTileDefinition.cs adds the Flammability field the spread-scoring code reads. That is direct proof the fork edits upstream-named files in place, not just files that would need editing to integrate elsewhere - the needs-upstream-edits downgrade from the drop-in seed is correct, and correct in the stronger 'fork-patches-upstream' sense. This doesn't change the verdict (real new fields still force upstream edits) but the claim overstates which specific fields are novel.

**[Chemical plumbing network](FORK-CATALOG/chemistry.md#chemical-plumbing) — starlight**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 0 forks carry it*

Chemists get a build-out toy: a hand-held Rapid Plumbing Device places hidden floor ducts (on three separate layers, with straight, bend, T, fourway and manifold pieces) and snaps together a chain of fluid machines, so reagents flow between them on their own instead of being ferried by hand in beakers. You pour into an input tank or a sink, then route the fluid through filters that split named reagents onto two outlets, reactors that pull set quantities and heat them until a reaction fires, synthesizers that conjure basic reagents like iron, oxygen and sugar straight out of battery power, buffer and 2000u storage tanks, a smart dispenser that fills a labeled jug automatically, and a pill press that stamps pills or patches at whatever dosage you set (including blending two feeds at a chosen ratio). Floor drains suck up spilled puddles into the network, a disposal unit quietly vaporises whatever you do not want, connectors show red for inlet and blue for outlet and look visibly jagged when you have misaligned a machine, and a plunger flushes anything stuck onto the floor.

- **Take** — It turns chemistry from repetitive beaker-shuffling into a buildable factory the chemist can design, debug and expand over a round, which is the single biggest depth upgrade available to that role and something upstream has no answer for.
- **Skip** — It is a large new node-network subsystem with roughly a dozen machines, six client UIs and its own construction tool, and its own guidebook advertises hooks into upstream ChemMaster, chemical dispenser and reagent grinder, so it will not sit quietly in a fork namespace and will need re-reconciling against upstream chemistry changes forever.
- **Touches** — science, medical, engineering, service
- **Verified** — supported. The two guidebook pages read almost line-for-line match the gameplay claim: RPLD tool building three-layer ducts (straight/bend/T/fourway/manifold), red=inlet/blue=outlet connector coloring with jagged-when-disconnected visuals, plumbing input/sink/drain, 2000u tank and 200u buffer, filter splitting up to 4 named reagents into filtered/passthrough outlets, reactor pulling set quantities and heating to trigger reactions, synthesizer generating iron/oxygen/sugar/etc from battery power, smart dispenser with labeled-jug auto-fill, pill press with configurable dosage and two ratio-mixing inlets for pills/patches, disposal that vaporizes reagents, and a plunger to flush buffers. Nothing in the gameplay text goes beyond what these files show. **[Cortical borer](FORK-CATALOG/antag.md#cortical-borer) — monolith**

*activity · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 1 forks carry it*

A dead player can come back as a tiny brain worm that crawls into a living crewmate's head (blocked by any helmet, and by sugar in the victim's stomach). Once inside it rides along invisibly, listens to everything the host hears, reads their vitals like a health analyzer, and spends a slowly regenerating pool of "chemicals" from a menu to dose them straight into the bloodstream, anything from bicaridine and painkillers to mute toxin, ephedrine, blinding acid or knockout sleep drugs. Every few minutes it can seize the host's body outright for 40 seconds and walk them around, and while in control it can spend a big chunk of its chemical pool to make the host vomit up an egg that another ghost can take over as a new borer. Hosts get freed by drinking sugar to suppress it and then having brain surgery to pull the worm out; borers share a private hive chat with each other.

- **Take** — It is a complete, self-driving parasite role with real counterplay already designed in (helmets block entry, sugar suppresses, brain surgery removes), so it gives ghosts something to do and gives medical a new patient problem without touching the round's win conditions.
- **Skip** — The prototypes lean on several non-upstream systems Monolith already has (a universal-language speaker tag, a collective-mind hive channel, thermal vision, a custom surgery step and a radio-overheard hook), so a graft means either bringing those along or stripping features out, and the role itself hands one player quiet control over another player's body, which needs admin attention and a playtime gate (Monolith uses 50 hours) to not be miserable.
- **Touches** — ghost-roles, antagonist, medical, all-players
- **Needs** — [language-system](FORK-CATALOG/roleplay.md#language-system), [surgery-and-limb-wounds](FORK-CATALOG/medical.md#surgery-and-limb-wounds)
- **Verified** — supported. **[Disease and virology](FORK-CATALOG/medical.md#disease-and-virology) — backmen**
