### Resuscitation and life support

<a id="resuscitation-and-life-support"></a>

Keeping a dying patient alive without surgery: chest compressions, blood transfusion equipment and live vitals readouts.

**goobstation**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 3 forks carry it*

This is a grab-bag of keep-them-alive tools rather than one system. A medic can slap a medical patch onto a patient and it drip-feeds its chemicals into them over time (some dump a dose the instant it goes on, and cheap ones crumple into trash once empty), or wield a healing beam gun that pours a continuous heal into a crewmate and charges up a meter as it works, which can then be spent on a short burst of supercharged healing on both of you. The health analyzer readout gains a total vital damage line plus disease information, telling you the genotype, how far the infection has progressed and how much immunity the patient has built. Elsewhere, an officer can spend about eight seconds scanning a crewmate to slip in a command tracking implant so they show up on crew monitors and call out when they die, and anyone who takes a nasty shock has their suit sensors randomly knocked to a different setting, so the monitoring console quietly starts lying about where they are until someone resets it.

- **Take** — The medical patch and healing-beam pieces give medics something to do on a patient who is not on an operating table, which is exactly the gap upstream leaves between "inject chems" and "do surgery".
- **Skip** — It is four loosely related mini-features stapled together, and the parts that matter most to players (the analyzer disease and vital-damage readouts, the suit sensor scrambling) plug into upstream health analyzer and suit sensor code, so you inherit merge friction on upstream files for what is otherwise small self-contained content.
- **Touches** — medical, command, security, all-players
- **Needs** — [disease-and-virology](#disease-and-virology), [surgery-and-limb-wounds](#surgery-and-limb-wounds), [character-traits](roleplay.md#character-traits)

  - unsupported: which can then be spent on a short burst of supercharged healing on both of you -- MedigunSystem.cs only calls _damage.TryChangeDamage on entities in HealedEntities (the beamed target(s), default MaxLinksAmount=1); the wielder/ParentEntity is never passed to the damage-change call, so uber mode heals only the target(s) being beamed, not the medic wielding the gun.

**sunrise**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 3 forks carry it*

The crew monitoring console gets a lot louder and more usable. A doctor can switch the console to alarm mode, and every fifteen seconds it will beep an alarm if anyone wearing a working suit sensor is dead or in crit anywhere except inside a morgue, so the medbay hears about a body in maintenance instead of noticing it twenty minutes later. The list can be filtered down to only the people you care about: by health band (healthy, hurt, badly hurt, critical, dead) and by department, with the option to hide implant trackers. Separately, a medical borg's hypospray speaks aloud what it just injected into whom (with a short cooldown so it does not spam), and some entities carry a healing aura that quietly ticks damage off nearby mobs every two seconds within a few tiles, capped so it stops once the target is healthy enough.

- **Take** — The corpse alarm plus department/health filtering turns the crew monitor from a wall of dots you squint at into something that actively tells medical where the bodies are, which is the single biggest quality-of-life lift the console can get.
- **Skip** — It is not one feature but three loosely related ones bolted together (console alerts, borg hypospray voice lines, a generic healing aura), and the console half is a partial class hanging off upstream's CrewMonitoringConsoleSystem and its UI, so it rides along with every upstream crew-monitor refactor; the alarm also reuses an SMG empty-magazine sound as its siren and has no locale of its own, with Russian-only comments throughout.

**rmc14**

*loop-change · entangled · high confidence · 3 forks carry it*

A medic can drop to their knees beside a dying marine and pump their chest by hand, buying roughly seven seconds of extra revival time per cycle and clawing back suffocation damage, with a nag popup if they push too fast ("Incorrect rhythm. Do it slower.") and faster compressions if they are trained. Around that sit wheeled IV stands you drag onto a patient to either pump blood in or draw it out, blood packs you jab into someone's arm by hand, portable dialysis units that scrub chemicals out of a bloodstream, and stretchers that can call a dropship down for a medevac pickup. In the field hospital, a sleeper pod console gives a live readout of the occupant's name, health, temperature, pulse, blood level and brute/burn/toxin/oxygen split, and lets the doctor inject measured doses of stocked chemicals, run dialysis, or set the pod to spit out anyone who dies in it; handheld health scanners and body scanners show the same vitals plus wounds and whether the patient can still be shocked back at all.

- **Take** — Gives medics a full pre-surgery toolkit - hands-on CPR, transfusions, dialysis, live vitals and a pod that keeps someone stable - so a crit patient becomes an ongoing job rather than a defib prompt.
- **Skip** — It is wired into RMC-14's own skill tiers, wound model, custom bloodstream/pulse/reagent-metabolism systems and even marine-vs-xeno checks, so almost none of it works without dragging those in or rewriting each gate against upstream equivalents.

**backmen, frontier, funky, harmony-station, impstation, serbia-strong**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 3 forks carry it*

This is a grab-bag of small additions around bringing dead people back. SS220 caps how many times one person can be defibrillated: after two revives you start taking brain damage a few minutes after each one, and there is a good chance of picking up a lasting ailment from the death, so a body that keeps dying eventually stops being worth saving. Funky Station adds a wheeled crash cart that medics roll to the patient with a compact defib and an oxygen (or nitrogen, plasma, laughing gas) tank slotted into visible holders, plus privacy curtains, auto-injector cartridge cases, a body scanner wired to the operating table, and objects that trickle healing into whoever is on them. Backmen replaces plain cloning with reincarnation: come out of the machine and you may be a Felinid, Oni, Arachne, Moth, Vox, or even a Skeleton instead of what you were, weighted so the strange ones are rare. Harmony and Frontier add the counterpart flag that marks certain bodies as simply unclonable, and Impstation adds a crew monitor that keeps reading vitals from far outside normal range.

- **Take** — The revive cap is the single highest-value piece here: it puts a real cost on dying and turns the defibrillator from an infinite undo button into a resource, without needing any new department or content.

### Mob damage, movement and gravity

<a id="mob-damage-and-movement"></a>

Baseline damage types and groups, crit and death thresholds, movement modifiers, pulling, climbing, buckling and gravity handling for mobs.

**rmc14**

*loop-change · entangled · medium confidence · 1 forks carry it*

Combat feels heavier and more punishing: getting flung into a wall or crate at speed hurts you for real, staggers you, flashes you red and bounces you back, with the damage scaling off how big you are and how fast you were moving. Being downed is no longer a safe holding pattern either — while you are in crit you can go deaf, your vision blurs out, and someone dragging you across the floor keeps chipping away at you, so a rescue is a race rather than a formality. Around that sit tuned armour and material toughness values (barricades, sandbags, girders, foam walls, vehicle plating, alien resin and weeds each resist different damage types), plus health HUD icons that let medics read at a glance whether a body is fresh, nearly unrecoverable, or flagged do-not-resuscitate, and colour-coded triage holocards pinned over patients.

- **Take** — The obstacle-slam and crit-degradation pieces make fights and rescues dramatically more tense, and the triage HUD icons give medics real information instead of a guess.
- **Skip** — It is a whole-pipeline rebalance tuned for RMC-14's marines-versus-aliens setting, wired into that fork's own size, slow, stun and status-effect systems, so lifting it means retuning every damage number on the station and maintaining a permanent divergence from upstream's damage handling.
- **Touches** — all-players, medical, security, antagonist
- **Needs** — [xenomorph-hive](antag.md#xenomorph-hive), [hud-and-status-readouts](ui.md#hud-and-status-readouts)
- **Verified** — partly supported. Every other specific in the gameplay text checks out against the actual files: RMCObstacleSlammingSystem.cs implements exactly the described slam-into-obstacle mechanic (damage = f(MobSizeCoefficient/size, ThrowSpeedCoefficient*speed), red color flash via _color.RaiseEffect, knockback via _size.KnockBack, plus optional stun/slowdown bonuses); DamageOnPulledWhileCritComponent.cs confirms drag-damage-while-downed; DeafenWhileCritComponent.cs confirms crit-deafness; health_hud.yml has exactly the Healthy/DeadAlmost/DeadDNR icon tiers described as "fresh/nearly unrecoverable/do-not-resuscitate"; holocard.yml has orange/red/black/purple triage icons; marine_modifier_sets.yml and xeno_modifier_sets.yml define per-material damage resistance for barricades, sandbags, girders, foam walls, vehicle armor/frames, resin, and weeds, matching the claim precisely. **sunrise**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 1 forks carry it*

Getting knocked down while you are running no longer just drops you in place: your character tumbles forward in the direction they were facing, skidding a tile or so, loses stamina from the impact and needs a moment before getting back up, and if you are already too winded you simply flop down with a "can't fall" message instead. Thrown objects hit according to how bulky they are, so a heavy toolbox or crate can floor someone and dent walls while small junk just bruises. Around that sit a pile of smaller body-and-motion tweaks: a visible dragging animation when you pull someone, the ability to fall asleep while strapped into a bed (with certain bedding making the bed heal you faster or slower), a stasis ability that freezes and hides a creature until it is woken, mobs that crumble into ectoplasm instead of leaving a corpse, character height and build actually changing how heavy and hard to shove you are, and extra damage resistance profiles so felinids, synthetics, dragons and similar take blunt, burn, shock and poison differently.

- **Take** — The tumble-on-knockdown and weight-scaled throwing turn two flat upstream mechanics into readable, physical moments that change how every melee fight and chase feels, without adding any new role or objective.
- **Skip** — It is a grab bag rather than one system, and the good parts (falling, throwing) sit next to unrelated pieces (stasis, ectoplasm death, profile-driven mass) whose value depends on fork content you may not have; the falling rules also rebalance every stun-and-beat encounter, so security and antag balance need retuning after the graft.
- **Touches** — all-players, security, medical, antagonist
- **Needs** — [extra-playable-species](species.md#extra-playable-species), [character-markings-and-editor](species.md#character-markings-and-editor)

**backmen**

*loop-change · entangled · medium confidence · 1 forks carry it*

Any crewmember can deliberately drop to the floor with a keybind and keep crawling around at about a quarter of normal speed, then spend a second getting back up; lose your legs and you stay down until they are replaced. On top of that the fork reroutes how hurt bodies work, so damage lands as actual wounds on the part that was hit rather than a single pooled health bar, adds an armour-piercing damage type and a Holy/immaterial damage category, and gives races like spirits, arachne, oni, harpies and shadowkin their own resistances and weaknesses (a spirit takes triple heat and quintuple holy but shrugs off cold). There is also a gib-on-collide behaviour where certain hazards splatter whoever runs into them outright, with a sound and a taunting popup.

- **Take** — Voluntary lying down and crawling is a genuinely expressive movement verb that makes downed and injured play feel active instead of just being a ragdoll, and the per-race damage modifier sets are a clean way to give species real combat identity.
- **Skip** — The damage half is not separable: BackmenDamageModelSystem is built on top of the fork's Consciousness, Wounds and body-part Targeting systems, so grafting it drags in the whole Backmen surgery rework, and even the laying-down system reaches into the fork's body/leg-counting code.
- **Touches** — medical, security, all-players, antagonist
- **Needs** — [surgery-and-limb-wounds](#surgery-and-limb-wounds), [extra-playable-species](species.md#extra-playable-species)
- **Verified** — supported.
