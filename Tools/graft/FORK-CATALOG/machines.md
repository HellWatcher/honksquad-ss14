### Access control rework

<a id="access-control-rework"></a>

Changes how doors and equipment check identity: rebuilt access sets, scan gates, secure terminals, keypad locks, grid ownership and ID-gated item use.

**rmc14**

*loop-change · entangled · medium confidence · 6 forks carry it*

Command staff work an ID terminal that first makes them badge in with their own card, then lets them slot a crewmate's card and re-badge it wholesale: pick a job, and the card instantly gets that role's entire door-and-locker set (UNMC Command, Medical, Engineering, Military Police, Requisitions, the marine squad roles, plus colony and corporate sets), tick individual accesses on top, post someone to a squad, flip their friend-or-foe tag, or "terminate" a card so it stops reading as friendly. Every one of those edits is written into the ship AI's public identification log, so a quiet promotion or a stripped card can be read back later by anyone who checks. Because access is bundled per role, your card is also what decides which equipment vendors will hand you gear, not just which doors open, and wall-mounted keycard stations need two people to swipe authorized cards at separate posts within a short window of each other before the ship will go to Red Alert.

- **Take** — Bundling access into named role groups turns ID editing from tag-by-tag clicking into one meaningful command decision, and the two-person keycard turn plus the public AI log give access changes real social weight.
- **Skip** — It is not a standalone access system: the console reaches into that fork's squad assignment, rank, skill, IFF faction and ship-AI logging systems, and the group tree itself is written around a marine job roster, so porting it means either dragging those in or rewriting most of the prototypes for your own jobs.
- **Touches** — command, security, medical, engineering, cargo, all-players
- **Needs** — [squads-ranks-and-skills](jobs.md#squads-ranks-and-skills), [extra-job-roster](jobs.md#extra-job-roster), [firearms-content-expansion](combat.md#firearms-content-expansion)
- **Verified** — supported. IdModificationConsoleSystem.cs directly imports and depends on SquadSystem, SharedRankSystem, SkillsSystem, GunIFFSystem, ARESCoreSystem, and Roles/OriginalRoleComponent — every mechanic the claim describes (job-group re-badge, squad assignment, IFF toggle, termination, AI logging) is implemented by calling into these fork-specific systems in the same file. The claim's own graft_shape ('entangled') is well supported and correctly overrides the seed's 'drop-in' path-data guess, which evidently could not see this C#-level coupling.
  - unsupported: "your card is also what decides which equipment vendors will hand you gear" — only indirectly evidenced (a single code comment in marine_access_groups.yml: "this exists because other squad members will have their own equipment vendors"); no vendor-gating system file was found/read to directly confirm access tags drive vendor permissions.
  - unsupported: "written into the ship AI's public identification log ... can be read back later by anyone who checks" — ARESCoreSystem.CreateARESLog and the ARESTabIdentificationLogs category are real and confirmed, but 'public'/'anyone who checks' access scope was inferred from folder names (ExternalTerminals/Tabs) rather than from reading an actual terminal-access-control file.

**rmc14**

*loop-change · entangled · high confidence · 15 forks carry it*

Instead of radio just working, players have to build and maintain the infrastructure that carries their voices. On the ground, comms towers and a sensor tower start broken: engineers weld the panels shut, cut and repair wiring, wrench the mast straight, restore power to the local APC, then tune the tower with an access tuner to add their side's radio channels — until that's done, nobody groundside can reach the ship, and the sensor tower feeds live enemy positions to the command map while it lasts (it randomly breaks again and needs re-repair). Where radio doesn't reach, players use a telephone network: rotary phones and wall phones are named after their location, you pick a destination off a tabbed list, the far phone audibly rings and emits a "starts ringing vigorously" emote, and whoever picks up hears you in a distinct purple anonymous whisper as long as you both keep the handset in hand and stay close. Command also gets an AI terminal you swipe your ID into to browse timestamped round logs by category, plus megaphones, a hand labeler, and squad-broadcast consoles that push announcements and map drawings to everyone.

- **Take** — The phone network and repairable comms tower turn communication itself into a contested objective — a genuinely fresh engineering job and a reason for command to leave the console — and the phone UX (named handsets, ringing emote, anonymous purple speech, hang-up on walking away) is a polished, self-contained interaction most forks have no equivalent of.
- **Skip** — RMC-14 is a total-conversion Aliens fork and this hardware is written against its own faction/IFF, access, squad and tactical-map stack; the ARES terminal in particular reads ID cards through RMC's faction system and pulls from an RMC-specific round-log pipeline, so porting it means either dragging those systems along or rewriting the plumbing, and the whole design assumes a two-site round (ship plus planet) where cutting radio is meaningful rather than just annoying.

### Mech and exosuit overhaul

<a id="mech-overhaul"></a>

Extends piloted exosuits with new chassis, weapon and utility modules, construction graphs, pilot support modules and cargo-handling power loaders.

**goobstation**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 3 forks carry it*

Engineers and roboticists build named exosuits piece by piece — a Ripley MkII, Clarke, Durand or Gygax harness plus its head, arms, legs or treads — then a pilot climbs in and bolts on hardpoints. Miners get drills (including a diamond-tipped upgrade) and a proto-kinetic launcher for tearing through rock; security and hostile pilots get a wall of mounted guns instead: heavy pulse rifles, "Firedart" and "Solaris" lasers, an immolation beam, a Tesla cannon, scattershot and incendiary shotguns, an autocannon, two sizes of missile rack that must be reloaded from outside the suit, a flashbang launcher for riots, non-lethal disablers and tasers, a chainsword, and joke mortars that lob banana peels or mousetraps. Weapons are class-locked so a mining suit cannot mount a pulse rifle, they run off the mech's own battery, and cargo posts bounties asking the station to ship a finished Clarke, Durand, Ripley or Gygax back to a client.

- **Take** — It turns the thin upstream exosuit into a real mid-round escalation object: a buildable, class-differentiated armed vehicle that gives engineering and roboticists a long-horizon project and gives security and antags something to fight over.
- **Skip** — A dozen lethal mounted weapons including missile racks and a Tesla cannon is a large balance surface to own, and the pieces it needs (per-chassis equipment tags, construction graphs, weapon upgrade slots, a pilot-faction hook fired from the upstream mech system) mean it does not land as a clean isolated drop.
- **Touches** — engineering, science, security, cargo, salvage, antagonist
- **Needs** — [gun-attachments-and-upgrades](combat.md#gun-attachments-and-upgrades)
- **Verified** — supported. Piloting an exosuit gets a proper gear wheel: hit the equipment key and a radial menu pops up at your cursor showing every weapon and tool bolted into the mech, plus a "bare hands" option, with the currently active one lit green — you pick your drill or gun directly instead of cycling through the list, and a popup confirms the swap.
