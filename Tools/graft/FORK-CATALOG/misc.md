### Hostile fauna and NPC roster

**sunrise**

*activity · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 9 forks carry it*

Players can pick a personal pet in the lobby before the round starts and it spawns next to them at roundstart already bonded to them, with a little control window for ordering it around and a petting interaction that shows hearts. The creature roster itself is much wider than vanilla: dog-eared "inferior vulpkanin" mooks (including syndicate reinforcement versions someone can be dropped in as), a book-shaped library familiar that can be taken over by a ghost, snowmen, elementals, clowns and extra dogs. Most notable is the carp queen, a ghost role shark-queen who eats to fuel summoning a shoal of up to five carp servants and heals herself off the same hunger, so a carp attack can escalate under a real player's direction instead of drifting as dumb mobs. A few pets in the picker are marked sponsor-only, and the same bundle carries linked explosive collars that detonate every collar in a chain when the host wearer dies.

- **Take** — A lobby-picked personal pet gives every player, not just the handful of jobs with a station animal, a small persistent companion to care about, and it is cheap flavour that pays off every single round.
- **Skip** — The spawn path is wired straight into Sunrise's sponsor-validation and player-cache services and a sponsor-only flag on the pet prototype, so grafting it means either importing that donator plumbing or rewriting the gate, and the picker still needs a panel added to the upstream lobby screen plus a component on the player mob prototype.
- **Touches** — all-players, ghost-roles, service, security, antagonist
- **Needs** — [sponsor-and-donator-perks](#sponsor-and-donator-perks), [lobby-and-character-ui](ui.md#lobby-and-character-ui)
- **Verified** — partly supported. Read PetSpawnSystem.cs, PetSelectionPrototype.cs, PetControlPrototype.cs/BUI, ExpCollarsSystem.cs, carp_queen.yml, familiars.yml, animals.yml, pets.yml, and pet_selections.yml directly from the sunrise raw repo, confirming the roundstart-bond spawn flow, radial pet-control menu, hearts petting popups, syndicate vulpkanin reinforcements, ghost-takeover library familiar, carp-queen hunger/heal/summon numbers, and linked exploding collars. Critically, I also found Content.Client/Lobby/UI/LobbyCharacterPreviewPanel.xaml(.cs) — a file OUTSIDE any _Sunrise namespace dir — already patched in place with ChangePetButton/OnChangePetRequested/SetPetSprite hooks, which the cited evidence list omitted. That both confirms the claim's needs-upstream-edits downgrade from the drop-in seed and shows it understates the coupling: the fork doesn't just need a hypothetical future panel added, its own tree already edits an upstream file to wire the picker into the lobby screen.
  - unsupported: "snowmen, elementals, clowns and extra dogs" — the actual pet roster file (Resources/Prototypes/_Sunrise/Entities/Mobs/Pets/pets.yml, not in the cited evidence list) has 22 pets: rabbit, anteater, octopus, otter, navy seal, corgi variants, McGriff, slimes pet, cat runtime/floppa, walter, crab atmos, possum morty/poppy, paperwork, goose, clown goblin, parrot. It is not 'a few' out of many free ones; the entire current picker roster is sponsor-gated, which cuts against the take_because framing of this as free flavor for every player.

**serbia-strong**

*activity · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 9 forks carry it*

Adds a grab-bag of new creatures. On the tame end: Largo the cargo gorilla (a ghost can take him over, follow simple orders from supply staff, and be petted), an opossum in tiny protective gear, and a watermelon dog you grow from seeds in botany that then plays as a ghost role loyal to its gardener. On the hostile end: cult-corrupted farm animals that hunt the crew as team antagonists, a Dark Reaper demon summoned from a bloody rune that phases in and out of the material world, screams to stun people, drags victims into itself to heal and grow into larger forms, and floods a room with blood mist. Topping it off is the Ash Drake, an admin-dropped boss that flies across the map leaving a trail of lava, calls down a 20-strong meteor barrage, throws great fireballs, roars, and raises a pack of skeletons to fight alongside it.

- **Take** — It is a ready-made spread of ghost-role content at three difficulty tiers, from a harmless department pet to a full admin-event boss with flight, meteors and summons, so one graft gives both flavour mobs and an event finale.

- **Touches** — ghost-roles, antagonist, cargo, botany, all-players
- **Needs** — [blood-cult](antag.md#blood-cult), [language-system](roleplay.md#language-system)
- **Verified** — partly supported. Fetched all cited files plus a sparse clone of the repo. **goobstation**

*activity · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 9 forks carry it*

Goob adds a large bestiary on top of the upstream one: asteroid-dwelling fermid bugs that come in small, large, warrior and queen sizes, brullbars and a brullbar king, bears, white wolves, rideable-looking horses, abyss crawlers, gluttons (something that used to be a clown), a "living curse" that shrugs off everything except holy damage, nanite swarms, rogue gunbots and syndicate assault bots, sharkminnows, plus a pile of pets and ghost-role critters (lootbug, homunculus, clown goblin, Trevor, syndicate mothroaches and bees, colour-variant mothroaches, sentient carp and mice). Two behaviours change how fights with them feel: hit one animal in a pack and every friendly creature nearby turns on you at once, and a mob's faction can be swapped on the fly by a status effect so a hostile thing can be temporarily made to stop caring about you. Separately, if a crewmember drifts too far from the station out in space they get a full-screen caution popup and a leviathan roar, and a segmented space whale locks onto them until they get back near the station. There are also worker-bot AI routines: a bot that tends hydroponics trays, one that patrols and welds things shut, and one that refills a machine it is linked to.

- **Take** — It is a big pick-and-choose bestiary you can graft one creature at a time, and the pack-retaliation rule alone makes existing animal encounters read as ecosystems rather than isolated punching bags.
- **Skip** — Every creature drags its own RSI, sounds and ghost-role locale strings, and the roster is heavily Goob-flavoured (goida bot, Trevor, clown goblins) so importing it wholesale imports someone else's server culture along with the monsters; the space whale in particular is a separate opinionated subsystem with its own CVars that punishes leaving the station.
- **Touches** — all-players, salvage, security, service, engineering, ghost-roles, science
- **Verified** — partly supported; assessed as drop-in, corrected by the verifier. The fork both patches upstream directories for this unit and would require the same edits to graft the pack-retaliation mechanic elsewhere.
  - unsupported: "one that patrols and welds things shut" — WeldbotWeldOperator.cs requires a RepairableComponent target and calls RepairableSystem.ApplyRepairs (heals damage; damages targets only when emagged); it is a repair bot, nothing is welded shut

  - unsupported: "graft one creature at a time" (take_because) understates per-creature dependencies: the living curse needs Goob's WeakToHoly component plus BiologicalMetaphysical/AdvancedSpirit damage container and modifier set from the religion stack, the space whale needs the MobCaller system and GoobCVars, the sharkminnow needs CollectiveMind

**rmc14**

*activity · entangled · medium confidence · 9 forks carry it*

RMC-14's wildlife and monsters are tuned for a drop-onto-a-hostile-planet round. Simple animals like a small scurrying dog wander the map on their own, while the dangerous creatures start asleep and motionless: they only rouse when a dropship touches down within about thirty tiles of them, so a landing zone that looked quiet suddenly has things waking up around the ramp. Hunting creatures pick their targets with a lot more discrimination than a generic mob would — they preferentially chase people who are still standing, ignore the already-downed, single out someone who can be infected by a facehugger, and steer toward empty resin traps and unfilled egg clusters to stock them, and the leapers close distance with a pounce that needs line of sight, a clear angle and roughly three and a half tiles of range rather than shambling in a straight line. Anyone who dies and turns into a zombie stops being a person on your side entirely: they lose their squad orders and specialist permissions, can no longer be taken over by a ghost or infected further, gain night vision, join a mindless hostile faction, start regenerating heavily (and faster while critical), heal further by biting people, move slightly slower, and their speech is replaced with zombie groaning.

- **Take** — The behaviour pieces solve real problems generic NPCs have: monsters that stay dormant until players actually arrive, hunters that pick sensible targets instead of the nearest body, and a leap attack with proper line-of-sight and angle checks.
- **Skip** — Almost none of it is standalone fauna — the targeting conditions are written against RMC's facehugger infection, resin traps and egg morphers, the wake trigger fires off a dropship-landed-on-planet event, and the zombie pass strips RMC marine order and specialist components, so grafting it means dragging the xenomorph and marine-drop systems along or rewriting every condition.
- **Touches** — all-players, security, antagonist, ghost-roles
- **Needs** — [xenomorph-hive](antag.md#xenomorph-hive), [shuttle-piloting-extensions](engineering.md#shuttle-piloting-extensions)
- **Verified** — supported. Every specific mechanical claim checked against the actual source is exact: WakeNPCOnDropshipLandingComponent.Range=30 tiles triggered by DropshipLandedOnPlanetEvent, SleepNPCComponent putting NPCs to sleep on MapInit, NPCLeapComponent.LeapDistance=3.5f/MaxAngleDegrees=5 with a LeapStatus enum literally containing NotInSight/TargetBadAngle/TargetOutOfRange, and RMCZombieSystem.OnZombified removing MarineOrdersComponent/ScoutWhitelistComponent/SniperWhitelistComponent/PyroWhitelistComponent/InfectableComponent/GhostRoleComponent/GhostTakeoverAvailableComponent while adding NightVisionComponent, faction 'RMCDumb', PassiveHealing, PassiveHealingCritMultiplier=1.5f, HealingOnBite, ZombieMovementSpeedDebuff=0.80f, and a ReplacementAccentComponent. Listing the Content.Server/_RMC14/NPC directory also turned up TargetIsStandingCon.cs, TargetIsDownCon.cs, TargetIsNotDeadCon.cs, TargetIsEmptyResinTrapCon.cs and TargetAvailibleEggMorpherCon.cs alongside the cited TargetInfectableCon.cs; these weren't in the evidence_cited list but directly back the standing-vs-downed, resin-trap and egg-cluster targeting claims, so the description is not reaching beyond what the repo contains. On graft_shape: the claim already downgraded the seed's drop-in guess to entangled, and that downgrade is correct. TargetInfectableCon and RMCZombieSystem pull in Content.Shared._RMC14.Xenonids.Parasite (xenomorph-hive) and RMCNPCSystem/WakeNPCOnDropshipLandingComponent pull in Content.Shared._RMC14.Dropship (the dropship/shuttle-piloting system), all while every file itself still lives inside _RMC14 namespace directories, confirming namespace containment does not equal standalone graftability here.

### Food, drink and cooking expansion

<a id="food-and-drink-expansion"></a>

New meals, cocktails, condiments and ingredients with their reactions, cooking recipes and the critics or vendors that consume them.

**rmc14**

*activity · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 9 forks carry it*

Players get sealed military ration packs that they tear open and unpack into a randomised spread of items: one hot entree, a side, a snack, a dessert, a flavoured drink-powder sachet, a bottled water, sometimes a fortune cookie, plus a mini pack of cigarettes and a matchbook, so no two rations are the same and swapping components with squadmates becomes a thing. There are faction-flavoured versions (marine, corporate, and a rival-force canned-food pack) that pull from different item pools. Alongside that comes a bar and mess-hall catalogue: refillable canteens and engraved flasks with adjustable pour sizes that shatter and spill their contents if smashed, named liquor bottles and syrups you can mix drinks from or turn into molotovs, canned sodas, coffee, milk, cups and glasses, and a large microwave cookbook covering pizzas, breads, breakfasts, pastas, desserts, soups, pies, salads and secret recipes.

- **Take** — Enormous ready-made consumables catalogue plus the ration-pack "open it and see what you got" loop, which gives every player a small self-contained thing to do with almost no new mechanics to learn.
- **Skip** — The prototypes are wired into RMC-14's own plumbing (fixed-size/remove-only storage grids, a fork steel material, molotov crafting, and xenomorph interaction blacklists on nearly every item), so every file needs de-linting before it will even load, and the flavour is hard military-sci-fi rather than generic station.
- **Touches** — service, cargo, all-players, security
- **Needs** — [storage-and-inventory-rework](ui.md#storage-and-inventory-rework)
- **Verified** — supported. Fetched mre.yml, canteen.yml, alcohol.yml, cans.yml, black_goo.yml, both guidebooks, and the two .cs component files directly from RMC-14/RMC-14. Every specific in the gameplay claim checks out: randomized OrGroup contents (entree/side/snack/dessert/drink/fortune-cookie-at-33%/cigarettes/matchbook), faction variants (base CMMRE, corporate We-Ya, TSE, SPP with distinct item pools), refillable canteens/flasks with SolutionTransfer transferAmounts lists and Destructible+SpillBehavior on 50 damage, alcohol.yml's CraftsIntoMolotov + MixableSolution, cans.yml's PhysicalComposition CMSteel, and the guidebook's exact cookbook category list (Pizza, Savory, Breads, Breakfast, Pasta, Dessert, Soup, Pie, BarsAndCookies, Cake, Salad, Secret). The claimed skip-reasons are also verified: FixedItemSizeStorage/RemoveOnlyStorage/MRERequireOpenBeforeStorage appear on the MRE container, InteractedBlacklist{Xeno} appears on nearly every consumable (canteen base, cans base, black goo), and CMSteel is a fork-only material — all confirming a real, non-trivial dependency load. I additionally spot-checked a vanilla file (Content.Shared/Nutrition/Components/DrinkComponent.cs) and it is unmodified plain upstream code, so the fork's copy is self-contained (not fork-patches-upstream); the "needs-upstream-edits" is really about the destination codebase needing to either adopt or strip RMC14-specific components (storage rework, Xeno tag, molotov crafting) to load these prototypes, matching the integration-would-require-upstream-edits reading.
  - unsupported: "swapping components with squadmates becomes a thing" is a social inference, not something any fetched file states — RemoveOnlyStorage/StorageFill only confirm randomized, removable contents, not a trading mechanic.
  - unsupported: "engraved flasks" — the actual descriptions say flasks are "embossed" with a logo (Marine/We-Ya), not engraved; trivial wording mismatch.

**sunrise**

*activity · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 9 forks carry it*

**goobstation**

*loop-change · entangled · medium confidence · 9 forks carry it*

Players can take gear to a magical altar, lay it out with a special "source" item and an enchanting tool, and watch a shimmering aura settle over it, permanently bolting on a named power that shows up when anyone examines the item. The roster is openly Minecraft-flavoured: Sharpness and Fire Aspect and Knockback for melee weapons, Thorns and Protection and Fire Protection for armour, Fortune for pickaxes and drills so mining pulls extra ore, Lavaforged boots that let you stroll through lava, Unslippable shoes, Slippery on anything you want to grease, Unbreaking so an item stops taking wear, and Insulated to turn any glove (or Hamlet) into real insuls. Most enchants stack in levels shown as roman numerals up to II-V, some enchants refuse to sit alongside their opposites, and the way you push an item to a higher tier is to kill a player-controlled mob on top of the altar, which announces to everyone nearby that their soul has empowered the item.

- **Take** — A single small framework that makes almost every existing item category more interesting, with an immediately legible Minecraft vocabulary players already understand and a built-in dark twist (soul sacrifice) that generates its own stories.
- **Skip** — The tier-upgrade path is a ritual murder mechanic wired directly into Goob's devil/Condemned system, so grafting it whole either drags that antag content along or means rewriting the upgrade loop; and the balance surface is wide (Sharpness V melee, Protection IV armour, free insuls, boosted ore yields all land at once) so it needs real tuning rather than a straight port.
- **Touches** — all-players, security, cargo, salvage, engineering, antagonist
- **Needs** — [devil-pacts](antag.md#devil-pacts)
- **Verified** — partly supported; assessed as needs upstream edits, corrected by the verifier. All 16 cited files check out almost exactly as described: the enchant roster (Sharpness/Fire Aspect/Knockback/Thorns/Protection/Fire Protection/Fortune/Lavaforged/Unslippable/Slippery/Unbreaking/Insulated), roman-numeral levels (maxLevel 2-5), incompatible-enchant blocking, and the sacrifice-based tier upgrade (EnchantSacrificeSystem.cs killing a player-mob on an altar, popping \"enchanting-sacrifice-single/multiple\") are all real and accurately paraphrased — this is a well-evidenced, carefully-read claim overall. But two things need correcting: (1) enchanting is gated to a CanEnchantComponent (Chaplain/Heretic/Wizard per the source comment), not open to \"players\" generally, and (2) the one real dependency I found (EnchantSacrificeSystem -> Devil.Condemned) is itself a fork-namespaced module, not an upstream file — I found zero evidence files or referenced components outside Content.Goobstation.*/Resources/Prototypes/_Goobstation/Resources/Locale/_Goobstation directories. That makes \"needs-upstream-edits\" an imprecise label for what is really a fork-internal entanglement with the devil-pacts feature; \"entangled\" is the more accurate shape, and neither of the two given upstream_reading definitions cleanly applies since the coupling isn't with upstream at all.
  - unsupported: "Players can take gear to a magical altar, lay it out with a special \"source\" item and an enchanting tool, and watch a shimmering aura settle over it" — implies any player can perform an enchant. Content.Goobstation.Shared/Enchanting/Components/CanEnchantComponent.cs gates this behind a component whose doc comment says \"Place this on any entity you want to allow to enchant... e.g. Chaplain, Heretic or Wizard\"; EnchanterSystem.cs pops \"enchanter-disallowed-enchant\" (\"You are not worthy!\") at anyone lacking it. This is a role-gated ritual for a handful of religious/magic-flavoured roles, not a general player action.
  - unsupported: The claim's own graft_shape="needs-upstream-edits" plus depends_on:["devil-pacts"] frames this as an upstream dependency, but the actual coupling (EnchantSacrificeSystem.cs importing Content.Goobstation.Shared.Devil.Condemned.CondemnedComponent) is entirely within the fork's own _Goobstation/Goobstation.* namespace — no evidence file, nor any file I could find in Content.Goobstation.Shared/Enchanting or Resources/Prototypes/_Goobstation/Enchants, touches an upstream directory. This is a fork-internal cross-feature dependency (entangled with the devil-pacts antagonist system), not an upstream-edit requirement in the sense the label implies.

**serbia-strong**

*cosmetic · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 9 forks carry it*

While you are temporarily turned into something else, a small two-digit countdown appears in your status icons ticking down the seconds you have left in that form, so you can time when to slip back before it drops you. The transformations shipped here are a wizard action that turns you into a spider ghost and a 35-second astral form for the fork's cult creature, each with its own entry and exit sound, and both snap you back to your real body if you go into crit or die. Separately, some items visibly flip between an on and an off look when you click them in hand, so a device reads as active or idle at a glance.

- **Take** — The countdown-digit alert is a genuinely reusable readability win: any timed transformation, buff or ability in your fork can show players exactly how many seconds remain instead of leaving them guessing.
- **Skip** — SS220 files carry an explicit EULA/CLA with a hosting restriction rather than AGPL, so grafting them is a licensing problem before it is a code problem, and what you actually get is a HUD widget plus two polymorph configs that point at their cult and wizard mobs, not a reusable enchantment framework.
- **Touches** — antagonist, all-players
- **Needs** — [wizard-magic](antag.md#wizard-magic), [blood-cult](antag.md#blood-cult)
- **Verified** — partly supported; assessed as drop-in, corrected by the verifier. The countdown's tick logic is not in the unit's SS220 files: SharedPolymorphTimerSystem only shows/clears the alert, while the actual AlertTime update lives in an SS220 patch block (//SS220-cult-hotfix-23-start #3988, ~lines 90-98) inside the upstream file Content.Server/Polymorph/Systems/PolymorphSystem.cs, which I read directly; without that upstream patch the recommended countdown widget never updates, so drop-in is wrong and the fork demonstrably patches upstream.
  - unsupported: "each with its own entry and exit sound" - only the MiGoInAstral polymorph defines polymorphSound/exitPolymorphSound (migo_astral_in/out.ogg); the full WizardSpiderGhost config contains no sound fields at all.
  - unsupported: "While you are temporarily turned into something else, a small two-digit countdown appears in your status icons" - implies both shipped transformations show it; only MobAstralMiGo carries PolymorphTimer (in migo_astral.yml), and the wizard spider ghost polymorph has no duration and no timer component, so it never shows a countdown.
  - unsupported: "some items visibly flip between an on and an off look when you click them in hand" - no item prototype consuming ToggleAppearanceOnUse was found in the cited files or via search, and the system subscribes to ActivateInWorldEvent, not an in-hand use event; the 'some items' consumer claim is unverified.

### Fishing minigame

<a id="fishing"></a>

Rods, lures and fishing spots with a timing minigame that yields fish, sometimes backed by its own points economy and vendor.

**goobstation, impstation, rmc14**

*activity · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 4 forks carry it*

Grab a fishing rod, walk to a patch of "suspicious waves" (water, lava, plasma, or a toilet), and cast your lure. After a wait something bites, and you get a tug-of-war: you keep working the rod to fill a progress bar while the fish periodically fights back and drags it down, with messages like "You feel something clinging to the fishing lure!" and "You lost control over the rod!" if it pulls too far and snaps free. Win and you haul up the catch, which is usually junk (scrap, maintenance trash, the odd bit of salvage treasure or a tool) but sometimes a live fish that flops around suffocating in your hands, can be butchered into fish meat for the kitchen, or sold to cargo for a few hundred spesos. Rare pulls include joke catches like an alien fish, a fish shaped like the nuke disk, and a fish version of Ian. RMC-14's take is a slower version of the same idea: plant the pole, wait five to ten seconds, then react inside a one- or two-second bite window, with a bait slot you can load to shift the odds toward rarer loot (ores, high-capacity cells, a clown burger). Impstation's "fishing rod" is not really fishing at all, it fires a hook that reels whatever it hits back toward you, with a plasma variant and a changeling flesh-tentacle version that yanks people across the room.

- **Take** — A self-contained idle activity with a real skill check and a loot table, giving quiet crew and bored departments something to do that also feeds the kitchen and cargo sale loops.
- **Skip** — The rod and minigame are only half the feature: fishing spots are anchored entities that have to be hand-placed on your station maps (and the toilet-fishing variant means editing upstream toilet prototypes), so a clean file-level copy lands you a working rod with nowhere to use it, and the three forks' versions are incompatible designs you would have to pick between rather than combine.
- **Touches** — service, cargo, all-players, salvage, antagonist
- **Verified** — partly supported. Every load-bearing gameplay beat checks out against the actual files: fishing.ftl has the exact quoted strings, FishingRodComponent.cs/ActiveFisherComponent.cs implement the progress/struggle tug-of-war, fishingspots.yml names the spot "suspicious waves", loot_tables.yml has the scrap/trash/salvage/maint-tools split plus the named joke fish (FishAlien, FishNukeDisk, FishIan) and per-biome tables (Water/Lava/Plasma/Toilet), fish.yml shows Butcherable->FoodMeatFish and Asphyxiation-based suffocation with StaticPrice 250-750. Impstation's fishing_rods.yml confirms it is really a Gun/BasicEntityAmmoProvider harpoon, not a minigame, with a plasma variant and a changeling-only "FishingRodLing" that hooks people. RMC-14's RMCFishingComponents.cs confirms the deploy/wait/bite state machine (WaitMin 5s/WaitMax 10s) and a bait component that shifts rarity-tier weights, and fishing.yml lists the ore/PowerCellHigh/FoodBurgerClown loot named in the claim. The one soft overclaim is rounding RMC's 0.5-2s bite window to "one- or two-second." On graft_shape: I found direct proof the assessor's own graft_shape call is right, though not from the cited evidence list — Resources/Prototypes/Entities/Structures/Furniture/toilet.yml (a plain upstream path, outside any _Goobstation directory) has been patched in place with a `type: FishingSpot` component and a physics fixture, both bracketed by "# Goobstation - fishing" comments. That is fork-patches-upstream in the literal sense the path-data seed could not see. Separately, FishingSpotWater is a placement:SnapgridCenter anchored entity with no spawner system, so getting fishing spots onto any station's map (this fork's own or a recipient's) is a second, independent upstream-edit requirement — integration-would-require-upstream-edits. Both readings are true here, which also means the seed's 'drop-in' guess was wrong for a reason more concrete than the claim itself demonstrates.
  - unsupported: RMC-14's bite window is described as "one- or two-second" but RMCFishingRodComponent.cs sets BiteMin = TimeSpan.FromSeconds(0.5) and BiteMax = TimeSpan.FromSeconds(2) — the true floor is half a second, not one full second.
