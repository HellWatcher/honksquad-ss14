### Research tree and lathe rework

<a id="research-tree-rework"></a>

Replaces the technology console with a different progression model, whether a searchable node graph, new disciplines, per-department fabricators or transferable research disks.

**rmc14**

*structural · entangled · medium confidence · 6 forks carry it*

Instead of researching tech in a lab, the crew earns progress by going out and recovering things: intel documents, uploaded data, retrieved items, analyzed chemical samples, rescued survivors, recovered bodies, and restoring the colony's power and comms. All of that feeds a shared points pool that command spends at a Tech Control console, picking from tiered unlock nodes with icons, prices that climb each time a repeatable node is bought, and some nodes time-locked until later in the round. Buying a node has an immediate round-wide effect rather than adding a lathe recipe: more supply budget, more dropship fabricator budget, an emergency supply drop, a vehicle unlocked, or a fresh squad of reinforcement marines spawning as ghost roles from cryo, each purchase announced station-wide and logged under the buyer's name. Separately, a handful of research data disks can be found and slotted into medical machines to teach an autodoc new procedures (internal bleeding, broken bones, organ damage, parasite extraction) or upgrade a sleeper's chemical range and dialysis.

- **Take** — It converts "research" from a lab minigame into a round-wide objective economy, where everyone's fieldwork buys reinforcements, budget and vehicles that visibly change how the rest of the round goes.
- **Skip** — It is not a tech tree you can lift in isolation: the console is wired into RMC-14's intel objectives, requisitions budget, dropship fabricator, ARES logging, marine announcements, player-count scaling and cryo reinforcement spawners, so grafting it means either importing or re-authoring all of those, and the actual node catalogue is not in prototypes, so every option would have to be rebuilt for your own fork's content.
- **Touches** — command, cargo, medical, ghost-roles, all-players
- **Needs** — [cargo-bounties-and-market](cargo.md#cargo-bounties-and-market), [gamemaster-and-ghost-roles](roleplay.md#gamemaster-and-ghost-roles), [squads-ranks-and-skills](jobs.md#squads-ranks-and-skills), [autodoc](medical.md#autodoc)

**backmen**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 6 forks carry it*

Science works off a rebuilt research console: a pannable node map of technologies you can recenter, filter to already-researched entries, and click to see a card showing the exact recipes it unlocks and the cost in research points. Instead of just needing enough points, a technology can be gated behind "research 3 more Tier 2 Engineering technologies to reach 75%", and once the station commits to a main discipline the higher tiers of the other disciplines grey out with a message saying that branch is locked. The tree itself is a fork-written progression spread across disciplines for engineering, security/arsenal, cargo, mining, medical, robotics and science, so what each department can eventually build, and in what order, is different from the usual station. Unlocking a technology also gets announced with the name and the points spent, and the package ships alongside a talking Oracle statue that demands specific items ("I demand one syringe! Do not question why!") and pays out in research knowledge when fed.

- **Take** — It turns research from a points-shopping list into a routed choice with real consequences, since committing to a main discipline visibly locks out the deep end of the others and forces science to pick a direction for the round.
- **Skip** — It is not a bolt-on: the fork replaces the whole technology tree and the console UI, so every upstream technology and lathe recipe would need re-slotting into their layout and hand-placed node positions, and every future upstream research change becomes a merge conflict you own forever.
- **Touches** — science, engineering, cargo, security, medical, salvage
- **Needs** — [psionics-and-glimmer](roleplay.md#psionics-and-glimmer)

**goobstation**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 6 forks carry it*

Clicking a card shows its point cost, its prerequisites, and picture cards of every recipe it will unlock, plus a percentage showing how far through that tier the station is, and a toggle to show only what has already been researched. **far-horizons**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 6 forks carry it*

Science works from a visual, searchable tech map instead of the stock discipline console: nodes sit in three tiers, each with an icon, a point cost and prerequisite lines to earlier nodes, and you pick one and click Research to see a progress bar fill toward its cost. Points collect in a station bank that has a hard ceiling and bleeds away once full, so the console nags over the radio that the bank is maxed and someone needs to queue work; scientists line up to three technologies in a queue (more once queue-size upgrades are bought), and every queue add, removal and completed unlock is broadcast on the radio with the approver's name. Alongside the usual lathe recipes, some nodes hand out station-wide perks rather than items, such as unlocking plastic surgery, making surgery faster, or raising the point bank and queue limits, and higher tiers only open after the station has banked enough total research.

- **Take** — It turns research from a menu of purchases into a visible map with pacing pressure, a bank that punishes idling and radio chatter that makes science's progress legible to the whole station.

- **Touches** — science, engineering, medical, security, cargo, command

**monolith**

*loop-change · entangled · high confidence · 6 forks carry it*

Research stops being one science tree and becomes a set of rival manufacturer catalogues: you first buy a shared spine of tiered "Material Research" and "Ballistics Research" nodes, then branch into a corporation or faction you want to arm yourself from, each with its own T1-T3 ladder priced in the tens of thousands of points. Mieyo sells breaching gear, wound treatment and bionics; the pirate PDV branch sells ballistics, thermal imaging, a portable hacking tool and blood-red imitation suits; other branches sell energy weapons, hardsuits, mechs, drones, and warship munitions like torpedoes, railgun shells and fighter ordnance. Several nodes unlock shipyard vouchers by grade rather than an item, so researching far enough is how a crew earns the right to buy bigger ships. A second console turns stored research points back into physical 1k, 5k and 10k data disks after a short print cycle, so points become objects you can pocket, hand to another crew or feed into building "data farm" machines instead of only spending them at your own lathe.

- **Take** — The point-disk printer is the genuinely portable idea here: making research points into a physical, printable, tradeable disk turns science output into something crews can carry, trade and steal instead of a number locked to one server.
- **Skip** — The tree itself is written for Monolith's multi-ship sector setting, with nodes whose payload is faction-branded gear, ship vouchers and torpedo ammunition that do not exist on a station fork, so grafting the disciplines means importing or reinventing hundreds of entity prototypes before a single node unlocks anything.
- **Touches** — science, engineering, cargo, security, medical, command, all-players
- **Needs** — [shipyard-player-ships](cargo.md#shipyard-player-ships), [ship-to-ship-combat](combat.md#ship-to-ship-combat)
- **Verified** — supported. ### Power grid and device networking

<a id="power-grid-and-device-networking"></a>

Cable-based power distribution with APCs, SMES units, substations and generators, plus wired and wireless device networks with link configurators. A console is only here if it reads or controls power or the device network; consoles that merely happen to be machines are `device-and-machine-additions`.

**rmc14**

*structural · entangled · medium confidence · 5 forks carry it*

Power here is not a web of wires you trace with a multitool, it is a handful of big fusion reactors feeding rooms directly. Engineers keep the reactors fed with fusion fuel cells (a slow ten-second swap), and patch broken ones back together through a wrench-then-wire-then-weld repair sequence while the reactor's face shows how much juice is left. Every room has its own APC with a battery, a main breaker, a charge toggle and three separate switches for equipment, lighting and environment, each set to auto, forced on, or forced off; it is ID-locked and screwdriver-locked, and a marine without real engineering training cannot do anything with it beyond stare at the screen. The sabotage side is the drama: someone can rig a reactor to overload, at which point it starts humming and hissing warnings at anyone nearby, and an engineer with a multitool has to get there and shut it back down before the lights go out. Bulbs and tubes are physical objects that shatter when something hits them, burn your bare hand if you grab them, and simply die when the reactors do, so losing power means fighting in the dark.

- **Take** — It turns power from background plumbing into a defendable objective: a small number of reactors that can be fed, broken, repaired and sabotaged, with room-by-room APC control and lights that actually go out when the reactors do.
- **Skip** — It replaces the upstream cable-and-node power grid wholesale rather than sitting beside it, so every powered machine, every map area and every APC on your stations has to be converted; on top of that the APC and reactor interactions are hard-gated behind RMC's engineer skill levels and its own access-level ids, and the overload path assumes a xeno-style antagonist exists to trigger it.
- **Touches** — engineering, antagonist, command, all-players

### Fission reactor

<a id="fission-reactor"></a>

A buildable multi-part nuclear reactor with fuel centrifuge, coolant loop, control computers and a steam or gas turbine as an alternative main power source.

**far-horizons**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 4 forks carry it*

Engineers get a nuclear reactor as an alternative to the station's usual engine: a big vessel whose interior is a grid you physically fill in, slotting fuel rods, control rods, heat exchangers and gas channel pieces into a 5x5 or 7x7 layout, then piping cool gas in one side and running the hot gas out into a gas turbine that spins up and feeds the power grid. ### Factory automation

<a id="factory-automation"></a>

Automated production lines built from robotic arms, constructors, conveyors and label or stack filters that route and process items without a player.

**goobstation**

*activity · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 3 forks carry it*

You can build a production line that keeps working while you walk away. Robotic arms sit next to machines and pick items up, feed them in, and pass the output along, and you slot a filter card into each arm to decide what it is allowed to grab: match a label, match part of an item's name, only take stacks above a certain size (and split off chunks of a set size), only take anchored or unanchored objects, only take mobs that are alive, dead, in crit, or handcuffed, or combine several filters with an AND/OR gate. - **Take** — It turns "make 200 of these by hand" into a build-once machine, giving engineering and cargo a genuinely new mid-round project that runs unattended and gives quiet rounds something to do.
- **Skip** — It is a large surface (60+ code files across client, server and shared, six filter types each with its own UI window) and only pays off if enough upstream machines are tagged as automatable, which means touching existing machine prototypes and keeping that list current through every upstream sync; a half-ported version where only two machines accept arms is worse than not having it.
