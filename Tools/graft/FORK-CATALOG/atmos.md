### Exotic gases and reactions

<a id="exotic-gas-expansion"></a>

Ports the wider gas roster (BZ, halon, healium, hyper-noblium, nitrium, pluoxium, proto-nitrate, zauker, metallic hydrogen) with their production and decomposition reactions, alarm thresholds and derived crafting materials.

**funky**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 6 forks carry it*

Atmospherics goes from a handful of gases to twenty: alongside oxygen, plasma and tritium the station can now make and handle BZ, healium, nitrium, pluoxium, hydrogen, halon, helium, proto-nitrate, zauker, hyper-noblium and anti-noblium, each with its own coloured cloud when it leaks, its own air-alarm danger rating, its own effect on anyone who breathes it, and its own cargo price per mole. A pile of new machinery gives those gases somewhere to come from and somewhere to go: a multi-part hypertorus fusion reactor driven from its own console, a crystallizer that freezes a held gas mix into solid items (metallic hydrogen, healium and proto-nitrate crystals, ammonia crystals, and at the lethal end a supermatter sliver), a fuel-fed electrolyzer, portable pipe scrubbers and gas extractors, and a temperature gate that only opens when the gas passing through is above or below a number you type in. A bluespace sender lets atmos push one custom mix into a station-wide network, and wall-mounted bluespace vendors elsewhere fill crew gas tanks straight from it.

- **Take** — It turns atmospherics from a life-support chore into a production department: twenty gases with distinct effects, real recipes that yield sellable solid products, and machinery that gives an atmos tech a whole round's worth of work.
- **Skip** — The gas roster is not additive content - it widens the engine's core gas array from 9 to 20 slots and rewrites the shared atmos constants file, so every future upstream atmos merge conflicts in the hottest, most performance-sensitive code in the game, and the simulation cost of the wider array is paid every tick on every tile.
- **Touches** — engineering, atmospherics, cargo, medical, science
- **Needs** — [atmospherics-simulation](#atmospherics-simulation), [fusion-reactor](engineering.md#fusion-reactor)

Machinery claims (multi-part HFR with console, crystallizer recipes including a dangerous supermatter sliver, fuel-fed electrolyzer, pipe scrubber, gas extractor, typed-threshold temperature gate, bluespace sender plus wall-mounted vendors) all verified in the cited files; only the per-gas leak-cloud and breathing-effect universals overreach, and reaction code additionally lives in the _Sunrise namespace, not _Funkystation.
  - unsupported: each with its own coloured cloud when it leaks - BZ, Pluoxium, Hydrogen and Helium have no gasOverlaySprite/gasOverlayState in Resources/Prototypes/Atmospherics/gases.yml, so those gases produce no visible leak cloud; the color field only tints UI/pipe visuals

**monolith**

*activity · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 6 forks carry it*

Atmos gains extra gases beyond the standard roster (BZ, healium, nitrium alongside frezon) and a crystallizer machine that turns a carefully tuned gas mix held at a narrow temperature band into solid goods: plastic, plasteel, plastitanium, iridite, plasma sheets, diamonds, even telecrystals. Getting a recipe right means hitting both the gas ratios and a temperature window (sometimes tens of thousands of kelvin), and the hotter recipes are flagged dangerous, so a botched run is an explosion rather than a wasted batch. Frezon-rich gas deposits exist out in the world to be tapped as feedstock, and a storage-chamber pressure alarm nags when the holding tank creeps toward its limit. Separately, temperature becomes something you feel: heaters warm everyone within a couple of tiles up to a capped comfortable temperature, sitting in a given temperature band applies effects to your body, and hugging or petting another crewmate actually shares body heat between you, so a warm person can thaw out someone who came in from the cold.

### Atmospherics simulation

<a id="atmospherics-simulation"></a>

Tile-based gas simulation with mixtures, reactions, pipe networks, vents, scrubbers, air alarms, canisters and space wind.

**backmen, deltav, frontier, funky, serbia-strong, sunrise**

*activity · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 6 forks carry it*

Six forks pile extra things to do on top of the standard gas game. - **Take** — Frontier's offshore gas mining is the rare thing that gives atmospherics a reason to leave its room: a full scan-drill-dock-filter-sell loop with its own guidebook page, tools and payout curve, aimed squarely at the department that usually has nothing to do after setup.

### Planet weather and fog

<a id="planet-weather-and-fog"></a>

Surface-level environmental simulation for planetary maps: weather effects, fog that hides the map before deployment and temperature effects outdoors.

**rmc14**

*loop-change · entangled · medium confidence · 0 forks carry it*

On RMC-14's planet maps the sky is doing something: dust storms and blowing sand on the red desert, snow, blizzards and driving rain on the frozen and urban maps, each with its own looping storm audio, so an outdoor firefight looks and sounds different from an indoor one. At round start the map also picks one of several pre-placed blocker groups and clears it, so which routes out of the landing zone are open changes between rounds while the rest stay walled off. The bulk of what actually ships here, though, is a fire rework: burning tiles left behind by flamethrowers that linger for the better part of a minute, characters catching fire and dropping and rolling to put themselves out, a teammate patting the flames off you, molotovs crafted from bottles, and rounds that ignite what they hit, with per-creature immunities and armour penalties layered on top.

- **Take** — The weather prototypes alone are almost free — a handful of overlay sprites plus looping storm audio that instantly makes any outdoor or planetside map feel like a place rather than a floor tile.
- **Skip** — The weather is the smallest part of the unit: the other forty-odd files are RMC-14's own fire and flammability rework, which replaces upstream burning behaviour and reaches into their xeno, map and temperature systems, so you cannot take the whole thing without importing a large chunk of a marines-vs-xenos codebase.
- **Touches** — all-players, security, antagonist, engineering
- **Needs** — [custom-station-maps](maps.md#custom-station-maps), [xenomorph-hive](antag.md#xenomorph-hive), [shared-utility-libraries](misc.md#shared-utility-libraries)
- **Verified** — partly supported. Fetched all six cited files directly. weather.yml confirms looping storm audio + per-biome overlay sprites (dust/sand/snow/blizzard/rain) exactly as described. TileFireComponent's Duration=55s matches "the better part of a minute". ### Spreading fire

<a id="spreading-fire-system"></a>

Fire that lives on the floor as its own layer, spreading from tile to tile or igniting from flammable spilled reagents with origin tracking and tunable behaviour.

**ephemeral-space**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 2 forks carry it*

Fire becomes a thing that sits on the floor and creeps: a burning tile crackles with its own flame sprite and sound, slows anyone who walks through it, scorches the floor with burn marks, and once a second tries to jump to a neighbouring tile, preferring tiles that are already hot and skipping ones with no oxygen or nothing flammable to catch. Flames come in four visible intensities and burn themselves down as they spread, eventually smouldering to a weaker flame or dying out on their own, so a blaze that gets going grows into a wandering front the crew has to cut off rather than a single fire they stand next to and extinguish. Anything that triggers, from a thrown incendiary to a rigged device, can drop a fire of a chosen intensity, and every fire remembers who started it, so an arsonist's whole spreading blaze is traceable back to them.

- **Take** — It turns fire from a per-entity status effect into a spatial hazard that moves through the station, giving atmos and engineering an actual firefighting job and giving arsonist play a real tool.
- **Skip** — It is not the self-contained fork-namespaced drop-in it looks like: the prototype leans on flammable fields (firestack visual divisor, minimum/maximum firestacks, delete-on-extinguish, basic fire spread) and a per-tile Flammability value that upstream does not have, so grafting it means patching upstream flammable and tile-definition code and then re-patching them on every sync.
- **Touches** — engineering, atmospherics, security, antagonist, all-players
- **Verified** — supported. Every gameplay beat checks out in the actual code: the ESTileFire prototype has four staged sprites/sounds (red_1..4, fire1..4.ogg), SpeedModifierContacts 0.8/0.8 for the floor-slow, TryAddBurntDecalsToTile for scorch marks, edge_spreaders.yml sets updatesPerSecond:1 for spread cadence, OnSpreadNeighbors weights neighbor tiles up for high temperature and zeroes tiles with tileDef.Flammability<=0 or low oxygen, fires lose firestacks on each spread (AdjustFireStacks negative) and the Update() loop either deletes or downgrades to a weaker stage once SmolderTime passes, ESTileFireOnTriggerComponent lets any BaseXOnTriggerComponent consumer (grenades, rigged devices) spawn a chosen Stage, and Origin/ESTileFireOriginComponent propagate through every child fire spawned by a spread, so a whole blaze traces to one originating entity. I then went beyond the cited evidence to settle the graft_shape question, diffing the fork's own Content.Shared/Atmos/Components/FlammableComponent.cs and Content.Shared/Maps/ContentTileDefinition.cs against space-wizards/space-station-14 master. Both files are NOT under the _ES/ namespace and both carry literal '// ES START ... // ES END' blocks in the fork's own tree: FlammableComponent.cs adds FirestackVisualDivisor, DeleteOnExtinguish, BasicFireSpread (and several atmos-energy fields the tile-fire system doesn't even use); ContentTileDefinition.cs adds the Flammability field the spread-scoring code reads. That is direct proof the fork edits upstream-named files in place, not just files that would need editing to integrate elsewhere - the needs-upstream-edits downgrade from the drop-in seed is correct, and correct in the stronger 'fork-patches-upstream' sense. One inaccuracy in the claim's own reasoning: it lists 'minimum/maximum firestacks' as fields 'upstream does not have', but upstream FlammableComponent.cs already defines MinimumFireStacks and MaximumFireStacks (and FireSpread) - only FirestackVisualDivisor, DeleteOnExtinguish and BasicFireSpread are actually new. This doesn't change the verdict (real new fields still force upstream edits) but the claim overstates which specific fields are novel.

**forky**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 2 forks carry it*

Spilled flammable liquid stops being scenery. Get a puddle of welding fuel, booze or other volatile chemistry hot enough (a welder, a hotspot, hot air on the tile) and it catches: the tile lights up with a roaring looping fire sound and a coloured flame that runs orange for weak mixes up through yellow to white-hot for the really volatile stuff, and the puddle visibly burns itself down. While it burns it heats the air on that tile, eats oxygen and belches CO2 and steam, leaves scorch marks on the floor, and reaches into the four neighbouring tiles: any puddle next door catches too, so a spill trail (and, if the server allows it, wet footprints) carries fire across a room, though walls and closed airlocks stop it. Anyone standing in the flames takes burn and structural damage each second, cut down by fire-protective gear, and a thin dribble burns out fast and weak while a big pool burns long and hot.

- **Take** — It gives every flammable spill real physical consequence, chaining chemistry, welding, atmos and arson-flavoured sabotage into one hazard that firefighters actually have to chase across a room, and every knob (damage, volume scaling, footprint flammability, gear protection) is a server CVar rather than a code change.
- **Skip** — It is a per-second per-tile fire sim doing entity lookups, decal writes, atmos mutation and damage on every burning puddle, and it is bolted to fork-only plumbing (a Funky `TileExposedEvent` carrying a spark source, a fork `TileFireEvent`, and the fork's footprint component) plus Starfall-style particle prototypes, so grafting it means importing or re-pointing that atmos wiring and accepting a spill-chain performance risk.
- **Touches** — engineering, atmospherics, medical, chemistry, security, antagonist, all-players
- **Needs** — [atmospherics-simulation](#atmospherics-simulation), [visual-feedback-effects](ui.md#visual-feedback-effects)
- **Verified** — partly supported. All five cited files fetched cleanly and back almost the entire gameplay description: TileExposedEvent-driven ignition with a per-flammability ignition temperature, colour scaling orange→white via GetFireColor, a looping bigfire.ogg sound, per-second burn that heats the tile, consumes O2, produces CO2/water vapor, drops burnt1-4 decals, spreads to the 4 cardinal neighbour tiles, damages standing entities (Heat+Structural) each second reduced by FireProtectionComponent, volume-scaled burn rate (small puddles burn fast/weak via SmallPuddleBurnThreshold/Percent, big ones slow via VolumeScalingReference/Curve), and every tunable (damage, footprint flammability, protection effectiveness, volume curve) is indeed a server-only CVar in ReagentFireCVars.cs. However, I re-read the neighbour-spread loop closely: the `isBlocked` (AirtightComponent) check only gates the atmos heat-radiation branch — the `foreach (var adjPuddle in GetPuddlesOnTile(...)) Ignite(adjPuddle, ...)` call right below runs unconditionally with no isBlocked check, so a puddle on the far side of a closed airlock/wall tile still ignites directly. The claim's 'walls and closed airlocks stop it' is not supported by the code as written. Separately and more importantly for graft_shape: I traced who raises the fork's TileExposedEvent and found it is not raised from any _Funkystation file — it's injected into the vanilla Content.Server/Atmos/EntitySystems/AtmosphereSystem.Hotspot.cs (inside HotspotExpose, wrapped in literal '// Funky start'/'// Funky end' comments), and the fork's TileFireEvent is likewise subscribed to inside the vanilla Content.Server/Atmos/EntitySystems/FlammableSystem.cs (SubscribeLocalEvent<FlammableComponent, TileFireEvent>). Both are genuine upstream-namespaced files edited in place, not fork files sitting in upstream directories by coincidence — this directly falsifies the 'drop-in' path-data seed and confirms the assessor's needs-upstream-edits call, specifically as fork-patches-upstream rather than merely a hypothetical integration cost.
