### Character markings and editor extensions

<a id="character-markings-and-editor"></a>

Expands the character editor with large libraries of markings, hair, tails, tattoos and scars plus extra setup options such as custom species labels and flavour text.

**corvax, cosmatic-drift, ephemeral-space, funky, impstation, rmc14, serbia-strong, stellar-station, sunrise**

*cosmetic · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 12 forks carry it*

In the character setup screen you get far longer lists to scroll: dozens of extra hairstyles and beards, cat and fox ears with separately colourable inner/outer parts, tails that come with a bow or a bell, lizard and moth and spider and snail-person body patterns, vitiligo patches, scars, neck tattoos, earrings, septum rings, eyebrow shapes, glowing freckles, and clown/mime facepaint you can wear as part of your face rather than as an item. A few forks add things you do during the round instead of only in the lobby: Sunrise gives a barber's razor that lets you sit someone down and add, remove or swap one of their hairstyles over a several-second cut with scissor sounds, plus hair dye bottles that recolour hair or beard, and a colour/gradient effect picker so a single marking can fade between two colours instead of being flat. Cosmatic Drift lets you type your own species label for your character, and Ephemeral Space replaces the usual setup with backgrounds (colonist, drifter, mercenary, ex-convict), pre-round relationships to other characters (friend, rival, ex, cousin), and examine text that describes someone's hair, eye and skin colour as identifying clues.

- **Take** — It is the cheapest possible player-visible win: the marking libraries are almost entirely YAML plus sprites, they show up the first time anyone opens character setup, and you can take a single species' file without taking the rest.
- **Skip** — Every marking is a hand-drawn RSI you inherit maintenance and licensing for, most of the interesting sets are keyed to species your fork may not have, and the parts that are actually mechanical (Sunrise's gradient effects and razor UI, Cosmatic Drift's custom species name with its database migration) reach into the upstream humanoid appearance system and character editor rather than sitting in fork files.
- **Touches** — all-players, service, ghost-roles
- **Needs** — [extra-playable-species](#extra-playable-species), [synthetic-and-ipc-species](#synthetic-and-ipc-species)
- **Verified** — partly supported. Fetched the fork sources directly and found two genuine in-place upstream edits, not just fork-namespace additions: Sunrise's Content.Shared/Humanoid/Markings/Marking.cs (an upstream path, no _Sunrise prefix) has a MarkingEffects field wrapped in literal '// sunrise gradient edit start/end' comments, wiring the gradient system straight into the core Marking data type; and Cosmatic Drift's Content.Shared/Preferences/HumanoidCharacterProfile.cs (also an upstream path) carries a CDCustomSpeciesName field plus validation/WithX logic added directly to the vanilla class, backed by dedicated EF Core migrations at Content.Server.Database/Migrations/{Postgres,Sqlite}/2025...._CDCustomSpecies.cs. That confirms the fork-patches-upstream reading exactly for the two mechanical pieces the claim's skip_because called out (Sunrise gradient/razor, CD custom species name); the rest of the unit (Impstation gastropoid markings, Funky facepaint, ES backgrounds/relationships/clues) is genuinely self-contained fork-namespace data, so the unit is a real mix rather than uniformly drop-in.

### Extra playable species

<a id="extra-playable-species"></a>

Additional playable species that reuse the upstream humanoid rig: the standard MobHumanoid-derived body prototype, the standard humanoid inventory template and the standard humanoid sprite layers, with the fork supplying only new species prototypes, organs, name datasets, markings, sprites and guidebook entries. Alien flavour does not move a species out of this id, body plan does: if it still wears a jumpsuit on a two-arm/two-leg doll, it is here. A species that ships its own body-part graph, its own inventory template or a non-bipedal rig belongs to `non-humanoid-species-pack`, and a multi-species pack is filed whole, never split across the two ids.

**corvax, cosmatic-drift, gaby-station-now-dumont-station, impstation, monolith, rmc14, serbia-strong**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 13 forks carry it*

At character creation you can pick from extra species beyond the upstream roster (cat-people, foxfolk, rat-people, bird-like Avali, shrimp-like Anomalocarids, hulking Ungu, shapeshifting Kodepiia, Thaven, Protogen and more), each with its own sprites, name pools, markings and a guidebook page explaining how it plays. Choosing one changes how your shift actually feels: Avali survive down to -50C but get shoved over easily, Rodentia stash small items in their cheeks and talk gibberish while doing it and starve a third faster, Ungu are too bulky to wear any hardsuit so must use two-piece EVA suits and stay conscious to 150 damage, Kodepiia can only digest raw meat and choke on waste gas, and Anomalocarids must periodically vent steam or overheat. Chemists, doctors and quartermasters have to account for species that burn in the heat, freeze in the cold, or refuse the standard food and suits.

- **Take** — Species are the single most visible identity lever a fork has, and the packs are mostly data (species prototypes, organs, name datasets, markings, sprites, guidebook pages) with real mechanical texture already tuned and documented for players.
- **Skip** — Every new humanoid silhouette drags a long tail of upstream work: per-species clothing/hardsuit sprite layers, loadout and job wiring, markings points, food and atmos balance, plus rules text (the Impstation entries lean on server-specific naming and RP rules), and each species is permanent maintenance against upstream humanoid churn.

- **Needs** — [character-markings-and-editor](#character-markings-and-editor), [assigned-roleplay-moods](roleplay.md#assigned-roleplay-moods)
- **Verified** — supported. ### Species racial abilities

<a id="species-racial-abilities"></a>

Per-species active and passive abilities such as melee bonuses, hairballs, sprints that leave trails, mouth storage, gliding, blink teleports and reach limits for short species.

**sunrise**

*loop-change · entangled · medium confidence · 12 forks carry it*

Picking certain species gives you buttons nobody else has. A cat-person can crouch over a bleeding crewmate (or themselves) and lick the wound to slow the bleeding, but only on bare skin, so the patient has to be undressed first. Winged crew get a "Spread Wings" toggle and a "Take Flight" toggle that trades stamina for a big burst of speed. Small bird-like crew get a short-range dash they can fire off three times before it has to recharge, letting them cross a room or a gap almost instantly. Other odd trinkets sit on top: characters who can conjure a bar of soap, a cookie or a candy out of nowhere, dog-tinted or heart-tinted vision, and a set of flip/jump/dance/lie-down emotes with real animations that anyone can use.

- **Take** — Species stop being pure cosmetics and start playing differently — a flying or dashing crewmate changes chases, escapes and station traversal in a way a hair-and-tail reskin never does.
- **Skip** — The interesting half of it (flight, dash, licking) is written for Sunrise's own added species and hangs off their mob prototypes, so grafting means both patching your species/mob prototypes to grant the actions and re-balancing mobility abilities that let players skip the station layout your maps assume.
- **Touches** — all-players, medical, security, service, movement
- **Needs** — [extra-playable-species](#extra-playable-species), [speech-accents-and-emotes](roleplay.md#speech-accents-and-emotes)
- **Verified** — supported; assessed as needs upstream edits, corrected by the verifier. Gameplay text checks out point for point: felinid.yml's own description literally says the target "must necessarily be undressed"; milira.yml gives exactly a Spread Wings toggle and a Take Flight toggle ("trading stamina for speed"); jump.yml's JumpMoth variant (sprite bird_call, used by the resomi mob prototype) has LimitedCharges maxCharges 3 with AutoRecharge, and the resomi mob prototype's JumpAbility component sets jumpDistance 5 — a genuine short dash, not a walk-through-walls exploit; FabricateSoap/Cookie/Candy components spawn exactly Soap, FoodBakedCookieOatmeal, FoodGumball and FoodLollipop; DogVisionComponent plus a lovevision.yml shader (found alongside dogvision.yml) match the dog/heart-vision claim; animations.yml's Flip/Jump/Dance/Lay emotes whitelist only generic Body/Hands components, so any playable character can use them as claimed. On graft_shape: the felinid/milira/resomi ability-granting components (FelinidLicking, WingToggle, JumpAbility) all live on brand-new mob prototypes entirely under Resources/Prototypes/_Sunrise/Entities/Mobs/Species/ — so that half really is self-contained new-species content, supporting the claim's "hangs off Sunrise's own added species" reasoning (integration-would-require-upstream-edits: a target repo would have to edit its own existing species/mob prototypes to grant these actions to non-Sunrise species). But the path-data "zero files outside namespace" premise is not actually true for this unit: Resources/Prototypes/Shaders/dogvision.yml is a shader prototype the DogVision ability needs, and it sits directly in the base game's Resources/Prototypes/Shaders/ directory (next to cooldown.yml, greyscale.yml, outline.yml, etc.), not under any _Sunrise path — a real fork-owned file living in upstream territory. So both upstream_reading branches are live: the fork's own copy does drop at least one file into an upstream directory, and separately, grafting the ability-granting mechanism onto a destination fork's existing species would mean editing that destination's own species/mob prototypes. Given the mix of self-contained new-species content plus a real dependency planted in a shared upstream directory plus its own already-flagged extra-playable-species dependency, "needs-upstream-edits" undersells how tangled this is for an "adopted by 12 / drop-in-seed" shortlist entry — it reads more like should-be-entangled than a clean single-flavor needs-upstream-edits case.

**starlight**

*structural · entangled · medium confidence · 12 forks carry it*

Playing a Shadekin, your character carries a "core" organ whose energy rises and falls with how dark the area around you is, shown as a five-step alert on your HUD (from pitch dark to dangerously bright) alongside an energy counter. Veteran Shadekin players who meet the requirements can roll as a Bright-eye: an otherworldly visitor who spends that energy to tear open a rift to "The Dark" on the station, blink onto someone to stun, blind and drag them into darkness, drop shadow snares that ensnare and stun whoever walks into them, and conjure a shade that follows them around. Their goals are to keep a rift open and to protect their core, so the crew's counterplay is to hunt for the core (which is worth a fortune to science and can't just be swapped into another Shadekin), flood the area with light, or use bluespace-crystal gear, goggles and anchors that destabilise the core and pin the Bright-eye out of nullspace. The same folder also adds smaller body-based quirks: an integrated gas-tank organ that gives a species its own internals toggle and tank readout instead of a backpack tank, a breath-holding effect, and a gnome curse that shrinks a victim, dresses them in a gnome outfit and replaces their scream with an endless giggle.

- **Take** — It turns a species pick into a genuine round-shaping threat with real crew counterplay (light, bluespace crystals, surgery for the core) rather than a cosmetic reskin, and the integrated gas-tank organ is a genuinely nicer internals experience for any breath-odd species.
- **Skip** — It is not a species ability pack you can lift a piece of: the Bright-eye antag needs the Shadekin species, the core organ, Starlight's nullspace dimension, a "The Dark" status effect, the Burned/Nightblind traits, playtime trackers and a pile of bespoke counterplay items, plus new maps/sprites, so the smallest honest graft is the whole subsystem and its lore.
- **Touches** — antagonist, science, medical, security, all-players
- **Needs** — [extra-playable-species](#extra-playable-species), [character-traits](roleplay.md#character-traits)
- **Verified** — partly supported. Read the cited guidebook, alert, objective, antag-role, abilities, gnome and breath-organ files directly. Nearly every specific gameplay beat checks out verbatim: the 5-state light alert (dark/low/normal/high/dangerous) plus a GenericCounterAlert energy counter; Shadeskip (blink+stun+blind+TheDark status), DarkTrap (ensnare+stun shadow snare), CreateShade, and Portal-to-The-Dark abilities; the antag role's SpeciesRequirement:Shadekin + inverted TraitsRequirement:Burned/Nightblind + 48h OverallPlaytimeRequirement gating; the core's guidebook text stating it can't be transplanted between Shadekin and is worth a fortune to science; the Gnome curse shrinking height, swapping outfit, and hijacking the Scream emote into a giggle plus a periodic auto-giggle emote; and the OrganBreathToolComponent giving an integrated gas-tank/internals-toggle organ. The graft_shape='entangled' downgrade from the drop-in seed is also directly confirmed: the Brighteye antag role's own prototype requires the Shadekin species (defined in a separate Species prototype file, i.e. the extra-playable-species unit) and two specific character traits (Burned, Nightblind, i.e. the character-traits unit) to exist, exactly matching the claimed depends_on. The one real defect is a small item-function mixup around the bluespace-crystal countermeasure gear (see overclaims) that does not change the overall verdict.
  - unsupported: "use bluespace-crystal gear, goggles and anchors that destabilise the core and pin the Bright-eye out of nullspace" — per the guidebook text itself, the goggles ('designed to allow the wearer to peer into NullSpace') only grant vision into nullspace; it is the Bluespace Crystal that destabilizes the core and the Nullspace Anchor (plus an unlisted Nullspace Drainer) that root/restrain entities out of nullspace. The claim lumps all three items into one destabilize-and-pin function the goggles don't actually have.

**impstation**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 12 forks carry it*

Each of Impstation's odd species gets its own button on the action bar, so picking a species is picking a way to play rather than just a sprite. A snail-person burns their thirst meter to sprint and leaves a slick trail of mucus behind them, a crab-like anomalocarid vents scalding steam out of its shell (and gets increasingly panicked "I'm burning from the inside out!" warnings if it waits too long) or spits a burst of water at someone, a bird-thing tears a chunk of flesh out of a downed victim and can rapidly rescramble its own face into a new one, a psychic can beam a cryptic vision into a stranger's head or pop a small animal from across the room, and a bull can charge forward into whoever it's aiming at. The mime gets a proper toolkit of mimed props that become real: invisible wall, chair, box and a three-tile blockade, all locked behind keeping the vow of silence (they can't even type on an AAC tablet). There are matching drawbacks too: very short characters can't reach a machine's console unless they're buckled into a chair, standing on a table or floating, some species physically can't two-hand a weapon, and hoofed species need special fat-handled tools to work at all.

- **Take** — It turns species choice into a real mechanical decision with both a signature toy and a signature handicap, and the individual pieces (a sprint that costs thirst, a heat vent, a charge, a mimed prop) are small self-contained abilities you can cherry-pick one at a time.
- **Skip** — Almost all of it is bolted to Impstation's own invented species roster, so grafting any single ability means deciding which of your species gets it and hand-wiring it into that species' prototypes; the drawback half (short-for-UI, can't-wield, cow tools) also gates upstream machine, wielding and tool interactions, which means balance complaints and edge-case bug reports on systems you didn't previously own.
- **Touches** — all-players, service, security, medical
- **Needs** — [extra-playable-species](#extra-playable-species), [assigned-roleplay-moods](roleplay.md#assigned-roleplay-moods)
- **Verified** — supported. Every specific gameplay claim checked out against actual code/locale: anomalocarid HeatVentSystem.cs has the exact escalating "too hot" popup mechanic (HeatStored/HeatDamageThreshold ratio picks from TooHotPopups) plus a separate water-spit gun; gastropoid's MobGastropoid prototype has SnailSprintComponent with thirstCost:100, speedBoost:1.5, reagentProduced:Mucin (i.e., burns thirst, leaves mucus trail, exactly as described); kodepiia's Consume/Scrambler actions and ftl match "tears a chunk of flesh" and face-rescramble; pleebnar has both Telepathy (EntityTargetAction, vision to a target) and Gib (small-animal-targeted) actions; ungu's ActionDash matches "charge forward"; the mime toolkit is a byte-for-byte match (ActionMimeInvisibleWall/Chair/Box/Blockade, the blockade literally three tiles wide, gated by ActionMimeVowRequirement, plus the AAC-tablet-block locale string). Crucially, for the graft_shape question: CowToolProficiencyComponent is not consumed cleanly through an attempt-event like the other two drawbacks — I opened the actual upstream file Content.Shared/Tools/Systems/SharedToolSystem.cs (not an _Impstation path) and it contains `using Content.Shared._Impstation.Tools.Components; // imp` plus an inline `// imp edit start` block that checks CowToolComponent/CowToolProficiencyComponent to alter tool doAfter duration. That is a real in-place edit of an upstream file living inside the fork's own copy, which the path-only seed could never see (it only sees _Impstation-prefixed paths). This directly validates the claim's "needs-upstream-edits" call and specifically the fork-patches-upstream reading, not just a hypothetical integration cost.

**monolith**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 12 forks carry it*

Playing a Reptilian here means your hands are a weapon that keeps growing on you. Claws pass through five visible stages: at the shortest you punch for 8 slash bare-handed, at the longest for 18 slash plus 12 piercing, and from the middle stage on you can wide-swing into a crowd instead of disarming. Everything else gets worse as they grow. Held weapons lose damage (down to half at the top stage), gun spread widens from 10% to 45%, and at the final stage you cannot pull a trigger at all: "You can't fit claws in the trigger guard", leaving you bows only. Anyone who examines you can read your stage off the line they get, from "You can barely see their claws" up to "Their claws are abnormally long". Wirecutters double as nail clippers: a do-after on yourself or on a patient shaves a stage off and resets growth progress, so a lizard who wants to carry a gun this shift keeps coming back for a trim. Chemistry works the other lever, with one reagent boosting growth twentyfold and another freezing it entirely. The dark end is declawing: a clipping attempt can rip the claws out permanently for 20 slash damage, after which the victim shakes and then flings whatever is in both hands onto the floor whenever they hold something too long.

- **Take** — It is a genuine risk/reward loop rather than a flat species stat bonus: growing claws trade melee power against guns and tools, and it gives wirecutters, chemistry and a permanent maiming interaction all a real job.
- **Skip** — It is a whole Reptilian rework rather than an ability you bolt on, it needs the reptilian mob and wirecutter prototypes patched to carry the components, and the gun-spread half is wired to a Delta-V accuracy-modifier component you would have to drag across too; permanent declawing also needs a policy call before you ship it.
- **Touches** — all-players, security, medical, antagonist
- **Verified** — supported. Every specific number in the gameplay text checks out against reptilian.yml: stage damages 8/11/13+2p/15+5p/18+12p slash across the 5 growth stages, melee coefficients 1.0/0.95/0.87/0.75/0.5 (half at top stage), gun spread multipliers 1.1/1.25/1.45 (10% to 45%), canWideSwing flipping on from stage 2 (the true middle of 0-4), stage 4 canShoot:false plus the exact locale line \"You can't fit claws in the trigger guard\", the five examine lines verbatim from claw.ftl, the 20 Slash DamageOnDeclaw, and Declaw.cs's jitter-then-drop-on-both-hands behavior after the 30s hold timer. I also fetched the two upstream-path files the claim's skip_because names (Resources/Prototypes/Entities/Mobs/Species/reptilian.yml and Resources/Prototypes/Entities/Objects/Tools/tools.yml, neither in the cited evidence) and confirmed Monolith has edited them in place: BaseMobReptilian's parent list literally includes ReptilianClaws, and tools.yml's Wirecutter entity carries \"type: NailClipper # Mono\". SharedClawsSystem.cs also imports Content.Shared._DV.Weapons.Ranged.Components.PlayerAccuracyModifierComponent directly, confirming the stated Delta-V gun-accuracy dependency. So the claim's needs-upstream-edits shape is not just plausible but directly demonstrated: this fork's own tree already patches upstream-owned prototype files for this feature, and grafting it elsewhere would require the same edits.

**cosmatic-drift**

*activity · drop-in · medium confidence · 12 forks carry it*

Rodentia characters get an "Open cheek storage" action button that opens a tiny 3x1 pouch inside their own mouth, so a rat person can pocket a couple of small items with nothing visible in their hands or bag. It is deliberately leaky: anyone who examines them reads a yellow line saying the person has something in their mouth, and the contents spill onto the floor the moment they get knocked down, disarmed, or take more than a token amount of damage. Living creatures cannot be stuffed in there, and the fork's code also checks whether the mouth is occupied so a full mouth gets in the way of ordinary mouth use.

- **Take** — It is a complete, small smuggling mechanic rather than a free extra pocket: the examine tell plus the drop-on-knockdown/disarm/damage rules give security a real counterplay, so it can be dropped in without becoming the obvious best place to hide contraband.
- **Skip** — It is one action and one 3-slot container, so the whole payoff is a niche flavour pocket for a single species; you also inherit a hand-rolled storage-in-a-container pattern that has to be re-checked against upstream storage and damage events on every sync.
- **Touches** — all-players, security, antagonist
- **Verified** — partly supported. All five evidence files plus the server/client systems were re-read and back the action, yellow examine tell, dump-on-Downed/Disarmed/damage-threshold, MindContainer blacklist, and mouth-blocked checks (mumble accent, ingestion cancel). The one contradicted specific is the container size: the grid is 0,0,2,1 = 3x2 (6 cells), not the claimed 3x1/3-slot. Drop-in confirmed: every file is under _CD namespaces, and the action's OpenStorageImplantEvent is handled by upstream's own SharedStorageSystem.OnImplantActivate on StorageComponent (verified identical in space-wizards master), so no upstream edits are patched or required; the only fork coupling is a YAML component block on the fork's Rodentia species.
  - unsupported: "a tiny 3x1 pouch" — the CheekStorage grid is 0,0,2,1, which is 3x2 (6 cells) under SS14's inclusive grid convention
  - unsupported: "one 3-slot container" (skip_because) — same error; it is a 6-cell 3x2 grid

*activity · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 12 forks carry it*

### Synthetic and IPC characters

<a id="synthetic-and-ipc-species"></a>

Playable machine-bodied crew that run on battery charge, are repaired with tools rather than medicine, and carry their own status UI and traits.

**backmen, corvax, cosmatic-drift, far-horizons, rmc14, serbia-strong**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 2 forks carry it*

You can roll a machine-bodied crewmember at character creation instead of a fleshy one. Instead of eating and bleeding you run on a power cell sitting behind a screwdriver-openable wire panel in your chest, and when you get low you right-click an APC, a charger or a spare cell and drain it (a "Ready to drain" toggle sits on your hotbar, and a "Battery charging disabled" message tells you when someone has cut you off). Your head is a monitor: an action opens a face picker with a few dozen expressions (hearts, static, goggles, RGB, a shower, a music note) that you can swap mid-round like a mood. When you get hurt the medbay is the wrong department for you: your limbs are their own machine parts that take extra damage from EMPs, and you get patched with tools and welding rather than chems and a bed. Different forks layer different extras on top: built-in radio, a thermal-regulation system that makes you care about overheating, installable modules, a lockable chassis, and positive/negative "custom frame" traits. RMC-14 does something different again, letting a synth pick a generation via an action, where each generation is a preset package of skills, movement speed, health threshold and damage resistances rather than a cosmetic choice. Cosmatic Drift's version is the featherweight one, just synthetic blood and a robot typing indicator on an otherwise normal character.

- **Take** — It is the most-requested "I want to play a robot" species and it rewrites one player's whole survival loop from food-and-doctors to power-and-engineers without needing a new antagonist or gamemode to justify it.
- **Skip** — A species that cannot be healed the normal way leaks into every healing, damage, food, blood and rejuvenate path in the game, so a half-done graft leaves you with a crewmember medbay silently cannot treat and cloning cannot bring back; the two richest versions here (Far Horizons, RMC-14) are built on those forks' own limb-damage and skill systems and will not travel without them.
- **Touches** — all-players, medical, engineering, science
- **Needs** — [character-markings-and-editor](#character-markings-and-editor), [extra-playable-species](#extra-playable-species), [surgery-and-limb-wounds](medical.md#surgery-and-limb-wounds)
- **Verified** — supported. The claim's specifics check out unusually well: Corvax's ipc.ftl/actions/face-profiles confirm the exact drain toggle strings ("Ready to drain", "Battery charging disabled") and specific face names quoted (Heart, Static, Goggles, Rgb, Shower, Music all present verbatim in ipc_face_profiles.yml). Far Horizons' IPCSystem.Battery.cs confirms the wire-panel-gated eject/insert and right-click alt-verb drain mechanic, IPCWeldClosesWoundsComponent confirms welding-heals-instead-of-chems, and the Body/Species/ipc.yml file contains literal `OrganEmpTarget` / `Shock: 3` entries on every limb, directly confirming the "extra damage from EMPs" line. RMC-14's synth_generations.yml shows each generation is exactly a bundle of Skills + MovementSpeedModifier + MobThresholds + damageModifier, matching the claim precisely. Cosmatic Drift's SynthSystem.cs is exactly "synthetic blood + robot typing indicator," nothing more, matching the "featherweight" framing. On graft shape, I found the concrete evidence the seed said path-data couldn't see: Far Horizons' own repo has `Content.Shared/Body/VisualOrganMarkingsComponent.cs`, a file outside the `_FarHorizons/` namespace, carrying an inline `// FH` tag marking a field FH itself added — the fork genuinely does patch a shared upstream-style file in place (fork-patches-upstream), which is what the claim's own graft_shape="needs-upstream-edits" call implicitly relies on and what the seed's drop-in guess missed. Separately, RMC-14's Skills system is fully self-contained under `_RMC14/`, so its own repo doesn't patch upstream, but its synth generations are functionally inert without RMC's skill/movement framework — exactly the "integration-would-require-upstream-edits" reading the assessor's skip_because argues. Both readings are independently true for different forks in this bundle.

### Non-humanoid species pack

<a id="non-humanoid-species-pack"></a>

Playable species that cannot use the upstream humanoid rig and ship their own body plan: a bespoke body-part graph, a custom inventory template, a non-bipedal or taur sprite rig, or bespoke damage and atmospherics handling such as water-breathing vaporizers. Packs are indivisible: every species shipped in one vendored species directory takes this id in every fork that vendors it, including the originating fork. The Macrocosm pack (`Resources/Prototypes/_MACRO/Species/` = Decapoid, Gastropoid, Allulalo, Gray, Ant, Apid, plus their markings, textures and guidebook pages) is that case and is canonically here, not in `extra-playable-species`, even for the members that look humanoid.

**impstation, sunrise**

*loop-change · entangled · high confidence · 5 forks carry it*

These are playable species that break the normal human body plan, so picking one changes how you play every job for the whole round. An Impstation Decapoid is a crab: it survives vacuum and cold bare-skinned and scuttles fast in zero-g, but walks slowly under gravity, can't wear shoes, can't fire guns, and its huge left claw is a pry bar, cutter, melee weapon and raisable shield rather than a hand, so it effectively plays one-handed. It also can't speak or breathe without its irreplaceable vaporizer and vapor tank, which examine as full/low/bad/empty and need water from atmos when they run dry, and medics can't inject it at all because syringes won't pierce the shell, forcing pills and cryo instead. An Allulalo is a tiny fast saurian that stands on chairs to reach consoles, holds one item, and bites allies to dose them with a clotting adrenaline toxin; Apids fly permanently, hold one item, can never wear a backpack or jetpack, and arrive as cargo-purchased cryo pods that ghosts wake up in as full crew. Sunrise's half is quieter: xeno, predator and demon playables with their own body sprites and their own inventory windows (a xeno gets bra/pants slots and is blocked from hardsuits).

- **Take** — A Decapoid or Apid is a genuine self-imposed difficulty mode that reshapes an entire shift of play (one hand, no guns, no injections, a breathing consumable you must maintain) rather than being a reskin, and Impstation ships in-game guidebooks that already explain all of it to players.
- **Skip** — Every one of these species is a permanent tax on the rest of the codebase: each new hardsuit, syringe, backpack, chair-height console or hand-requiring interaction has to be re-checked against a one-handed, shoeless, unpierceable, wing-blocked body, and the vaporizer is an irreplaceable item whose loss is a hard round-ruiner that admins will be asked to fix.
- **Touches** — all-players, medical, atmospherics, security, cargo, ghost-roles, science
- **Needs** — [character-markings-and-editor](#character-markings-and-editor)
- **Verified** — partly supported. I pulled the full impstation and sunrise-station repo trees plus the cited files directly. Decapoid guidebook and vaporizer.ftl confirm one-handed claw/no-shoes/no-firearms/no-injection/vapor-dependent breathing verbatim; Allulalo and Apid guidebooks confirm chair-standing, one-item hold, bite-clotting-toxin, permanent flight, and cargo-purchased cryo-pod-as-full-crew claims. Sunrise's humanoid_xeno inventory template shows the cited bra/pants slots and a Hardsuit tag blacklist exactly as claimed, and demon.yml/predator.yml both use their own markingLimits/dollPrototype/bodyTypes tied into the shared marking/character-editor system, supporting the stated character-markings-and-editor dependency and the 'entangled' verdict. The repo tree also shows Decapoid-specific sprite files added inside upstream Resources/Textures/Clothing/Belt/*.rsi folders and a Content.Shared/_Impstation/Decapoids/Silicons subsystem hooking into borg/AI code, both of which independently corroborate downgrading away from a clean drop-in and toward entanglement with other fork/upstream systems (equipment tagging, hardsuit whitelist/blacklist checks, silicon interop) — consistent with the claim's own 'entangled' call, which I confirm rather than override.
  - unsupported: Sunrise's half is quieter: xeno, predator and demon playables with their own body sprites and their own inventory windows (a xeno gets bra/pants slots and is blocked from hardsuits)" — only HumanoidXeno actually has a distinct inventory template (`type: Inventory` with `templateId: humanoidxeno` in Resources/Prototypes/_Sunrise/Entities/Mobs/Species/humanoid_xeno.yml, matching the cited humanoid_xeno_inventory_template.yml with bra/pants slots and a Hardsuit blacklist). So 'their own inventory windows' is true only for xeno, not for predator/demon as stated.
  - unsupported: "its examine as full/low/bad/empty" for the vaporizer — the actual Fluent keys in Resources/Locale/en-US/_Impstation/decapoid/vaporizer.ftl are vaporizer-examine-state-normal/-low/-bad/-empty ("functioning normally", not "full"); a minor wording mismatch.
