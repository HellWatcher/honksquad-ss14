### Firearms and ammunition expansion

<a id="firearms-content-expansion"></a>

A larger gun roster with new ammunition, magazines and projectiles, plus gun subsystems such as fire modes, bipods, accuracy modifiers and firing restrictions on who may pull the trigger.

**ephemeral-space, forky, funky, gaby-station-now-dumont-station, goobstation, impstation, monolith, rmc14, serbia-strong, starlight, stellar-station, viva-station**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 10 forks carry it*

Players get a much larger armoury: extra pistols, revolvers, snipers, SMGs, shotguns and energy guns, each with its own calibre of boxed cartridges, magazines and speedloaders to print, carry and reload, plus themed rounds like ion bolts, disabler spreads, a clown-launching shot and a .45 magnum "temporal" round. Guns also gain handling depth you feel in a fight: a right-click menu to pick which bolt an energy weapon fires, screw-on barrel and sight attachments listed when you examine the gun, weapons that only work when dual-wielded, and an ammo readout that names what you are loading ("12/24 Shells") instead of showing a bare number. Several forks also gate the trigger itself, so a weapon missing its firing mechanism just clicks uselessly and some guns break outright in the hands of a species they were not built for.

- **Take** — It is the cheapest way to make firefights feel less samey: the ammo-type and fire-mode layers turn "point gun, hold click" into an actual loadout decision, and most of the content is plain prototype data you can cherry-pick a calibre at a time.
- **Skip** — Every fork wires its gun subsystems (fire modes, accuracy, firing pins, dual-wield, attachment UI) into upstream's shared gun and projectile code, so you inherit merge pain on the single hottest upstream file family, and the twelve implementations disagree enough that you cannot mix two of them without picking a winner.
- **Touches** — security, antagonist, cargo, science, all-players
- **Verified** — partly supported. All 10 cited evidence files check out on their specific content: goobstation's ammo_selector_protos.yml really does define BulletEnergyGunIon ("Ion bolt"), BulletDisablerSmgSpread ("Disabler bolt spread"), and BulletEnergyGunClownshot ("sending clowns, and only clowns, flying away"); goob_gun.ftl really has both the fire-mode-select verb and a RequiresDualWieldComponent string; viva's magnumtemporal.yml really defines a ".45 magnum temporal" cartridge; starlight's firingpins.ftl really has "firing-pin-weapon-failure = The gun clicks." for a missing firing mechanism; Dumont's SpeciesRestrictedTriggerComponent.cs really breaks a weapon's trigger for a configured species list; stellar's ammo.ftl really supports a named-count readout. The one gap: ephemeral-space's cited attachments.ftl (9 lines total) only defines a "barrel" attachment slot name — I pulled the full ESSharedGunAttachmentsSystem.cs and its component files and found no "sight"/"scope" slot anywhere in that fork's attachment code, so "screw-on barrel and sight attachments" overclaims a second attachment type the evidence doesn't show (may exist in some other fork not cited here, but not in what was cited). On graft_shape: I settled it by diffing against real upstream (space-wizards/space-station-14). Ephemeral-space's attachment system and Dumont's species-trigger/broken-trigger system are both 100% additive — they subscribe to pre-existing vanilla events (GunRefreshModifiersEvent, ShotAttemptedEvent, both byte-for-byte identical to current upstream) and touch zero upstream files; those two slices alone would be near drop-in. But goobstation's Content.Shared/Weapons/Ranged/Systems/BatteryWeaponFireModesSystem.cs — sitting at the plain upstream path with no fork-namespace marker — is a genuinely modified copy of the real upstream file (removed `partial`, swapped the injected prototype manager, changed PopupEntity→PopupClient), i.e. goobstation's copy really does diverge from upstream in an upstream directory. So the seed's needs-upstream-edits holds, but the mechanism differs by fork: some forks (goobstation) directly carry a diverged file in upstream's own path, while others (ephemeral-space, Dumont) achieve the same feature family purely through existing extension points with no upstream edits at all.
  - unsupported: "screw-on barrel and sight attachments listed when you examine the gun" — the cited ephemeral-space evidence (attachments.ftl, and the full ESSharedGunAttachmentsSystem.cs/component files) only defines a "barrel" attachment slot; no "sight" slot exists anywhere in that fork's attachment code.

### Melee weapons, shields and blocking

<a id="melee-weapons-and-blocking"></a>

Added melee weapons plus the defensive layer around them: rechargeable shields, blocking, parrying, whetstones and personal energy shields.

**goobstation**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 5 forks carry it*

Melee fights stop being pure damage trades and become about what you are holding. Goob adds a big rack of hand weapons (a crafted hammer, canes, sabres, the Head of Security's self-recharging "Justice" energy sword that can be flipped between a cutting mode and a stun mode, a Syndicate contractor baton that ion-shocks borgs and staggers people, flatcaps with glass shards sewn into the brim in plasma/uranium variants, boomerangs that fly back to your hand after a throw) alongside a defensive layer: the Blueshield's battery-powered greatshield soaks a large fraction of incoming blunt, slash, piercing and heat damage even when you are not actively blocking, drains its charge doing so, hums and glows while raised, and has a slot to sheath your baton in. There are also gloves that teach the wearer Krav Maga while worn. Some blades cut deeper wounds than their raw damage suggests, and several of these are theft-target or contraband items that antagonists and security both want.

- **Take** — It turns melee from a damage-number exchange into a real defensive game (passive vs active blocking, shield charge as a resource, weapons with two firing modes) and hands security and antags a much wider, more characterful weapon roster.
- **Skip** — The good parts lean on Goob's own extensions to blocking, item-switching and the wound system rather than upstream's plain BlockingComponent, so you cannot take just the shields without porting or reimplementing that combat plumbing, and rebalancing a whole new melee roster against your existing security kit is ongoing work.
- **Touches** — security, antagonist, command, service, all-players
- **Needs** — [martial-arts](#martial-arts), [surgery-and-limb-wounds](medical.md#surgery-and-limb-wounds)
- **Verified** — partly supported. shields.yml, contractor_baton.yml, justice.yml, bladed_caps.yml, hammer.yml, kravmagagloves.yml and BoomerangComponent.cs all read exactly as claimed (passive/active block fractions, battery-drain-on-block, baton sheath slot, StunBorgsOnHit+Ion:35 on the contractor baton, self-recharging battery on Justice, plasma/uranium bladed flatcaps, Construction-graph hammer, ClothingGrantComponent Krav Maga, boomerang return fields). But going beyond the cited evidence to check the C# backing the shields: Content.Shared/Blocking/BlockingSystem.cs sits at the literal upstream path (no _Goobstation prefix) and, diffed against space-wizards/space-station-14 master, is substantially rewritten with an inline "// Goobstation" marker on an added ItemToggleSystem dependency and older API idioms than current upstream -- a fork copy patching an upstream-path file in place, not a self-contained addition, exactly the case path-data can't see. RechargeableBlocking additionally lives under Content.Server/_White/Blocking (a merged sub-fork, not _Goobstation), and the Justice sword's ItemSwitch plumbing lives under Content.Shared/_Shitmed/ItemSwitch (the wound/surgery sub-fork) -- neither is drop-in-clean even by the fork's own internal boundaries.
  - unsupported: "the Head of Security's self-recharging 'Justice' energy sword that can be flipped between a cutting mode and a stun mode" -- justice.yml's ItemSwitch actually defines five states (off/stun/ion/lethal/empowered), including an ion mode and an explosive 'empowered' mode with ExplodeOnMeleeHit; describing it as a two-mode cutting/stun toggle understates what the file shows.
  - unsupported: "a crafted hammer, canes, sabres" -- canes and sabres are not among the evidence_cited paths and were not found in any file fetched; only the hammer (hammer.yml) is verified.

**rmc14**

*loop-change · entangled · medium confidence · 5 forks carry it*

Instead of every swing and shot landing automatically, characters carry a dodge rating: small or invisible targets are harder to hit, big ones easier, and lying down makes you easier to hit, with a separate looser roll for shots from your own side so friendly fire past a squadmate often whiffs. On the alien side, several castes get a temporary absorbing shield they pay plasma for and trigger as an ability, with its own overlay on the sprite, a shatter sound when it runs out and a slow drain that eats it if nobody hits you; some flavours also soak explosions for a couple of seconds. Melee itself gets a training layer, so a trained marine hits harder with the same weapon than an untrained one, some weapons stun on contact or do bonus damage against particular targets, some creatures shrug off punches entirely, you cannot hit yourself unless you opt in, and a dedicated key fires a held weapon's special function without digging through a right-click menu.

- **Take** — The dodge-and-shield layer turns flat trade-blows melee into something with defensive counterplay, and the separate friendly-fire miss chance is a genuinely elegant fix for the "my own team shot me in the back" problem.
- **Skip** — It is built for a Colonial Marines round, not a station one: the shields are alien caste powers keyed to a hive and plasma economy, and the melee damage scaling reads from a marine skills/rank system, so grafting it means bringing those systems or gutting the parts that make it interesting.
- **Touches** — security, antagonist, all-players, command
- **Needs** — [xenomorph-hive](antag.md#xenomorph-hive), [squads-ranks-and-skills](jobs.md#squads-ranks-and-skills)
- **Verified** — partly supported. All cited files are RMC-14's own _RMC14 namespace files (self-contained, no upstream-directory edits in this unit's evidence), but SharedRMCMeleeWeaponSystem.cs directly imports and calls into Content.Shared._RMC14.Marines.Skills (_skills.ApplyMeleeSkillModifier, _skills.GetSkill for RMCMeleeDamageSkillComponent) and Content.Shared._RMC14.Xenonids (_xenoQuery checks in ImmuneToUnarmed and MeleeReceivedMultiplier logic), and the shield system's ShieldType enum and components (VanguardShieldComponent, CrusherShieldComponent) are named for specific Xenomorph castes with plasma costs — confirming genuine functional entanglement with the fork's separate skills/rank system and hive/xenomorph system, exactly as the claim states, even though file-locality alone (path data) would read as drop-in.
  - unsupported: "lying down makes you easier to hit" as part of the dodge-rating system: EvasionComponent's only extra modifiers found in EvasionSystem.cs are size (SizeSmall +10, SizeBig -10) and Rest (-15); DownedEvent/StoodEvent only trigger a recompute with no downed-specific evasion penalty visible anywhere in the evasion code. The only prone-related combat effect actually found is a flat damage multiplier (gun.Comp.ProneDamageMult) applied to point-blank ammo shots on downed targets in CMGunSystem.cs — a damage bonus, not a hit-chance/evasion mechanic, and scoped to point-blank shots rather than general ranged combat.

**sunrise**

*loop-change · entangled · medium confidence · 5 forks carry it*

Sunrise fills out the melee shelf with weapons that each change a fight in a specific way: a pocketable energy shield you flick open to soak hits and bounce lasers until its battery runs dry (it recharges itself after a lull), a two-handed double energy sword that only lights up when wielded and blocks well while held, a wieldable double stun baton that knocks the wind out of two people at once, a switchblade that flicks from pocket-lint to stabbing knife, a heavy breaching hammer that pries airlocks open and caves in structures when swung two-handed, a shock-cane, and an exotic scythe that flings molten projectiles while wielded. A whetstone-style sharpener puts a temporary edge on any slashing or piercing weapon for a limited number of swings, then the blade visibly dulls and the stone wears out. Some syndicate weapons are biocoded, so a crewmember who loots an energy sword off a dead traitor cannot swing it at all.

- **Take** — Rechargeable pocket shields, sharpening and biocoded antag weapons give melee fights a defensive and upkeep layer instead of pure damage trading, and most of it is item prototypes rather than new mechanics.
- **Skip** — It is a grab bag, not a coherent system: 155 files where individual pieces quietly reach into the fork's other content (the abductor silencer needs the abductor tag and mute toxin, the double energy sword doubles as a Sunrise surgery scalpel, the sharpening component lives in their blood cult namespace), and nothing reaches players until you also edit upstream uplink, vendor and loadout catalogs to stock it.
- **Touches** — security, antagonist, all-players, cargo, salvage
- **Needs** — [abductors](antag.md#abductors), [surgery-and-limb-wounds](medical.md#surgery-and-limb-wounds)
- **Verified** — partly supported; assessed as needs upstream edits, corrected by the verifier. **serbia-strong**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 5 forks carry it*

Shields stop being passive luck and become something you actively hold up: a dedicated keybind raises the shield, an "Active Blocking" indicator appears on your HUD, your character visibly changes stance, and your chance to stop incoming melee and gunfire jumps (a riot shield goes from roughly a coin-flip to blocking about three attacks in four), at the cost of not being able to throw anything while braced. Shields now have their own condition you can examine and they wear down under fire, eventually shattering into steel, glass, planks or webbing depending on what they were made of, and there is a full ladder of them: cardboard, wooden buckler, makeshift steel, web, riot, laser-dissipating, heavy ballistic (which slows you down), collapsible telescopic, mirror, clockwork, and toggleable energy shields including a syndicate red variant. Armour and shields also work by threshold rather than percentage, so a weak hit bounces off entirely while a heavy one punches through, holding a raised shield resists the instant stun from batons, and some armour converts part of a stunbaton's stamina hit into shock damage instead of dropping you outright.

- **Take** — It turns melee and close-range fights from a stat-check into an active exchange, and specifically defangs the stunbaton-into-cuffs instant-win that dominates security versus antagonist encounters.
- **Skip** — It replaces the whole armour and stun pipeline, not just shields: threshold armour, stamina conversion and instastun resistance rebalance every existing weapon, armour set and antagonist kit at once, and it needs a new keybind wired into upstream input handling, so you inherit a permanent balance-tuning job.
- **Touches** — security, antagonist, all-players, cargo
- **Verified** — supported. I found direct edits to three genuinely-upstream, non-SS220 files: Content.Shared/Input/ContentKeyFunctions.cs (a vanilla file listing dozens of engine key functions) has 'ToggleActiveBlocking' spliced in at line 11 among stock entries like Drop/ExamineEntity/FocusChat; Resources/keybinds.yml has a block literally fenced '#SS220 shield rework begin' binding ToggleActiveBlocking to key B; and Content.Client/Input/ContentContexts.cs adds 'human.AddFunction(ContentKeyFunctions.ToggleActiveBlocking); //SS220 shield rework'. That settles needs-upstream-edits as fork-patches-upstream, not just integration-would-require-upstream-edits. The gameplay claim itself checks out in fine detail: shields.yml shows 11 named shield prototypes (Riot, RiotLaser, RiotBullet, WoodenBuckler, Card/cardboard, Makeshift/steel, Web, Clockwork, Mirror, Energy, Telescopic) each with AltArmor tresholdDict/durabilityTresholdDict (threshold not percentage) and Destructible behaviors spawning SheetSteel/SheetGlass, MaterialWoodPlank, or MaterialWebSilk on break; RiotBulletShieldAlt has ClothingSpeedModifier/HeldSpeedModifier 0.9 (the slow-you-down heavy ballistic shield); EnergyShieldAlt is ItemToggle-based and syndi.yml adds a red-lit EnergyShieldSyndi variant; BaseShieldAlt carries InstastunResist/InstastunResistOnActiveBlocking (baton-stun resistance while blocking); StaminaConvertArmorComponent.cs literally converts a DamageCoefficient of stamina damage into a configured DamageType (e.g. Shock); blocking.yml is the 'Active Blocking' HUD alert; and SharedAltBlockingSystem.User.cs wires the toggle to ContentKeyFunctions.ToggleActiveBlocking and cancels ThrowAttemptEvent while blocking (can't throw while braced). Only minor imprecisions found, noted in overclaims.
  - unsupported: "your chance to stop incoming melee and gunfire jumps (a riot shield goes from roughly a coin-flip to blocking about three attacks in four)" blends two different stats: RiotShieldAlt's actual AltBlocking values are rangeBlockProb 0.50 -> activeRangeBlockProb 0.60 (ranged/gunfire), and meleeBlockProb 0.65 -> activeMeleeBlockProb 0.75 (melee). No single stat goes from 0.50 to 0.75; the claim cherry-picks the passive number from one axis (ranged) and the active number from the other (melee) to build a punchier 'coin-flip to 3-in-4' line.
  - unsupported: "there is a full ladder of them" implies an exhaustive list, but the fork also ships a Dagon's Scale cult shield (Resources/Prototypes/SS220/Entities/Objects/Shields/dagots_scale.yml, parent BaseShieldAlt) that isn't in the enumerated ladder.

### Explosion rework and effects

<a id="explosion-rework"></a>

Changes how explosions propagate and how they read on screen: directional obstruction-aware blasts, shockwave overlays, explosion effect entities and shrapnel projectiles.

**stellar-station**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 9 forks carry it*

Explosions hit harder to look at and to stand near. A blast throws a red-orange flash and a rippling shockwave that visibly warps the screen, shakes your camera (harder the closer you are), and plays a different, muffled boom for people far enough away to only hear it. Small blasts no longer just chew tiles: they knock you off your feet, stun you, and fling you away from the centre, but only if you actually had line of sight to it, so ducking behind a wall or a locker genuinely protects you. Some bombs also spray shrapnel outward in all directions as real flying projectiles, and different explosive types (standard, fire, demolition, EMP, and several unique device blasts) each get their own coloured flash, light, and shockwave feel.

- **Take** — Cover suddenly matters in a firefight: obstruction-checked knockback, knockdown and stun turn grenades from a damage number into a positional threat, and the shockwave/screenshake presentation makes every explosion read instantly.
- **Skip** — It is a second explosion system living alongside upstream's, so every grenade, bomb and explosive device you want it on needs its prototype rewired, and the screenshake half pulls in a separate camera-effects system vendored from another fork.
- **Touches** — security, antagonist, engineering, salvage, all-players
- **Needs** — [visual-feedback-effects](ui.md#visual-feedback-effects)
- **Verified** — partly supported. The fork's own explosion-rework files (explosions.yml, StellarExplosiveComponent.cs, SharedStellarExplosionSystem.cs) are self-contained under _ST/Content.Stellar.Shared and do not edit any upstream/vanilla prototype file in place — grenades.yml and Bombs/*.yml still carry only the plain vanilla Explosive component, unmodified. But SharedStellarExplosionSystem.cs has a real compile-time dependency on Content.Shared/_ES/Camera/ESScreenshakeSystem.cs, a separately-authored (SPDX 'Mirrorcult', MIT-licensed) subsystem outside the _ST namespace that the evidence_cited list omits entirely, confirming the claim's own depends_on: visual-feedback-effects. Giving the new system the game-wide reach the gameplay text implies would require editing a target codebase's own upstream grenade/bomb prototypes, since as-is only a couple of fork-specific ForkFiltered antagonist grenades use it.
  - unsupported: "only if you actually had line of sight to it, so ducking behind a wall or a locker genuinely protects you" — in SharedStellarExplosionSystem.cs the obstruction check (_interaction.InRangeUnobstructed) gates ONLY the physical knockback throw. Knockdown and Stun are applied in a separate loop over all entities in range with no obstruction check whatsoever (only a whitelist filter), so cover does not protect you from being knocked down or stunned, only (sometimes) from being physically flung. One shipped prototype (StellarGrenadeGorlexC4) even sets `ignoreObstruction: true`, explicitly disabling the one check that does exist.
  - unsupported: "Small blasts no longer just chew tiles: they knock you off your feet, stun you, and fling you away" reads as a description of explosions in general, but as actually wired in the repo the StellarExplosive/StellarShockwave system is applied only to a handful of new fork-specific antagonist grenades (StellarGrenadeCybersunEmp/Flash, StellarGrenadeGorlexExplosive/C4 — all tagged `categories: [ForkFiltered]`). The vanilla frag grenade, flashbang, minibomb and all the Bombs/*.yml prototypes (Resources/Prototypes/Entities/Objects/Weapons/Throwable/grenades.yml, .../Bombs/*.yml) are untouched and still use only the plain upstream `Explosive` component with no knockback/knockdown/stun/shrapnel/shockwave at all.

**rmc14**

*loop-change · entangled · medium confidence · 9 forks carry it*

Explosions stop being a flat circle of damage and become a thing you read and react to: a nearby blast punches a visible shockwave ripple across your screen, throws shrapnel streaks outward, and burns scorch marks into the floor around it (darker at the centre, lighter at the edges, and moppable afterwards). A blast happening far away plays a distant rumble instead of the same close-up bang, so you can tell roughly how far off the boom was, and heavy ordnance like mortar and orbital-strike blasts is tuned to chew through walls rather than just hurt people. On the throwing end, priming a grenade makes your character shout a warning that everyone nearby sees and hears, and grenades gain new ways to go off: on landing after a throw, after flying a set distance, when the round is shot out of a launcher, or on a sword impact. Cluster and shrapnel grenades scatter their payload in a configurable arc (including backwards when they bounce off something). Separately you can plant a claymore mine, which takes a few seconds to place and arm, ignores your own side, blows up if someone shoots it twice, and can be carefully disarmed with a pulsing tool. Some explosives implode instead, dragging everything nearby into the centre before they go off.

- **Take** — It makes every explosion in the game legible: screen shockwave, shrapnel, floor scorching and near-vs-far blast audio give players real feedback about where a bomb went off and how close it was, and the trigger variants (throw-end, distance, shot-to-arm, claymores) turn explosives from one-shot timers into placeable, plannable tools.
- **Skip** — It is built for a Colonial Marines game, not a station one: claymores and cluster payloads key off RMC-14's own IFF faction system, the explosion presets are tuned around mortars and orbital bombardment, and the trigger layer hooks upstream TriggerEvent and extends the upstream ProjectileGrenadeComponent as a partial, so you cannot take the pretty half (shockwave, shrapnel, scorch, far-sound) without either untangling it from the marine content or accepting the whole 150-file block and its upstream-touching edges.
- **Touches** — security, antagonist, all-players, cargo

### Ship-to-ship combat

<a id="ship-to-ship-combat"></a>

Vessel-scale warfare: crew-served ship guns wired to fire-control and gunnery consoles, guided missiles, radar contacts, armour plating, cloaking and thermal detection.

**monolith**

*structural · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 3 forks carry it*

Ships fight other ships. A gunner sits at a gunnery control console wired to a gunnery server, selects which of the ship's turrets are live, and fires by aiming the cursor on the console map, so a turret only shoots when the target is inside its arc and someone has to fly the ship to bring guns to bear while someone else pulls the trigger. Three weapon families exist: ballistic autocannons and railguns that burn cartridges, energy guns with effectively unlimited shots, and guided missiles that lock onto any grid carrying a shuttle console and can reach well past what the turrets themselves can see. Ballistic and missile mounts need ammunition hauled to them, a pneumatic ammo loader can be multitool-linked to up to eight guns to feed them, and loose ammo is volatile if it gets hit. Meanwhile your ship is broadcasting: firing guns, running thrusters, powering generators and charging an FTL drive all raise a thermal signature that decides how far off enemies see you on radar, while a cloaking device hides you until its heat gauge climbs from Low to Critical and trips an overheat lockout with a visible countdown. Enemy vessels can also be crewed by NPC ship AI that steers and shoots on its own. Bundled in the same unit is a personal armour-plate system: carriers take insertable plates with their own durability that shatter when spent, absorb or amplify specific damage types, convert part of what they soak into stamina damage, and slow your walk or sprint by weight.

- **Take** — It is a complete, documented, player-facing vessel-combat package: gunnery consoles and servers, three weapon families, guided missiles, ammo loaders, radar/thermal detection, cloaking with heat management and NPC ship AI, all shipped with an in-game guidebook already written for players.
