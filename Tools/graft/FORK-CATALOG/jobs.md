### Extra jobs and roles

<a id="extra-job-roster"></a>

Additional crew job entries with their own gear, icons, access and playtime trackers, from senior variants to wildcard and CentComm roles.

**rmc14**

*structural · entangled · high confidence · 7 forks carry it*

Instead of a station crew list, you pick from a marine unit's roster: commanding officer, executive and staff officers, senior enlisted advisor, intelligence officer, dropship and gunship pilots, crew chiefs and support crew, each slotted into a military chain of command with a stated supervisor and an authority level that decides who can lawfully give you orders. Your character joins with a rank tied to how many hours you have logged in that role (a dropship pilot starts as a warrant officer and works up to second lieutenant), wears a rank prefix in front of their name, spawns in role-specific uniform and kit, and gets a point budget to spend at the unit's supply vendors. Senior roles unlock only after long playtime across other departments, and command roles announce themselves to the whole crew on arrival, hand out commendations, see intel objectives, and show up as their own labelled blip on the tactical map.

- **Take** — It is a fully worked example of a rank-and-chain-of-command roster: playtime gates that ladder across departments, in-name rank prefixes, per-role authority levels and supervisors, and role-specific spawn kits, all expressed in readable per-job files.
- **Skip** — These jobs are not crew jobs at all, they are USCM marine roles that only make sense on RMC-14's dropship-and-planet round; every single one leans on that fork's skills, ranks, squads, marine vendors and tactical map, so grafting one job means grafting several large systems and then rewriting the job itself to mean anything on a space station.
- **Touches** — command, security, cargo, engineering, medical, all-players
- **Needs** — [squads-ranks-and-skills](#squads-ranks-and-skills), [vending-and-loot-catalogs](cargo.md#vending-and-loot-catalogs), [access-control-rework](machines.md#access-control-rework)
- **Verified** — supported. Fetched all 5 cited files plus 4 more (gunship_pilot.yml, dropship_crew_chief.yml, staff_officer.yml, executive_officer.yml, all HTTP 200) to check the roster claim. Every specific mechanic the claim describes is directly present in the YAML: JobPrefix (rank prefix), ranks: blocks with RoleTimeRequirement hour thresholds (dropship pilot literally starts at RMCRankWarrantOfficer: [] and ladders to RMCRankChiefWarrantOfficer at 25h then RMCRankSecondLT at 70h, matching 'starts as warrant officer, works up to second lieutenant' exactly), CMVendorUser with a points value (supply-vendor budget), multi-department DepartmentTimeRequirement/TotalJobsTimeRequirement gating for senior roles (commanding_officer.yml spans CMSquad/CMMilitaryPolice/CMMedical/CMCommand/CMRequisitions/CMEngineering), joinNotifyCrew: true plus CommendationGiver/RMCAwardRecommendation on command roles, ViewIntelObjectives, TacticalMapIcon per-job blips, marineAuthorityLevel and supervisors fields (chain of command). The claim's downgrade from the seed's 'drop-in' to 'entangled' is itself well-founded: the same files show heavy component-level coupling to other fork subsystems (Skills/ranks, CMVendorUser vending, AssignSquad/SquadArmorWearer squads, accessGroups access control) that the depends_on list names correctly, even though those dependent files may live inside the same _RMC14 namespace (so the narrow 'zero files outside namespace' path signal was technically true but misleading about actual graftability).

**starlight**

*activity · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 7 forks carry it*

Adds a long list of extra jobs you can pick at the lobby screen, each with its own title, outfit, ID access and a one-line "here is your job" blurb. Station-side additions include a Magistrate and Internal Affairs Agent who put crew on trial, a Blueshield Officer who bodyguards the heads instead of doing normal security work, a Duty Officer who runs the prison wing under the Warden, a Mail Technician who delivers packages for cargo cash, a Mining Specialist who works the salvage magnet without leaving the station, a Salvage Lead who runs the salvage team, a Surgeon, Roboticist, Zookeeper, Performer and Boxer. On top of that sit CentComm and military roles (NanoTrasen Representative, Greenshield and Navy Corps bodyguards, NTSF and Decimus operatives, CentComm chef/bartender/janitor/service worker), most of which are locked out of the normal roster and spawned in by admins, arrive in heavy gear with mindshield implants, and are told in their briefing that they serve NanoTrasen or Central Command rather than the crew. Many roles are gated behind playtime in a related job or department, so the lobby screen tells you how many hours you still need before you can pick one.

- **Take** — It is the cheapest way to add roster variety: each job is a self-contained YAML file with gear, access and playtime gates, and you can cherry-pick just the two or three roles you want without taking the rest.
- **Skip** — The roles only exist if the station map has spawn points and job slots for them, so every map you run needs editing, and the CentComm/military half drags in a large pile of custom uniforms, hardsuits, PDAs, weapons and job icons that do not come from upstream.
- **Touches** — cargo, security, command, service, medical, science, ghost-roles, all-players
- **Needs** — [clothing-and-wardrobe-pack](art.md#clothing-and-wardrobe-pack), [department-restructure](#department-restructure)
- **Verified** — partly supported. All nine cited files check out and the specific gameplay claims (Blueshield 'not a security officer', Duty Officer under the Warden, Mining Specialist staying station-side, playtime-gated requirements with hour displays, cc-aligned briefing text 'you do NOT serve the crew') are near-verbatim matches to the fetched .ftl/.yml content. Critically, I went beyond the cited evidence and fetched Resources/Prototypes/Roles/Jobs/departments.yml (NOT under _Starlight/) — it is directly edited in place with new job IDs (MailTech, MiningSpecialist, Performer, Zookeeper, CentralCommandGreenShield, Magistrate, NanoTrasenRepresentative, Surgeon, DutyOfficer, Roboticist) each tagged with comments like '# Starlight Addition' / '# Starlight-edit'. This proves the unit genuinely touches a shared upstream-path file to become playable, confirming the claim's downgrade from the seed's 'drop-in' to 'needs-upstream-edits' — and if anything the claim understates it, since the fork itself (not just a future integration) already patches that file.
  - unsupported: "most of which are locked out of the normal roster and spawned in by admins... arrive in heavy gear with mindshield implants" generalizes across the whole CentComm/military role list, but only one of those job files (greenshield.yml) was actually fetched and confirmed (setPreference: false, AddImplantSpecial MindShieldImplant, SMG/energy-magnum gear). ### Character loadouts

<a id="loadout-system"></a>

Per-job character loadout groups letting players pick starting clothing and items at setup, with item groups capping how many picks come from each pool.

**frontier**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 9 forks carry it*

In character setup, each job gets its own slot-by-slot wardrobe menu: jumpsuit or jumpskirt, hat, coat, badge, gloves, shoes, mask, glasses, backpack, belt, plus functional picks like PDA cartridges, encryption keys, a survival box, a wallet, implanters, in-hand items and even an emotional support pet. Picks are drawn against a spending budget shown as "Total Loadout Cost" and "Available Balance", so a player trades a fancy coat against a useful gadget rather than taking everything. The menu quietly hides options your character cannot use ("That item is not permitted for your role", "not permitted for your sex"), and species-aware filtering means a Diona is never offered shoes and a Vox is never offered the wrong breathing gear, while mandatory items like your job PDA and ID badge are auto-assigned without cluttering the list.

- **Take** — It turns the loadout screen from cosmetic dress-up into a real pre-round choice: a budget plus role/species/sex filters means players pick starting kit they will actually use, and every job can be given a curated wardrobe without handing out free gear.
- **Skip** — Nearly all 192 prototype files are wardrobes for Frontier-only jobs (Bailiff, Brigmedic, Contractor, Mercenary, Pilot, Mail Carrier, Traffic Controller, Valet, Sheriff) wearing Frontier-only clothing, so what actually ports is the effects-and-price framework, and you then write every job's wardrobe yourself and re-curate it each time a job or an item set changes.
- **Touches** — all-players, security, medical, service, command, cargo
- **Needs** — [extra-job-roster](#extra-job-roster), [clothing-and-wardrobe-pack](art.md#clothing-and-wardrobe-pack), [extra-playable-species](species.md#extra-playable-species)
- **Verified** — supported. The gameplay description is well supported: loadouts.ftl confirms the "Total Loadout Cost"/"Available Balance" budget strings, loadout-effects.ftl confirms the exact role/sex rejection messages quoted, loadout-groups.ftl lists the same slot vocabulary (backpack items, hand items, encryption keys, survival box, cartridges, wallet, implanters, emotional-support animals), loadout_effects.yml shows real species-gated groups (ShoesCapableNF excludes Diona, NFNitrogenBreather/NFOxygenBreather gate Vox/SlimePerson), and bailiff_loadout_groups.yml shows a PDA group marked hidden with "gets auto-selected" — matching the auto-assigned-badge/PDA claim. But the graft_shape claim is more right than the assessor even argued: fetching Content.Shared/Clothing/LoadoutSystem.cs and Content.Shared/Preferences/Loadouts/RoleLoadout.cs (both outside any _NF directory, i.e. shared/upstream namespace) shows explicit "// Frontier: ..." / "// End Frontier" edit markers wrapping extra fields, hidden-loadout/fallback logic, and subgroup checks — meaning Frontier's own repo copy patches the core loadout engine files in place, not just adds _NF prototype data on top of an untouched upstream system. None of these non-_NF core files were in the cited evidence list, so the claim under-cited the strongest reason this is needs-upstream-edits (it undersold its own case) while still landing on the correct verdict via a different, weaker argument (mostly-Frontier-only wardrobe prototypes).
  - unsupported: Evidence citation issue (not a gameplay overclaim): Resources/Locale/en-US/_NF/preferences/ui/humanoid-profile-editor.ftl was cited as evidence but its full content is only 'humanoid-profile-editor-preference-messenger = Messenger' and 'trait-category-height = Body' — it contains nothing about loadouts and does not back any claim in the text.

**serbia-strong**

*cosmetic · entangled · high confidence · 9 forks carry it*

At character setup each job gets its own list of wardrobe slots to fill in: a captain picks between two headset styles and white or black gloves, a salvager chooses which hard hat and jumpsuit to spawn in, a clown picks a classic suit, a Pennywise outfit or a gold one. Some of the fancier options stay greyed out until you have logged enough hours in that specific role (30, 50, 70 hours for salvage variants; 5 or 50 hours for clown outfits), and a large slice of them are reserved for donators, so the setup screen doubles as a visible progression ladder. Separately, every character spawns with cash tucked in their bag scaled to how senior the job is, roughly 250 spesos for a civilian job, 500 for a technical one and 700 for command, so people have spending money for vendors from the first minute of the round.

- **Take** — It is a huge, ready-made per-job wardrobe catalogue that makes the character setup screen feel personal without touching a single round mechanic, and the playtime-gated variants give returning players something to unlock.
- **Skip** — Almost every entry hangs a sponsor-tier effect off itself and points at fork-only clothing entities, so grafting it means importing SS220's donator system and its whole sprite wardrobe first; the loadout files also have to be wired into upstream job prototypes, and the SS220 files carry a CLA with a hosting restriction rather than a plain permissive licence.
- **Touches** — all-players, command, security, cargo, service, medical, engineering
- **Needs** — [sponsor-and-donator-perks](misc.md#sponsor-and-donator-perks), [clothing-and-wardrobe-pack](art.md#clothing-and-wardrobe-pack)
- **Verified** — supported. Every substantive detail checks out against the raw files: clown.yml has RoleTimeRequirement gates at exactly 18000s (5h) and 180000s (50h) for the Pennywise/Classic clown variants; salvage_specialist.yml has RoleTimeRequirement gates at 108000/180000/252000 (30/50/70h) exactly as claimed; cash_loadouts.yml defines SpaceCash250/500/700 tied to CivilianCash/WorkerCash/SeniorStaffCash loadout groups matching the civilian/technical/command tiers described; loadout_groups.yml shows CaptainGloves (CaptainGloves + CaptainWhiteGloves) and CAPHeadset (CaptainHeadset + CaptainHeadsetAlt), matching "two headset styles and white or black gloves"; the touches list (command, security, cargo, service, medical, engineering) all appear in loadout_groups.yml. The CLA.txt I fetched directly confirms "hosting restriction" language (prohibits commercial use and public hosting without SS220's permission) so that specific claim is accurate, not embellishment. On graft_shape: the seed's "drop-in" was a raw path-containment signal, and the assessor correctly downgraded it. Sponsor-tier group definitions (BigShlopaSponsorTier, GoldenShlopaSponsorTier, SeniorDevTeamTier, etc.) do live in a sibling file in the same Loadouts folder (sponsor_tier_requirements.yml), so namespace containment holds narrowly, but nearly every job file (clown, mime, lawyer, salvage) hangs multiple loadout entries off these sponsor-tier requirements and off entity IDs (ClothingSponsorGoldClownSuit, ClothingUniformJumpsuitMoryak, etc.) that are not among the cited evidence and are almost certainly defined in a separate SS220 clothing/entities tree, plus the underlying SponsorTierRequirementLoadoutEffect mechanism implies a live donator/account-status backend, not just static YAML. ### Silicon lawsets and AI appearance

<a id="ai-lawsets-and-appearance"></a>

Extra silicon lawsets, per-job or per-module law assignment, physical lawboard swapping and selectable AI hologram and core-screen appearances.

**cosmatic-drift**

*cosmetic · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 5 forks carry it*

The station AI gets a much bigger wardrobe: 10 extra hologram bodies it can project when it talks to the crew (an alien, a swarm of bees, an oversized moth, a ghost, a shapeless horror, drifting leaves, Nar'Sie, Ratvar, and a couple of abstract forms) and 27 extra faces for the display on its core and its cameras (thinking, database, firewall, matrix, rainbow, monochrome, static, a cat, the Nanotrasen logo, a "not malf" smiley, a presidential face, an evil red dot, and so on). Each core face has a matching cracked/dead version, so when the AI is destroyed the crew sees its chosen face go dark rather than a generic broken screen. It is purely a look: the AI still picks its appearance the same way it already does, there are just far more options in the list.

**goobstation**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 5 forks carry it*

Adds a programmable law board: you click it open, type your own laws into a list (up to 15 laws, 512 characters each), then slot it into the AI upload console to overwrite the station AI's or a borg's entire lawset with text you wrote yourself. There are two versions: a freeform NanoTrasen board that command can buy and fill with anything, and a Syndicate "experimental" board sold in the traitor uplink that comes pre-loaded with Antimov and plays an ominous upload jingle. It also adds joke and role-flavoured lawsets to the pool, such as Helpimov (the first person who talks to the AI becomes "the courier" and the AI must agree with and obey them) and Gambamov (the AI must gamble constantly, protect its gambling, and insist it is not an addiction), plus short "you must heal the crew" / "repair the station" / "clean the station" / "mine materials" style law lines for department-themed lawsets.

- **Take** — A freeform law board turns the AI from a fixed-lawset NPC-ish role into whatever the crew (or a traitor) writes that shift, which is the single biggest lever on how an AI round plays, and the joke lawsets give the same payoff cheaply.
- **Skip** — Player-authored law text goes straight into everyone's chat and law screen with only newline stripping and a length clamp, so it is an unmoderated free-text broadcast channel your admins now have to police; the "AI appearance" half of the catalogue feature is not in this fork's files at all.
- **Touches** — silicon, command, antagonist, science, all-players
- **Verified** — partly supported; assessed as drop-in, corrected by the verifier. The custom-lawboard upload path is a Goob edit inside the upstream file Content.Server/Silicons/Laws/SiliconLawSystem.cs (OnUpdaterInsert branches on CustomLawboardComponent), and the Helpimov/Gambamov pool weights live in the upstream-path Resources/Prototypes/silicon-laws.yml, so the fork's own copy patches upstream files and a graft would need equivalent edits; without them the board uploads its dummy Crewsimov lawset and the joke lawsets never enter the random pool.
  - unsupported: "a freeform NanoTrasen board that command can buy" — NTCustomLawboard is referenced in no cargo product, store, or catalog file anywhere in the repo (code search hits only its own prototype file); it has a StaticPrice of 5000 and BaseCommandContraband parentage, which makes it legal for command to possess, but no purchase route is shown
  - unsupported: "comes pre-loaded with Antimov and plays an ominous upload jingle" (attributed to the Syndicate board) — the upload sound (/Audio/Ambience/Antag/emagged_borg.ogg) is set on the shared abstract BaseCustomLawboard, so the NT freeform board plays the same jingle; it is not a Syndicate-board feature

### Latejoin spawn flow and cryosleep

<a id="latejoin-spawn-and-cryo"></a>

Reworks how players enter and leave a round in progress: spawn region selection, arrivals markers, personal lockers, cryosleep pods and lost-and-found for stored gear. When a fork ships cryosleep inside a larger medical rework, only the cryosleep and arrivals subtree lands here; the wounds, surgery, cloning and life-support subtrees take their own medical ids.

**ephemeral-space**

*structural · entangled · medium confidence · 1 forks carry it*

Instead of the usual latejoin list, joining players get a "Spawning" window that groups jobs by department, shows how many slots are still open (or infinity for uncapped ones), and lets you pick which station or ship you want to arrive on before riding in on an arrivals shuttle. Once you arrive, a locker in your department already has your name printed on it and only opens for your ID. If you leave the round, your body freezes into a nameless, sluggish "Cryohusk" with a frozen ID card that other players can find and loot, and dying puts you on a countdown ("Respawn available in N seconds") before you can come back as someone new. The same package scatters the station's loot differently every round: armory, cargo and tech caches hand out a randomised split of guns, armour and gear across marked rooms, and maintenance fills with random crates, closets, posters, magazines and trash.

- **Take** — It replaces the whole "how do I get into and out of a round" flow at once — slot-aware job picking across multiple stations, a body left behind when you leave, timed respawns, and per-round loot scatter that stops veterans from memorising where every gun lives.
- **Skip** — It is not a feature you can drop next to your existing latejoin — it assumes multi-station/ship spawning, its own item and loot-table catalogue, and hand-placed region and spawner markers on every map, so adopting it means re-mapping your stations and replacing upstream arrivals rather than adding to it.
- **Touches** — all-players, security, cargo, science, command, ghost-roles, admin

**frontier**

*structural · entangled · high confidence · 1 forks carry it*

Joining late opens a picker that first asks whether you want a job on a station or a berth on another player's ship, then lists the specific ships and their open slots. Taking a crew slot drops you in as a translucent "Applicant" hologram: you and the ship's deed-holder negotiate terms in character, both press Approve, and only then do you spawn as your real character (anyone can dismiss the hologram back to the lobby, optionally closing the slot). Later in the same shift you can request to wake up and return to your body at any free cryopod, with the explicit rule that you remember nothing that happened while you slept.

- **Take** — It turns joining and leaving a shift into an in-character transaction, hiring interviews on the way in and a checked, reversible cryo exit on the way out, instead of a menu click and a vanished body.
- **Skip** — The whole flow is built around player-owned ships: the picker's second tab is "which ship's crew", Approve requires holding a shuttle deed, and the cryo warnings are largely about deeds and unsold ships, so on a single-station fork most of it has nothing to point at.

**serbia-strong**

### Xenobiology and slime rework

<a id="xenobiology-rework"></a>

Rebuilds the slime husbandry loop with new slime types, processing equipment, extract crafting and a dedicated console.

**goobstation, starlight**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 0 forks carry it*

Xenobiology becomes a full slime-ranching loop: you breed slimes through roughly twenty-two colour breeds (grey, metal, bluespace, sepia, gold, adamantine and so on), each splitting into new colours on mitosis with a mutation chance you can push up with destabilizer jelly or clamp down with stabilizer. A handheld scanner tells you a slime's breed, its mutation odds, which colours it can become and how many extracts it would yield right now, and a backpack-sized "xenovac" containment tank lets you suck up slimes and carry them around instead of herding them by hand. Feeding slimes with steroid jelly raises extract yield, boost jelly raises litter size, and a docility jelly tames them into pets. Extracts are then activated by squirting reagents on them: grey extract reacts with water to make epinephrine, with blood to spit out three monkey cubes, and with plasma to burst into three baby slimes, and each extract goes dry and useless after one reaction. Goob adds a xenobiology bounty console that pays out for specific extracts or "any three tier-2 extracts", so slime farming feeds cargo; the jellies also let anyone drinking them turn fireproof, swap species into a slimeperson or felinid, or change gender. Loose slimes are genuinely dangerous now: they latch onto a living crewmember, pump them full of sedative hallucinogenic slime toxin that holds them alive in crit at zero blood, and can tear organs and limbs off corpses. Starlight's version covers the same ground more compactly, with a remote xenobiology console you drive from safety (pick up and place slimes, drop monkeys from stored cubes, recycle dead monkeys back into cubes, apply mutation and stabilizer potions at range), a slime processor you activate to grind slimes into extracts, and a wide potion shelf including sentience, mind transference, speed, bluespace radio, name change and gender change.

- **Take** — It turns xenobiology from a thin upstream stub into a real department loop with its own progression, its own equipment, its own payout path through bounties, and a genuine failure mode when the slimes get out.
- **Skip** — This is two large parallel implementations (161 and 49 files) that overlap heavily but are not interchangeable, so you must pick one and then own a breeding/mutation table, roughly twenty-two extract prototypes with per-reagent reaction lists, custom HTN slime AI, and a bespoke bounty database forever — and the species-swap and sentience potions leak balance decisions into parts of the game far outside science.
- **Touches** — science, cargo, all-players, ghost-roles
- **Verified** — supported.
