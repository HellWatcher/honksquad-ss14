### Reagent and reaction expansion

<a id="reagent-and-reaction-expansion"></a>

Adds reagents, reactions, flavour profiles and glassware on top of the base chemistry set, covering medicines, toxins, narcotics and novelty chemicals.

**rmc14**

*loop-change · entangled · medium confidence · 13 forks carry it*

Replaces the standard medicine shelf with a Colonial-Marines-style drug set: bicaridine for brute, kelotane and dermaline for burns, dexalin and dexalin plus for suffocation, dylovene for toxins, tricordrazine as the weak all-rounder, plus specialty picks like inaprovaline, epinephrine, leporazine and cryoxadone. Every one of them has an overdose and a critical-overdose line, so a medic who doubles up on a screaming patient starts doing brute damage instead of healing, and dosing becomes a judgement call rather than "empty the syringe". Alongside the medicines it adds recreational narcotics that make you laugh, see rainbows, jitter and eventually black out, and a bench of incendiary ordnance chemistry (sticky napalm, high-combustion napalm, B-gel, CLF3, sulphuric acid) that a technician mixes into flamethrower and incinerator fuel of varying nastiness. An in-game guidebook lists every reagent by group so players can look up exactly what a chemical does before drinking it.

- **Take** — The overdose / critical-overdose tiering turns healing into a dosing decision with a real downside, which gives medics an actual skill ceiling instead of a syringe-emptying reflex, and it comes with player-readable guidebook entries generated from the effects themselves.
- **Skip** — It is a replacement set, not an addition — the CM-named drugs cover the same ground as upstream's medicines, so you either displace the existing chemistry (rewiring every medkit, vendor, dispenser and chem recipe that references the old reagents) or ship two parallel medicine sets that confuse everyone; the incendiary half is also only meaningful if your fork already has flamethrowers and spreading fire to burn things with.
- **Touches** — medical, science, cargo, security, all-players
- **Needs** — [spreading-fire-system](atmos.md#spreading-fire-system)
- **Verified** — partly supported; assessed as needs upstream edits, corrected by the verifier. All cited evidence files do live entirely under _RMC14 namespace directories (Resources/Prototypes/_RMC14/Reagents/, Resources/ServerInfo/Guidebook/_RMC14/, Content.Shared/_RMC14/Chemistry/), and the new reagents merely inherit vanilla reagent IDs (Bicaridine, Dexalin, etc.) via YAML `parent:` — so it does not literally patch upstream files, contradicting a plain 'fork-patches-upstream' reading. But reading the actual effect classes shows the reagent system is built on RMC14's own parallel damage/health/status/zombie infrastructure (SharedRMCDamageableSystem, SharedRMCBloodstreamSystem, a bespoke 'Genetic' damage group, RMCUnconsciousComponent, CureZombieInfection) rather than vanilla SS14 mechanics, so a graft needs to bring that whole substrate along, not just point vendors/medkits at new reagent IDs as the claim's 'skip_because' implies. That is a deeper coupling than 'needs-upstream-edits' captures.
  - unsupported: "Every one of them has an overdose and a critical-overdose line" — false per the cited medicine.yml itself: CMCryoxadone, CMClonexadone, RMCMethylphenidate, RMCCitalopram, and RMCAntiZed have no overdose/criticalOverdose fields at all.
  - unsupported: "a medic who doubles up on a screaming patient starts doing brute damage instead of healing" — verified against RMCChemicalEffect.cs (base TickOverdose/TickCriticalOverdose are no-op virtuals) plus Antitoxic.cs and Neogenetic.cs: overdose consequences vary per effect type. Bicaridine's Neogenetic effect deals BURN damage on overdose (not brute); Dylovene's Antitoxic overdose tier is a literal commented-out TODO no-op; only the Biocidic effect (used by Arithrazine/RussianRed specifically) deals brute damage. There is no universal 'brute damage instead of healing' consequence.
  - unsupported: depends_on lists only 'spreading-fire-system', but the reagent effect implementations (Antitoxic.cs, Neogenetic.cs, RMCAntiZed's CureZombieInfection) show real dependencies on RMC14-only shared systems: SharedRMCDamageableSystem.DistributeHealingCached, SharedRMCBloodstreamSystem, a custom 'Genetic' damage group, RMCUnconsciousComponent, and RMC14's own zombie-infection cure mechanic — none of which are vanilla SS14 or guaranteed to exist in a target fork.

**goobstation**

*loop-change · entangled · high confidence · 13 forks carry it*

Goob's chemistry additions lean hard into drugs and their downsides. Fentanyl is a full narcotic experience: it kills pain almost completely for half a minute while the victim stutters, jitters, sees rainbows, slows to a crawl and gets whispered lines like "They're under my skin, they're crawling through my bones!" Push past five units and it starts suffocating them and making them claw their own skin open. Amnestizine wipes the mind: slurred dementia speech, staggering movement, and pacification so the drugged person literally cannot remember how to fire a gun or swing a sword, while messages like "You... should listen to your superiors." nudge them along. Blind toxin blacks out vision. On the medical side there is a chain of new drugs rather than one wonder cure: probital heals brute but stacks fatigue until you drop into a forced power nap, mitotrophin heals well but you have to eat it, tirimol fixes suffocation at the cost of speed, and stasizium is a corpse-grade revival chem that slows rot but crushes you if you overdose. A whole shelf of joke toxins turn the drinker into a gondola, a bingle or a sentient carp at high doses, or just make them swear uncontrollably. Formaldehyde lets a coroner embalm a body so a wraith cannot absorb it. Rounding it out: exotic gases as drinkable reagents, botany fertiliser inputs, triple citrus for the bar, a pile of new taste descriptions, and a dispenser that runs off beakers slotted into it rather than a fixed inventory.

- **Take** — The drugs are written as trade-offs rather than stat sticks: every painkiller, heal and high comes with a specific, readable drawback the player feels and can play around.
- **Skip** — Pieces of it are wired to Goob's own content, so fentanyl's pain suppression needs their pain system, the polymorph toxins need their mobs, and formaldehyde's headline use only matters if you also run their wraith.
- **Touches** — medical, science, service, security, antagonist, botany
- **Needs** — [exotic-gas-expansion](atmos.md#exotic-gas-expansion), [medical-status-conditions](medical.md#medical-status-conditions)

**cosmatic-drift**

*activity · drop-in · high confidence · 13 forks carry it*

This fork's chemistry additions are built around psychiatric and pain medicine as roleplay props. Each condition gets a mild, standard and strong drug: antidepressants (Serenitol, Neurozenium, Blissifylovene), anti-anxiety meds (Calmafluxine, Tranquinase, Equilibrazine), painkillers (Stubantazine, Soretizone, Agonolexyne), plus an antipsychotic that quiets hallucinations, an anti-amnesia drug, and a shelved stimulant. While one is in your blood you get occasional private messages telling you how you feel ("The world seems a bit brighter", "You forgot what pain feels like"), and a fade message when it wears off. Take too much and the messages turn on you: paranoid shouting, "Run! RUN! HIDE!", or drowsiness you cannot shake. Drinking on your meds makes your body absorb more of them, so alcohol pushes you into overdose, and stronger antidepressants purge weaker ones out of your bloodstream. Heavy use leaves Addictine behind, which nags you for another fix and neutralises charcoal so you cannot simply flush it out. Separately there is a drug-cooking ladder from Mindleaf sap: brew it with water for a relaxing tea, bake it into an euphoric powder, then acid-refine it up through rust, bake and grit, each step more potent and more toxic, with smuggler packets and wrapped packages to craft for moving the product. Medical also gets forearm crutches and single-use TTE medcases colour-coded by injury (burn, radiation, poison, asphyxiation, brute, bleed) that pop out the right supplies for crew who are not doctors.

- **Take** — It is the rare chemistry expansion aimed at roleplay rather than combat: tiered psych meds with self-narrating flow text, alcohol and cross-medication interactions, withdrawal that resists charcoal, and a full drug-cooking and smuggling ladder from a single plant sap.
- **Skip** — The mental-health drugs only produce text popups and small poison ticks, so their whole value depends on a playerbase that wants to roleplay conditions; on a lower-RP server they are twelve reagents nobody prints, and someone has to maintain the interaction web (each drug's thresholds, ethanol rules and purge rules are hand-tuned per reagent).
- **Touches** — medical, chemistry, antagonist, all-players
- **Verified** — supported. I re-fetched medicine.yml, narcotics.yml, the reactions/crafting files, both .ftl locale files, medcase.yml, crutches.yml and ResetParacusia.cs: every named reagent, the three quoted popup strings, the ethanol/purge/Addictine-vs-Charcoal mechanics, the Mindleaf tea-Breakout-Rust-Bake-Grit ladder with smuggler/wrap constructions, and the six colour-coded SpawnItemsOnUse medcases are all present as written.   - unsupported: "Drinking on your meds makes your body absorb more of them, so alcohol pushes you into overdose" - true for six of the drugs (Neurozenium, Blissifylovene, Tranquinase, Soretizone, Agonolexyne, Cortexene) but not universal: Serenitol is explicitly commented safe with alcohol, Stubantazine responds with vomiting instead of increased absorption, and Calmafluxine has no ethanol rule at all.

**monolith**

*activity · entangled · medium confidence · 13 forks carry it*

Monolith's chemistry additions give the chemist a spread of new bottles with real trade-offs rather than straight upgrades. Celoxradine slowly patches the damage types nothing else covers (shock, radiation, heat, a trickle of brute) but makes you jitter, drunk and eventually vomiting if you keep drinking it. Rhymatine eats cellular damage and even works on the dead, charging cold and shock damage as the price. Peranol is a cooler Leporazine that also mends heat burns, and phenoxzine and meroxzine simply make claws grow faster or stop growing. The centrepiece is letoferol, an illegal Romerol derivative: get ten units into someone and they do not become a shambling zombie, they transform into a chimera. A "natural" strain needs a bigger dose, sprouts fleshy kudzu on the floor where it splashes, and converts animals as well as people, so a doctor's job becomes chasing the cure chain: electrolyse the stuff into protoletoferol, cook that into mesotaxinide for a full revert or the cheaper mesophenerol for the natural strain only. Fentanyl is a strong painkiller in tiny amounts and a slurring, stuttering, rainbow-seeing sleep that suffocates you in larger ones, and mixed 5:1 with tricordrazine and heated it crystallises into a valuable drug you can only batch one at a time because dylovene makes it explode. Bar and kitchen get a wall of tea and coffee powders (chamomile, berry, fruit, Yorkshire, decaf, a Waffle Co. syndicate blend) plus nanites, superconductant and lithium hydroxide as industrial reagents, and pre-labelled bulk jugs like a 200u omnizine jug on the shelf.

**funky**

*loop-change · entangled · medium confidence · 13 forks carry it*

Funky's chemistry drops a full SS13-style medicine ladder into medbay. Instead of one generic burn or brute cure, a chemist mixes tiered drugs: Aiuri and Lenturi for burns (Lenturi dehydrates the patient as it heals), Oxandrolone that barely works on a healthy person but heals hard once someone is badly hurt, Libital and Probital for brute, Multiver and Pentetic Acid for poison, plus Salbutamol, Convermol, Sievertadone, Mutadone and a Pyroxadone that only forms in a hot beaker. Almost all of them punish greed: push a patient past the overdose line and they start jittering, take blunt or caustic damage, scream or vomit while the drug keeps healing. Poisoners get their own shelf, including Heparin that makes wounds bleed hard, Rhigoxane that flash-freezes whoever drinks it, Spewium that induces constant vomiting, and Pestiline as a slow cellular killer. Above all that sits an exotic tier: a multi-step chain through Stasicillin, Revivopine, Negacordrazine and stabilised stimulants that eventually yields Omnizine, Barozine and Ichor, deliberately built to need cooperation from several departments. Odds and ends round it out, machine oil and nanite slurry as bleedable robot fluids (slurry can be centrifuged back into plasma, uranium and silicon), peanut-based napalm, and pre-labelled vials and beakers of the nastier reagents for spawns and loot.

- **Take** — It is a complete, self-consistent medicine economy rather than a handful of extra chems: every drug has a dose ceiling with a real penalty, so chemistry and treatment become a dosing decision instead of "make more healing".
- **Skip** — Grafting it rewires how medbay heals, so it has to be balanced against whatever healing already exists, and the exotic chain reaches for reagents (Razorium, Licoxide, Vestine, Cognizine, Tazinide, Fresium) defined outside the files in this unit, so the top of the ladder will not come across intact on its own.
- **Touches** — medical, science, chemistry, antagonist

### Chemical plumbing network

<a id="chemical-plumbing"></a>

Fluid piping for chemistry: reactors, filters, synthesizers, pill presses and smart dispensers connected into an automated chem network.

**starlight**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 0 forks carry it*

Chemists get a build-out toy: a hand-held Rapid Plumbing Device places hidden floor ducts (on three separate layers, with straight, bend, T, fourway and manifold pieces) and snaps together a chain of fluid machines, so reagents flow between them on their own instead of being ferried by hand in beakers. You pour into an input tank or a sink, then route the fluid through filters that split named reagents onto two outlets, reactors that pull set quantities and heat them until a reaction fires, synthesizers that conjure basic reagents like iron, oxygen and sugar straight out of battery power, buffer and 2000u storage tanks, a smart dispenser that fills a labeled jug automatically, and a pill press that stamps pills or patches at whatever dosage you set (including blending two feeds at a chosen ratio). Floor drains suck up spilled puddles into the network, a disposal unit quietly vaporises whatever you do not want, connectors show red for inlet and blue for outlet and look visibly jagged when you have misaligned a machine, and a plunger flushes anything stuck onto the floor.

- **Take** — It turns chemistry from repetitive beaker-shuffling into a buildable factory the chemist can design, debug and expand over a round, which is the single biggest depth upgrade available to that role and something upstream has no answer for.
- **Skip** — It is a large new node-network subsystem with roughly a dozen machines, six client UIs and its own construction tool, and its own guidebook advertises hooks into upstream ChemMaster, chemical dispenser and reagent grinder, so it will not sit quietly in a fork namespace and will need re-reconciling against upstream chemistry changes forever.
- **Touches** — science, medical, engineering, service
- **Verified** — supported. The two guidebook pages read almost line-for-line match the gameplay claim: RPLD tool building three-layer ducts (straight/bend/T/fourway/manifold), red=inlet/blue=outlet connector coloring with jagged-when-disconnected visuals, plumbing input/sink/drain, 2000u tank and 200u buffer, filter splitting up to 4 named reagents into filtered/passthrough outlets, reactor pulling set quantities and heating to trigger reactions, synthesizer generating iron/oxygen/sugar/etc from battery power, smart dispenser with labeled-jug auto-fill, pill press with configurable dosage and two ratio-mixing inlets for pills/patches, disposal that vaporizes reagents, and a plunger to flush buffers.
