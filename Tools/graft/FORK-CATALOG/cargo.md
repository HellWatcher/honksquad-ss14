### Vending machines and container fills

<a id="vending-and-loot-catalogs"></a>

Fork-specific vending machine rosters with their own inventories and ad barks, plus the crate, locker, box and maintenance loot tables that stock the station.

**ephemeral-space**

*activity · entangled · medium confidence · 11 forks carry it*

Adds a set of station-flavour vending machines with their own product lists and chatty ad barks, including a pride-clothing "Gender Bash" dispenser, a scrap-dealer machine hawking military and engineering surplus scavenged from derelicts, a plain water cooler, and a lumber-and-welding-fuel machine that warns you not to light fires indoors. Their stock is rolled from loot tables at round start rather than being a fixed shelf, so two rounds of the same machine can offer different goods. It also rewrites what you find when you crack open lockers, dressers, wardrobes, backpacks, briefcases and maintenance crates: theatre wardrobes hand out whole themed costumes (jester, chicken suit, cowboy in one of three colours, shrine maiden, Santa), and there are hidden ceiling caches that spawn a matched antagonist kit, for example a silenced pistol with spare magazine, sleepy pens and smoke grenades, or a chameleon backpack with an emag and C4.

- **Take** — Randomised, table-driven vending stock plus themed costume and cache fills is a cheap way to make looting and maintenance scavenging feel different every round, and the machinery for it is a single small fork-namespaced component and system.
- **Skip** — The content is only as good as the entity ids it points at: the traitor caches pull in the fork's own guns, magazines, emag and chameleon backpack and its assassin/infiltrator/marauder/subverter archetypes, so those tables need rewriting against your own item set, and the vending machines are inert until someone places them on your maps.
- **Touches** — cargo, service, salvage, antagonist, all-players
- **Needs** — [firearms-content-expansion](combat.md#firearms-content-expansion), [traitor-uplink](antag.md#traitor-uplink)

**impstation**

*activity · entangled · high confidence · 11 forks carry it*

Impstation stocks the station with its own branded vending machines that chatter their own ad barks: a sports-drink cooler pushing "healthy bodies, healthy bottom line", a Discount Dan's shrimp-snack machine that warns you the snacks "cannot and will not smile back", a juice machine that nags you about booze, a salvage wardrobe selling mining gear with jokes about fresh-looking corpses, and Deadly Dan's, which sells a cheap disposable pistol and hidden ammo behind lines like "Safety feature? The point is to be Deadly!" Each machine also has a wire-hacked contraband row (a sniper rifle, a clown mask, a cigarette pack) so breaking one open is worth doing. Alongside that, the crates cargo can order and the lockers, closets and boxes scattered around the map are refilled from fork tables, so you order a princess dress-up crate, a theremin, a drum kit or a box of 500 cigarettes, and the wall closets you pry open hold the fork's own uniform and clothing sets rather than the stock ones.

- **Take** — It is the cheapest possible way to make a station feel like your fork: pure YAML lists of items, ad lines and prices, no C# at all, and you can adopt one vendor or one crate at a time.
- **Skip** — Almost every entry points at Impstation's own items (shrimp snacks, Asterade sodas, the Valu pistol, fork instruments and plushies), so a naive copy yields a wall of missing-prototype errors unless you first port the content those lists sell; and several fill files reuse upstream prototype ids like ClosetWallAtmosphericsFilled, which collides with the upstream definitions rather than sitting quietly beside them.
- **Touches** — cargo, salvage, service, security, all-players

**rmc14**

*loop-change · entangled · medium confidence · 11 forks carry it*

Instead of a machine that spits out one item per click, RMC-14's vendors open a shop window with named tabs (Ammo, Smokables, Radio Keys, Kits) where every item carries a price and you spend a personal budget that the machine remembers per person. Vendors only unlock for the right job or rank, some categories let you take exactly one thing and then close for you, some hand you a pre-filled box instead of a loose item, and squad specialist kits are limited server-wide so once two people have claimed a kit nobody else can. Buying certain kits also rewrites your visible role title, name prefix and map marker, so the crew can see what you picked. Points can trickle back over time on some machines, a machine with the right engineering know-how can be hacked open after a short delay to bypass its access lock, and anyone can top a machine back up by feeding it spare stock.

- **Take** — Turns "walk to vending machine, click item" into a real per-character equipment budget with scarcity, role gating and public role identity, which gives every department a meaningful loadout decision at round start instead of a free-for-all grab.
- **Skip** — The vendor gating is wired straight into RMC-14's rank, skill and squad systems (rank lists, an engineering skill level for hacking, squad mapping, global specialist-kit caps), so grafting it means either importing those systems or rewriting every gate, and then hand-authoring an entire priced catalogue per machine before a single one is usable.
- **Touches** — all-players, command, security, medical, cargo, service
- **Needs** — [squads-ranks-and-skills](jobs.md#squads-ranks-and-skills), [loadout-system](jobs.md#loadout-system)
- **Verified** — partly supported. Core mechanics check out against source: CMAutomatedVendorComponent has Jobs/Ranks lists, Hackable/HackSkill="RMCSkillEngineer"/HackSkillLevel=2/HackDelay, CanManualRestock, and points-per-person via CMVendorUserComponent.Points. CMVendorSection has named sections (confirmed live in portable_vendor.yml with tabs like "Smokables"/"Radio keys"/"Ammo"), TakeOne/TakeAll, and Box/BoxAmount/BoxSlots for pre-filled containers (HasBoxes). RMCVendorSpecialistComponent.GlobalSharedVends + SharedCMAutomatedVendorSystem.cs (lines ~610-650) confirm a server-wide shared cap that sums vends across every vendor instance and blocks once the total hits the section's SharedSpecLimit. But I grepped the entire _RMC14 prototype tree (2012 yml files) and found exactly one live SharedSpecLimit value in the whole repo: `specialist.yml:13: sharedSpecLimit: 1`, not 2 — so per the actual data the cap is "one person claims it and it's gone," not "once two people have claimed it." Role-title/prefix/icon/map-blip rewrite on purchase is real (GiveSquadRoleName/GivePrefix/GiveIcon/GiveMapBlip fields, all populated in Squad/specialist.yml with rmc-job-name-weapons-specialist-* strings). Recharge-over-time is real but used by exactly one job (Liaison) via RMCVendorUserRechargeComponent — "some machines" is defensible as-is. Manual restock (CanManualRestock) is used by several vendors (intel, pilot, requisitions, squad engineer/prep). Hackable is used on several PVE-faction vendors. On graft_shape: the claim already downgraded the path-data "drop-in" seed to "entangled," citing squads-ranks-and-skills + loadout-system. I confirm the ranks/skills half hard: RankPrototype and SkillDefinitionComponent (engineer skill for hacking) are DataFields directly on the vendor component, not optional. The loadout-system half is weaker than claimed: I found zero references to "loadout" anywhere in Content.Shared/_RMC14/Vendors/*.cs, and the two loadout evidence files cited (civilian.yml patches, backpacks.yml satchels) belong to the separate character-creation loadout/cost system with no code path connecting it to vendors that I could find. That doesn't change the bottom-line verdict — ranks+jobs+skills entanglement alone is sufficient to keep this "entangled," not drop-in — but the depends_on list overstates the loadout-system link specifically.

**starlight**

*activity · entangled · high confidence · 11 forks carry it*

Starlight's machines come in two kinds. The free ones stock and dispense like normal: a DeForest medical dispenser, its Syndicate counterpart Interdyne, a Donk Pocket machine, an ice cream freezer of packaged popsicles, cornuti, sundaes and frozen sandwiches, a lawyer's Legaldrobe, plus mining, salvage, engineering, fashion, plushie, secret and CentComm units and an abductor gland dispenser handing out mystery organs. Each one barks its own advertising patter while idle and a farewell when you walk off, written per machine (the Legaldrobe nags you to go pester Security; Interdyne cheerfully says its stuff kills). The second kind charges money: clown, mime, medical and security storefronts list every item with a credit price, a stock limit and an ID check, so the clown buys a fake grenade or a double toy sword with earned credits and once the last of four is gone nobody else gets one that round. Away from the machines, the fork rewrites what is inside things: boxes, crates, lockers, backpacks, duffels, toolboxes, first aid kits, briefcases, suit storage and paper folders get randomised contents, so a second-hand chameleon kit may arrive complete or missing its mask and headset. Machines you build yourself spawn empty rather than fully stocked.

- **Take** — It is the only version here that turns vending into a priced, ID-gated, limited-stock storefront per job rather than a free dispenser, so buying from a machine is a real spending decision.
- **Skip** — A large share of the rosters are credit-priced listings with buyer-access and limited-stock conditions, so without Starlight's credit economy and access plumbing those vendors arrive dead, and the fill tables reference Starlight's own item prototypes throughout.
- **Touches** — service, medical, security, cargo, engineering, antagonist, all-players
- **Needs** — [player-economy-and-banking](#player-economy-and-banking), [traitor-uplink](antag.md#traitor-uplink), [abductors](antag.md#abductors)
- **Verified** — partly supported. Most of the claim checks out against the actual files (the specific ad barks, the fake-grenade stock:4 / double-toy-sword clown storefront, the secondhand chameleon kit's 65%-probability mask/headset, the EmptyVendingMachineComponent for player-built machines), and the entangled graft_shape is confirmed or understated. But the claim misclassifies six machines: mining.yml, salvage.yml, engi.yml, fashion.yml, plushies.yml, and secret.yml are all `type: listing` (SalvageTicket/NTCredit cost, ListingLimitedStockCondition, BuyerAccessCondition/MindRoleCondition-gated) — the same priced-storefront mechanism as the clown/mime/security/medical vendors the claim puts in its 'second kind' — yet the claim lists them under 'The free ones stock and dispense like normal.'
  - unsupported: "plus mining, salvage, engineering, fashion, plushie, secret and CentComm units" listed as part of "the free ones [that] stock and dispense like normal" — mining.yml, salvage.yml, engi.yml, fashion.yml, plushies.yml and secret.yml are all `type: listing` priced storefronts (SalvageTicket or NTCredit cost, ListingLimitedStockCondition stock caps, BuyerAccessCondition/MindRoleCondition access gates), the same mechanism the claim itself later describes as 'the second kind' that charges money — only CentComm (centcomm.yml) among that group is actually a free vendingMachineInventory.

**serbia-strong**

*activity · entangled · medium confidence · 11 forks carry it*

Two different things ride under one name here. The Shlepovend is a kiosk you walk up to and open like any vending machine, except it does not sell to everyone: it reads the Discord role tied to your account, shows you the reward groups your tier unlocks (groups above your tier are either greyed out or hidden entirely), and gives you a small allowance of tokens at the start of each round to spend. Pick an item, it spawns straight into your hand with a vend clunk, and your token count drops. Tokens do not bank; whatever you did not spend is wiped when the next round starts, and some groups are locked to an exact role rather than "this tier or better", so a developer-only shelf stays developer-only. Alongside that, the fork rewrites what is actually inside things you open. Survival boxes are split per role (a brigmedic box carries a medsec breath mask, a space medipen, a flare, a nutribrick and a crowbar; nitrogen variants exist), ERT backpacks, duffels and satchels are packed per specialty, there are cultist backpacks and filled briefcases, filled lockers for CI agents and officers, Centcomm pilots, SRT operatives and the CBURN commander, and cargo can order a full kvass tank.

- **Take** — The container-fill half is a large, self-contained restock of survival boxes, ERT kits and event-role lockers that lands entirely in fork-namespaced YAML and needs no code at all.
- **Skip** — The Shlepovend half is not a vending machine so much as a donator-perk dispenser: it will not function without the fork's Discord sponsor-tier stack and a token field bolted onto server-side player data, so taking it means taking that whole account-linking apparatus too.
- **Touches** — all-players, cargo, security, medical, ghost-roles, command
- **Needs** — [sponsor-and-donator-perks](misc.md#sponsor-and-donator-perks), [discord-and-external-services](misc.md#discord-and-external-services)
- **Verified** — supported. Fetched all 8 cited evidence files plus several read_in_order files not in the narrow evidence list (SharedShlepovendSystem.cs, client ShlepovendWindow/Group.xaml.cs, CultistBackpack.yml, briefcases.yml, duffelbag.yml, satchel.yml) and every substantive claim checks out: OnPurchaseAttempt in ShlepovendSystem.cs literally spawns the item, calls TryPickupAnyHand, decrements ShlepovendTokens, and plays SoundVend; the code comment reads "Check if player got discord role that is required by reward group"; OnRoundStart nulls ShlepovendTokens each round; ShlepaRewardGroupPrototype has IsExactRoleRequired with the doc comment "Use this for stuff like developer rewards" plus IsHiddenOnInsufficient; the client's AddAllGroups/UpdateButtonAvailabiity split matches "greyed out or hidden entirely." The container-fill half matches exactly: BoxSurvivalBrigmedic/Nitrogen in emergency.yml, per-role ERT backpacks in backpack.yml, cultist backpack, filled briefcases, per-role satchels (IAA, Psychologist), lockers.ftl naming CI agent/officer, Centcomm pilot, SRT operative, CBURN commander, and FoodCrateKvassTank in cargo_food.yml. The entangled classification is well earned: the purchase path hard-codes ContentData().SponsorInfo and a Discord-tier check, so the vending half cannot run without the fork's sponsor/Discord stack, exactly as depends_on states, while the take/skip split correctly isolates the self-contained loot-table half.

### Expedition missions and objectives

<a id="expedition-missions"></a>

Structured off-station runs with typed objectives (capture, rescue, purge, shutdown), mission consoles, rewards and deployable shelters.

**deltav**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 5 forks carry it*

Salvagers earn Mining Points by feeding ore into the station's ore processor and smelting it, then tap their ID card on the processor to bank the points to themselves. Those points are the currency for going back out: firing the salvage magnet to haul in another wreck costs a listed number of Mining Points, so the shift becomes a self-funding loop of drag ore home, cash it in, buy the next wreck. Miners also get a voucher they redeem at a vendor for exactly one loadout of their choosing (a kinetic crusher kit, a fulton extraction-and-rescue kit that lets them ship ore and dead bodies home without the shuttle, a survival kit with explorer's webbing and a holoflare gun, a crusher-dagger small arms kit, or a conscription kit that outfits a second crewmember as a miner). Out in the field they can throw down a shelter capsule, which shakes, warns everyone to stand back, and unfolds into a small prefab room on solid ground for a pressurised place to retreat to.

- **Take** — It turns salvage from an open-ended "go get stuff" chore into a closed economic loop where the department funds its own next expedition, which gives miners a concrete score to push and a reason to keep processing ore rather than hoarding it.
- **Skip** — Mining Points are not self-contained: the earning side is bolted onto the upstream lathe (a new recipe field, a print event hook, and a "claim points" button in the lathe UI) and the spending side patches the salvage magnet and ID card prototypes, so grafting it means touching upstream files that will conflict on every sync. **frontier**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 5 forks carry it*

A crew flies their ship out to open space, uses the salvage console aboard, and picks one of five offered planetside missions, each listing the enemy faction, a time limit, and hazard modifiers like night time, extreme cold, no breathable air, plasma atmosphere, or a rushed clock. ### Lavaland mining planet

<a id="lavaland-mining-planet"></a>

A procedurally generated hostile planet reached by mining shuttle, with ruins, chasms, tendril nests, elite fauna and dedicated miner equipment.

**far-horizons, goobstation**

*loop-change · entangled · medium confidence · 6 forks carry it*

Salvagers board a mining shuttle, punch in a destination on a docking console, and fly down to a hot volcanic planet made of basalt caves: ore-veined rock walls to drill, scattered wrecks and skeleton-strewn ruins holding treasure and salvage loot, bone-armour cult shrines, and nasty surprises like a rocket launcher sitting on a stone table with a hidden turret pointed at whoever grabs it. The planet's thin atmosphere is part of the fight — armour and weapons behave differently at low pressure, so kit that works on-station does not work the same down there. The headline draw is the big monsters: named bosses that telegraph their attacks with glowing warning shapes on the floor so you can read the wind-up and dodge, cycle through named moves (a warning shot that trails each player before dropping, a wide sweep aimed at whoever is furthest away, a flashy spin around itself), spawn healing clones you have to ignore rather than attack, and then shrink the arena for a final showdown once they are nearly dead. Boss music kicks in, and killing one drops upgrade parts you bolt onto your melee weapons and guns.

- **Take** — It gives salvage an actual destination with escalating stakes — a mining planet, readable telegraphed boss fights, and looted weapon upgrades — instead of the station-bound magnet-and-asteroid loop.
- **Skip** — Goob's version is roughly 900 files spanning procedural planet generation, a dedicated shuttle console, a pressure-based combat rules layer, megafauna, and a weapon-upgrade economy; you are adopting a whole department's worth of content and its maintenance burden, not one system, and the Far Horizons boss layer additionally leans on vendored Starlight sprites and its own Abyss faction.
- **Touches** — cargo, salvage, science, ghost-roles, all-players
- **Needs** — [hostile-fauna-and-npcs](misc.md#hostile-fauna-and-npcs)
- **Verified** — supported. Every specific gameplay beat I checked was directly backed by real code: elder.yml's BossCombat/mechanics field pointed at elder_boss_mechanics.yml, whose named moves (warning-shot-that-trails-then-drops, furthest-target sweep, self-centered flourish, healing-decoy clones that heal the boss extra when attacked, final-showdown arena shrink at 20% health) match the claim's move-by-move description almost verbatim; bait.yml literally contains a TableStone + WeaponLauncherRocket + WeaponTurretHostile cluster; DockingConsoleSystem.cs implements the FTL docking-console shuttle flow; PressureArmorChangeComponent.cs implements pressure-tiered armor penetration; BossMusicSystem.cs and MeleeUpgradesSystem.cs/MegafaunaWeaponLooterComponent.cs confirm boss music and weapon-upgrade looting.
