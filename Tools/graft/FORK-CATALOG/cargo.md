### Vending machines and container fills

<a id="vending-and-loot-catalogs"></a>

Fork-specific vending machine rosters with their own inventories and ad barks, plus the crate, locker, box and maintenance loot tables that stock the station.

**ephemeral-space**

*activity · entangled · medium confidence · 11 forks carry it*

Adds a set of station-flavour vending machines with their own product lists and chatty ad barks, including a pride-clothing "Gender Bash" dispenser, a scrap-dealer machine hawking military and engineering surplus scavenged from derelicts, a plain water cooler, and a lumber-and-welding-fuel machine that warns you not to light fires indoors. Their stock is rolled from loot tables at round start rather than being a fixed shelf, so two rounds of the same machine can offer different goods. It also rewrites what you find when you crack open lockers, dressers, wardrobes, backpacks, briefcases and maintenance crates: theatre wardrobes hand out whole themed costumes (jester, chicken suit, cowboy in one of three colours, shrine maiden, Santa), and there are hidden ceiling caches that spawn a matched antagonist kit, for example a silenced pistol with spare magazine, sleepy pens and smoke grenades, or a chameleon backpack with an emag and C4.

- **Take** — Randomised, table-driven vending stock plus themed costume and cache fills is a cheap way to make looting and maintenance scavenging feel different every round, and the machinery for it is a single small fork-namespaced component and system.
- **Skip** — The content is only as good as the entity ids it points at: the traitor caches pull in the fork's own guns, magazines, emag and chameleon backpack and its assassin/infiltrator/marauder/subverter archetypes, so those tables need rewriting against your own item set, and the vending machines are inert until someone places them on your maps.
- **Touches** — cargo, service, salvage, antagonist, all-players
- **Needs** — [firearms-content-expansion](combat.md#firearms-content-expansion), [traitor-uplink](antag.md#traitor-uplink)

**impstation**

*activity · entangled · high confidence · 11 forks carry it*

Impstation stocks the station with its own branded vending machines that chatter their own ad barks: a sports-drink cooler pushing "healthy bodies, healthy bottom line", a Discount Dan's shrimp-snack machine that warns you the snacks "cannot and will not smile back", a juice machine that nags you about booze, a salvage wardrobe selling mining gear with jokes about fresh-looking corpses, and Deadly Dan's, which sells a cheap disposable pistol and hidden ammo behind lines like "Safety feature? **rmc14**

*loop-change · entangled · medium confidence · 11 forks carry it*

Instead of a machine that spits out one item per click, RMC-14's vendors open a shop window with named tabs (Ammo, Smokables, Radio Keys, Kits) where every item carries a price and you spend a personal budget that the machine remembers per person. Vendors only unlock for the right job or rank, some categories let you take exactly one thing and then close for you, some hand you a pre-filled box instead of a loose item, and squad specialist kits are limited server-wide so once two people have claimed a kit nobody else can. Buying certain kits also rewrites your visible role title, name prefix and map marker, so the crew can see what you picked. Points can trickle back over time on some machines, a machine with the right engineering know-how can be hacked open after a short delay to bypass its access lock, and anyone can top a machine back up by feeding it spare stock.

- **Take** — Turns "walk to vending machine, click item" into a real per-character equipment budget with scarcity, role gating and public role identity, which gives every department a meaningful loadout decision at round start instead of a free-for-all grab.
- **Skip** — The vendor gating is wired straight into RMC-14's rank, skill and squad systems (rank lists, an engineering skill level for hacking, squad mapping, global specialist-kit caps), so grafting it means either importing those systems or rewriting every gate, and then hand-authoring an entire priced catalogue per machine before a single one is usable.
- **Touches** — all-players, command, security, medical, cargo, service
- **Needs** — [squads-ranks-and-skills](jobs.md#squads-ranks-and-skills), [loadout-system](jobs.md#loadout-system)

### Cargo bounties, contracts and markets

<a id="cargo-bounties-and-market"></a>

Extends cargo beyond ordering with player-posted bounty contracts, market consoles, trade crates with destination routing and delivery-based hauling work.

**rmc14**

*loop-change · entangled · high confidence · 3 forks carry it*

The supply tech works a counter rather than a console: crew queue up at a shutter and ask for gear by name, and the tech decides whether each request is reasonable before handing it over from a bank of specialist vendors. Bigger or rarer orders go through an ASRS terminal that spends a shared budget, and the goods physically arrive on a cargo elevator that has to be raised and lowered — standing in the open pit while it comes down is genuinely lethal. Every delivered crate comes with a printed invoice listing its contents; the tech tears it off, stamps it approved and returns it for a cash rebate, and empty crates are re-packed by hand and set on colour-coded launch pads so command (or the tech, for the emergency pad, using coordinates radioed up from the ground) can fire them down to a team fighting elsewhere.

- **Take** — It turns cargo from a menu-and-wait chore into an actual shift of work: a physical delivery elevator, hand-packed crates, stamped invoices for income, and a remote drop system that makes the supply role matter to people who are nowhere near the department.
- **Skip** — Almost none of it is portable as-is: the catalogue is wall-to-wall RMC-specific ammunition and attachment crates, the drop pads are keyed to that fork's squad system, and the elevator/pit and pad layout are built into their map, so you would be reimplementing the loop rather than copying it.
- **Touches** — cargo, command, engineering, all-players
- **Needs** — [squads-ranks-and-skills](jobs.md#squads-ranks-and-skills), [firearms-content-expansion](combat.md#firearms-content-expansion)
- **Verified** — supported. Fetched RMCRequisitions.xml, requisitions_catalog.yml, RequisitionsSystem.Rewards.cs, and SupplyDropComputerComponent.cs directly. The guidebook text confirms, near verbatim, the counter/shutter workflow, tech judgment on requests, the four vendor bank, the ASRS terminal's shared budget, the Pit elevator (RequisitionsElevatorComponent has a ChasmCheckEvery field and the guide warns "you might not wake up" when lowered), printed/stamped invoices for a cash reward (PrintInvoice + SubmitInvoices in RequisitionsSystem.Rewards.cs), and the five colour-coded drop pads with CIC controlling four squad pads and the tech controlling the coordinate-fed Echo pad. The catalog file is 417 lines of RMC-specific ammo/attachment crates, and SupplyDropComputerComponent.cs has a hard `EntProtoId<SquadTeamComponent>? Squad` field, directly substantiating the claim's own downgrade from the seed's 'drop-in' guess to 'entangled' via a real dependency on the fork's squad system plus firearms-specific content.

**white-dream**

*activity · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 3 forks carry it*

A glowing red target circle burns onto the floor with a long incoming-mortar whistle, and about ten seconds later something drops out of the sky onto that spot: a supply pod that cracks open with a small blast and spills its contents, or a rocket that just detonates. The pods come in a range of flavours and payloads, from a surplus cargo crate of random goods to Syndicate surplus bundles worth anywhere from 5 to 125 telecrystals, a Cybersun Juggernaut kit, or a live gondola that wanders off. Crew who see the marker get a few seconds to sprint toward the free loot or run away from it, and the landed pods stay behind as containers you can pry open, weld apart for steel, or even build yourself from three steel sheets.

- **Take** — It turns any loot delivery into a telegraphed, dodgeable event the whole room reacts to, and the whole thing is prototype-only YAML built on generic spawner/despawn components.
- **Skip** — Despite sitting under the cargo-market heading it posts no bounties and runs no market: it is a delivery-and-spectacle wrapper around payloads upstream already hands out, and it drags in the fork's own telegraph, falling-pod and rocket sprites plus a custom whistle sound before it looks like anything.
- **Touches** — cargo, antagonist, all-players, admin
- **Verified** — partly supported; assessed as drop-in, corrected by the verifier. The telegraph/pod/rocket flavor description checks out against the cited YAML (red target circle, 10s TimedSpawner + 12s TimedDespawn, mortar whistle sound, SmallExplosionInstant crack-open, MediumExplosionInstant for rockets, 5/10/15/20/25/50/125 TK syndicate SurplusBundle tiers, CybersunJuggernautBundle StorageFill, MobGondola). But the feature is not "prototype-only YAML built on generic spawner/despawn components": the actual implementation of the Syndicate surplus loot lives in Content.Server/Traitor/Uplink/SurplusBundle/SurplusBundleComponent.cs and SurplusBundleSystem.cs, brand-new C# files sitting inside the upstream Traitor/Uplink directory (not under _White), and that system calls Content.Server.Store.Systems.StoreSystem.GetAvailableListings against the vanilla uplink Store/currency prototypes (StorePresetUplink in Resources/Prototypes/Store/presets.yml) to pick which Telecrystal-priced items go in each bundle. That is a real dependency the seed's drop-in path-scan missed (it only scanned _White-prefixed prototype paths), so this should be downgraded off drop-in.
  - unsupported: "the whole thing is prototype-only YAML built on generic spawner/despawn components" (take_because) — the Syndicate surplus-bundle payloads are generated by custom C# (SurplusBundleSystem.cs) that queries the upstream Store/Uplink catalog system at runtime, not by static YAML on generic components.

**starlight**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 3 forks carry it*

Departmental cargo orders arrive shrink-wrapped in a coloured tamper seal keyed to whoever paid for them, so a cargo tech's job becomes hauling the crate to the right department rather than dumping it in the lobby: when someone from that department opens the seal, the deliverer is paid a bounty in spesos, and someone ripping it off by hand or cutting it open instead gets the deliverer fined and the buyer refunded. If a station keeps breaking seals, the Interstellar Trade Guild sends a station-wide announcement telling command to look into whoever is handling their orders. Alongside that, cargo gets roughly three dozen extra order options (armoury, atmos, engineering, livestock, novelty, mail trolleys and bags), bulk 1000-mol gas orders including chilled liquid variants, a pallet that buys and sells gas straight through connected pipes instead of by the canister, and a handheld Mail Companion that scans a letter or parcel and relays the recipient's live suit-sensor coordinates on a countdown before needing to recalibrate.

- **Take** — It turns cargo from a vending machine into an actual courier job with per-delivery pay, a fine for cutting corners, and a station-wide reputation that command hears about.
- **Skip** — Tamper seals wrap every departmental order, so you are retuning how fast every department on the station gets its supplies, not just adding a cargo minigame, and the payout/fine loop assumes the fork's department-account and seal-on-spawn wiring rather than sitting beside it.
- **Touches** — cargo, atmospherics, engineering, medical, security, science, service, command, all-players
- **Needs** — [mail-delivery](#mail-delivery), [exotic-gas-expansion](atmos.md#exotic-gas-expansion)
- **Verified** — supported. SharedTamperSealValueSystem.OnSealOpened/OnSealDestroyed calls TryAdjustBankAccount(bank, mutation.Account, ...) against a per-department "Accounts" dictionary (Cargo/Engineering/Medical/Science/Security/Service) with RevenueDistribution and PrimaryCut/LockboxCut fields. I fetched Content.Shared/Cargo/Components/StationBankAccountComponent.cs directly (an upstream-namespaced path, not under _Starlight) and it already contains this multi-account dictionary structure baked in — meaning the Starlight fork's own repo has rewritten a genuinely upstream file to support the department-account model the seal reward/fine loop requires. That confirms the claim's 'needs-upstream-edits' call and specifically proves the fork-patches-upstream reading: the path-data's 'zero files outside fork namespace' signal was blind to this in-place edit, exactly as the seed_means note warned.

**ephemeral-space, gaby-station-now-dumont-station, goobstation, impstation, monolith, serbia-strong, sunrise**

*activity · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 3 forks carry it*

Mostly this is more stuff to buy and more stuff to hunt down. Several forks bolt extra pages onto the cargo order catalog (armory, food, fun, materials, medical, hydroponics, ship ammo, armor plates) and add their own pools of bounties, so the bounty board keeps throwing new fetch jobs at the crew with written-out flavour requests: send us muffins for our apology service, send us insulated gloves, grow pyrotton for a picky pet. Monolith adds separate pirate and Soviet bounty boards that pay for looted faction hardsuits, ship turrets and bluespace drives, and Sunrise adds tiered salvage jobs that pay salvagers for hauling back specific expedition loot. The one real system here is Ephemeral Space's supply request computer: any department can type a request into a console, it lands in a queue cargo can see, and the requester watches its tag move through Pending, In Progress, Completed, Cancelled or Denied, with a searchable warehouse manifest computer showing what is actually sitting on the storeroom pallets. Impstation also prints a paper invoice with each delivered order naming the item, who requested it, which account paid, who approved it and why.

- **Take** — The bounty pools and catalog pages are pure YAML content that gives cargo and salvage more to do all round without touching how anything already works, and Ephemeral's request console finally gives non-cargo departments a real channel to ask for things instead of shouting on radio.
- **Skip** — Almost every bounty and catalog entry points at entities, tags and sprites the donor fork already has, so a graft is a long tail of missing-prototype errors, and the Monolith pirate board is unusable without that fork's ship-combat content; the request console and printed invoices are the only parts that are real systems and they need console prototypes on your maps plus hooks into the upstream order flow.
- **Touches** — cargo, salvage, command, all-players, antagonist
- **Verified** — partly supported. I fetched all 8 cited files plus follow-up browsing. Salvagers board a mining shuttle, punch in a destination on a docking console, and fly down to a hot volcanic planet made of basalt caves: ore-veined rock walls to drill, scattered wrecks and skeleton-strewn ruins holding treasure and salvage loot, bone-armour cult shrines, and nasty surprises like a rocket launcher sitting on a stone table with a hidden turret pointed at whoever grabs it. The planet's thin atmosphere is part of the fight — armour and weapons behave differently at low pressure, so kit that works on-station does not work the same down there. The headline draw is the big monsters: named bosses that telegraph their attacks with glowing warning shapes on the floor so you can read the wind-up and dodge, cycle through named moves (a warning shot that trails each player before dropping, a wide sweep aimed at whoever is furthest away, a flashy spin around itself), spawn healing clones you have to ignore rather than attack, and then shrink the arena for a final showdown once they are nearly dead. Boss music kicks in, and killing one drops upgrade parts you bolt onto your melee weapons and guns.

- **Take** — It gives salvage an actual destination with escalating stakes — a mining planet, readable telegraphed boss fights, and looted weapon upgrades — instead of the station-bound magnet-and-asteroid loop.
- **Skip** — Goob's version is roughly 900 files spanning procedural planet generation, a dedicated shuttle console, a pressure-based combat rules layer, megafauna, and a weapon-upgrade economy; you are adopting a whole department's worth of content and its maintenance burden, not one system, and the Far Horizons boss layer additionally leans on vendored Starlight sprites and its own Abyss faction.
- **Touches** — cargo, salvage, science, ghost-roles, all-players
- **Needs** — [hostile-fauna-and-npcs](misc.md#hostile-fauna-and-npcs)
- **Verified** — supported. Every specific gameplay beat I checked was directly backed by real code: elder.yml's BossCombat/mechanics field pointed at elder_boss_mechanics.yml, whose named moves (warning-shot-that-trails-then-drops, furthest-target sweep, self-centered flourish, healing-decoy clones that heal the boss extra when attacked, final-showdown arena shrink at 20% health) match the claim's move-by-move description almost verbatim; bait.yml literally contains a TableStone + WeaponLauncherRocket + WeaponTurretHostile cluster; DockingConsoleSystem.cs implements the FTL docking-console shuttle flow; PressureArmorChangeComponent.cs implements pressure-tiered armor penetration; BossMusicSystem.cs and MeleeUpgradesSystem.cs/MegafaunaWeaponLooterComponent.cs confirm boss music and weapon-upgrade looting. The 'entangled' graft_shape is also confirmed in the code itself: TileChaserSystem.cs depends on the fork's own Anger and Megafauna namespaces, elder.rsi is sprited from a vendored _Starlight/Mobs/Abyss path with an Abyss NPC faction, and the Content.Server/_Lavaland tree spans Procedural, Biome, Megafauna, Weapons, Atmos, Shuttles as separate subsystems — exactly the sprawl the claim describes as the reason to skip it.
