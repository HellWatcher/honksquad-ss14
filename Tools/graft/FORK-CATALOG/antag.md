**rmc14**

*structural · entangled · high confidence · 9 forks carry it*

Instead of personal antag objectives, the whole marine side works one shared intelligence economy. Players scavenge the colony for paper scraps, progress reports, folders and technical manuals, read them in order (you can't understand a manual until you've read simpler documents), type what they learned into the intel computer, then store the document back in the intel room. Other point sources include hauling out experimental devices, decrypting data disks at a SIGINT terminal using a password found in earlier clues, hacking planetside data terminals (which need colony power and comms restored first), cracking safes whose combinations come from document clues, rescuing survivors back to the ship, morgue-ing dead colonists, and dragging dead xenos into the research containment room (bigger corpse, more points). A handheld detector pings the direction of nearby intel, a shared "View Intel Objectives" action shows team progress, and the points get spent at a CIC tech tree console on tiered unlocks: an armoured vehicle, extra requisitions and dropship budget, orbital bombardment warheads, waking reinforcement troops, each with a station-wide announcement when it lands.

- **Take** — It turns "objectives" from a private checklist into a visible, shared side-wide economy where exploration and corpse recovery buy real round-changing assets, giving non-combat players a concrete way to shape how the round goes.
- **Skip** — Nothing about it is portable as-is: the point sources assume RMC-14's colony/warship map layout, xeno corpses, survivors and dedicated intel jobs, and the spend side buys orbital cannon warheads, dropship and requisitions budgets and reinforcement waves that only exist in that fork, so you'd be rebuilding both ends around your own round structure.
- **Touches** — command, cargo, science, medical, security, all-players
- **Needs** — [alternate-round-types](#alternate-round-types), [extra-job-roster](jobs.md#extra-job-roster), [cargo-bounties-and-market](cargo.md#cargo-bounties-and-market)
- **Verified** — supported. Re-fetched both cited files plus the Content.Shared/_RMC14/Intel component directory listing and the detector system source. Every specific gameplay beat in the claim checks out against the guidebook text almost verbatim: read-order gating (scrap->report->folder->manual), entering knowledge into the intel computer then re-storing the document, SIGINT terminal disk decryption with clue-derived keys, planetside data terminals gated on colony power+comms, safe combinations from clues, survivors counted on ship arrival, colonist corpses to morgue, xeno corpses to research containment scaled by size, and a CIC tech tree spending intel points on an ARC vehicle, requisitions/dropship budget increases, orbital-cannon warheads, and waking cryo troops/specialists, each firing a TechAnnounceEvent. The detector system I read independently (not in the cited evidence) confirms a motion-detector-style blip mechanic keyed to IntelDetectorTrackedComponent (present on all intel item prototypes), matching the 'pings nearby intel' claim. On graft_shape: all files I found (Resources/Prototypes/_RMC14/..., Content.Shared/_RMC14/Intel/...) stay inside the fork's own _RMC14 namespace, so the seed's path-based 'zero files outside namespace' finding holds — but the detector system's imports (Xenonids.Parasite, motion detector, requisitions inventory) and the tech tree's direct calls into RMCOrbitalCannonWarhead*, VehicleHumveeARC, dropship/requisitions budget events, and cryo-marine waking confirm genuine functional entanglement with other RMC14-specific systems, exactly matching the claim's own 'entangled' downgrade and its listed reasons.
  - unsupported: "each with a station-wide announcement when it lands" — the TechAnnounceEvent fires at the moment the tech-tree purchase is made, not tied specifically to a delivery/landing event; every tier purchase (including non-delivery ones like tier unlocks) gets the same announcement, so 'when it lands' slightly over-specifies the trigger.

**starlight**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 9 forks carry it*

Traitors get a set of open-ended "story" objectives aimed at a randomly chosen department instead of only steal/kill tasks: frame someone in Engineering as a Syndicate agent, spread rumours that Medical is starting an uprising, publicly disgrace a department head, impersonate a Cargo tech and trash their reputation, stir up a feud between two departments, or waste a department's resources however you like. Because nothing checks those automatically, an antagonist gets a "Write objective summary" button and a text window they can edit all shift to explain how they pulled it off; whatever they wrote is printed under their name in the round-end report. The uplink also grows a "Second Hand" tab that stocks 8-14 random cut-price used Syndicate items per round (one each, then the entry vanishes), plus limited-stock listings, listings that only appear for certain roles, at certain player counts, or when you rolled a specific objective (for example a whole Die-A-Glorious-Death aisle of grenade rigs, ammo duffels and Antimov boards that only unlocks if you drew the suicide-mission objective), and extra aisles for allied cyborgs, cybernetics, implants and joke purchases.

- **Take** — The story objectives plus the player-written round-end summary turn traitoring from a checklist into social play, and they are the rare antag content that makes the whole crew's shift more interesting rather than just arming one player harder.
- **Skip** — Unverifiable objectives lean entirely on player honesty and admin attention, and the bundle drags in a pile of antag-specific conditions (abductor, absorb, blood drain, damn, brighteye) that are dead weight unless you already run those antags.

**ephemeral-space**

*structural · entangled · high confidence · 9 forks carry it*

Antagonists no longer get a private shopping list of "steal X, kill Y" tasks. Instead the whole traitor side shares one visible chain: a handful of smaller jobs first (kill a specific crewmember, bug a department's APC, sabotage the comms computer, crew monitoring server, criminal records, emergency access terminal or telecom processor), and only once those are done can they hack three Security Override Terminals scattered in the HoP's secure room, the captain's office and secure tech storage to unlock the nuclear code, then arm the nuke with the captain's disk. Each objective shows in a panel tagged Team or Solo with a live percentage of how far along it is, and at round end each line reads Success or Failed with that percentage. Kill targets can be handed to you under a codename rather than a name, and an ability lets you sense roughly how far away and in which direction your target is. The crew have their own counterpart objective (keep the station powered, outrun the radstorm and charge the warp drive to escape), and a sympathizer variant simply asks a hidden helper to work out who the traitors are and help them set off the nuke.

- **Take** — It turns antag objectives from a private end-of-round scorecard into the round's actual spine: a visible, prerequisite-gated team chain with live progress percentages that both sides are effectively racing over, which is a far stronger driver of round narrative than upstream's independent steal/kill list.
- **Skip** — It is not an objective library you can bolt on, it is Ephemeral Space's whole round: the objectives assume secret identities and organisations to assign them, a warp drive and radstorm for the crew side, fork-only consoles (emergency access terminal, radio scrambler, crypto nuke terminals) as sabotage targets, and a three-terminal nuke flow that replaces the standard nuke round. Grafting it means importing those systems or rewriting every condition against your own station's machines.
- **Touches** — antagonist, all-players, command, security, engineering, ghost-roles
- **Needs** — [secret-identities](#secret-identities), [warp-drive](engineering.md#warp-drive), [alternate-round-types](#alternate-round-types)
