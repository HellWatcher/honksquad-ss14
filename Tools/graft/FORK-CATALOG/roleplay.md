### Server rules, space law and SOP guidebook

<a id="guidebook-rules-and-sop"></a>

Fork-authored in-game documentation covering server rules, space law, escalation policy, departmental standard operating procedure and onboarding guides.

**corvax, cosmatic-drift, funky, gaby-station-now-dumont-station, goobstation, harmony-station, impstation, monolith, rmc14, serbia-strong, sunrise, viva-station**

*loop-change · drop-in · high confidence · 11 forks carry it*

Opening the in-game guidebook gives players a full shelf of server-written reference material instead of just upstream's mechanical guides: numbered server rules (Cosmatic Drift's Rule 1.0 through 3.9, Harmony's R0-R8 and S1-S3), a written space/corporate law codebook listing named offences with sentences, and a Standard Operating Procedure tree split department by department and often job by job (Funky Station has separate SOP pages for Captain, HoP, Warden, Detective, Chemist, Paramedic, Roboticist, Bartender, Mime, and so on, plus a page per alert level explaining what Green through Delta actually authorise). Security and Command can look up the charge or the procedure mid-round rather than arguing from memory, and a new player can read the server's actual expectations before their first shift. Viva Station goes further with a Command-only hidden SOP: the pages exist but are metashielded, and your character only "knows" them once you physically find a head of staff's issued guidebook, with higher-clearance books unlocking more of the document, which is what gives an RP-led riot something concrete to be leaking. Sunrise and RMC-14 attach onboarding on top of the text (a guided tutorial and new-player popups), which is a much larger build than the documentation itself.

- **Take** — It is pure content in fork-namespaced XML plus guideEntry prototypes, and it is the cheapest way to make Security and Command play to a codified law and procedure instead of improvising a different standard every shift.
- **Skip** — None of the text is yours: SOP, space law and numbered rules only work if they describe your server's actual policy, so grafting is really a rewrite of every page plus permanent upkeep as your rules drift, and nothing here is mechanically enforced.
- **Touches** — all-players, security, command, service, medical, engineering, cargo, science
- **Verified** — supported. Every checkable specific held up: CD's guides.ftl lists exactly Rule 1.0-3.9 plus per-department SOP and a crimes list; Harmony's guides.ftl lists exactly R0-R8 and S1-S3; Funky's sop.yml defines per-job guideEntry pages for every job named (Captain, HoP, Warden, Detective, Chemist, Paramedic, Roboticist, Bartender, Mime) plus Green-through-Delta alert pages whose XML (GreenAlert.xml) does spell out what is authorised; Corvax's CorporateLaw dir has numbered offence chapters plus Punishments.xml/CrimeList.xml; Viva's CommandHiddenStandardOperatingProcedure.xml states verbatim the metashield and the three clearance tiers unlocked by finding specific heads' guidebooks (worth knowing: enforcement is AHelp/admin convention, not a mechanic, which the claim's "metashielded" framing correctly implies); Sunrise has _Sunrise/Tutorial and RMC-14 has _RMC14/NewPlayer, backing the onboarding aside. Drop-in is confirmed at the mechanism level: upstream GuidebookWindow.GetSortedEntries computes roots as all entries ExceptWith children, so parentless fork-namespaced guideEntry prototypes (like Funky's SOP101) surface as top-level guidebook roots with zero upstream edits. One small practical trim: Funky's sop.yml references an OctarineAlert page and alert-level-octarine locale key from its cosmic-cult port, and Violet/Octarine alert levels are fork-specific, so a graft drops or rewrites those child entries — content pruning, not entanglement.

### Speech accents, verbs and emotes

<a id="speech-accents-and-emotes"></a>

Extra accents, speech verbs, emote prototypes, typing indicators and quick-phrase sets, including equipment- and species-driven voice filters, plus paired and animated social emotes. Everything here is prototype- and component-level chat decoration that runs entirely in-process. External speech synthesis (an API client, voice picker, job queue, cached audio) is `text-to-speech`, not this id; per-character bark sounds, which just play a shipped .ogg on speech, stay here.

**rmc14**

*activity · entangled · medium confidence · 14 forks carry it*

Typing gets quietly cleaned up: a filter rewrites out-of-character shorthand and meta-slang as you speak, so "surv" comes out as "survivor" and "OB" as "orbital bombardment", with separate word lists for the human side and the alien side. Socially, you can offer a fistbump, high five, handshake, hug or tail swipe to a specific person, who has about ten seconds to take it before you are visibly left hanging, and each one plays its own animation and sound. Pointing at something spawns a large arrow other people can see, with a differently coloured arrow when you point for your own squad, and different species scream, laugh and chirp with their own voice clips instead of a generic one. Radio prefixes are also policed so the alien hivemind channel only works for aliens and never for humans.

- **Take** — The paired hand emotes (offer, accept, or get left hanging) and the visible pointing arrows are genuine two-player social interactions rather than decoration, and they cost nothing balance-wise.
- **Skip** — The chat core is welded to RMC's own factions: the sanitiser word lists are all Aliens-franchise jargon, the prefix routing keys off their marine/xeno components, and the squad-coloured pointing arrow reaches into their squad system, so only the emote and species-voice slice travels cleanly and the rest needs rewriting for your setting.
- **Touches** — all-players, antagonist, command, security
- **Needs** — [squads-ranks-and-skills](jobs.md#squads-ranks-and-skills), [xenomorph-hive](antag.md#xenomorph-hive)
- **Verified** — partly supported. Direct reading confirms the entanglement: RMCPointingComponent's squad-coloured arrow branch (RMCPointingSystem.cs) requires SquadMemberComponent from Content.Shared._RMC14.Marines.Squads, and SharedCMChatSystem gates the hivemind radio channel on MarineComponent/XenoComponent from the marine/xeno namespaces — exactly the squads-ranks-and-skills and xenomorph-hive dependencies claimed. The hand-emote system (fistbump/high-five/hug/handshake/tailswipe, 10s LeftHangingDelay, per-emote sound+effect, "left hanging" popup) and the pointing arrow both check out as real, self-contained mechanics. However the flagship chat-sanitizer example is factually backwards: the locale files show cm-chatsan-word-ob/orbital/orbital-bomb/orbital-bombardment all resolve cm-chatsan-replacement-* to "skyfire", not to "orbital bombardment" as the claim states — the filter turns real/meta terms into in-fiction alien slang, the opposite of what's described for that example.

**sunrise**

*activity · entangled · medium confidence · 14 forks carry it*

Adds a social interactions panel: you target another player and pick from categorised gestures (Friendly, Face, Tail, Neck, Wings, Horn) such as a handshake, high-five, hug, sniff, cheek-pinch, face-pat or tail-stroke, each firing a randomly varied third-person line plus a sound effect and a short per-gesture cooldown. Which options appear depends on the situation: a tail-pat only shows if the target actually has a visible tail, licking someone's face requires your own mouth to be uncovered and only certain species can do it, so a mask or a helmet quietly removes options from your list. On top of that, players can write their own custom interactions (name, messages, icon, sound, cooldown), pop a floating cloud emote above their head with a chat command, and factions like xenos, blob, carp and zombies get their own colour-coded telepathic chat channel with a single-letter prefix; a profanity filter and anti-spam pass sit over normal speech.

- **Take** — It hands every player a rich, condition-aware social toolbox that makes downtime roleplay actually expressive, and the interaction prototypes are pure YAML so a fork can add its own gestures without touching code.
- **Skip** — The shipped interaction messages are hardcoded Russian strings in the prototypes rather than locale keys, so every gesture needs rewriting by hand, and the bundle drags in unrelated systems (chat sanitisation partials on upstream ChatSystem, a replacement headset radio UI, collective-mind channels tied to antag content) that a fork taking "emotes" does not necessarily want.
- **Touches** — all-players, roleplay, antagonist, ghost-roles
- **Needs** — [extra-playable-species](species.md#extra-playable-species), [clothing-and-wardrobe-pack](art.md#clothing-and-wardrobe-pack)
- **Verified** — partly supported; assessed as needs upstream edits, corrected by the verifier. categories.yml, friendly.yml, face.yml and tail.yml directly confirm the interaction-panel mechanics (categories Friendly/Face/Tail/Neck/Wings, HasTargetCondition, BodyAreaTagCondition requireExposed for mouth-covering, SpeciesCondition gating FaceLickDoggyStyle to Vulpkanin/Felinid, HasVisualLayerCondition gating tail-pat on a visible Tail layer, randomized interactionMessages + interactionSounds + numeric cooldowns), CustomInteraction.cs confirms the custom-interaction fields (name/messages/icon/sound/cooldown), collective_mind.yml confirms single-letter-keycode colour-coded telepathic channels for Xeno/Blob/Carp/Zombie (plus more), and CloudEmoteCommand.cs confirms the chat-command cloud emote. **ephemeral-space**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 14 forks carry it*

Radio stops being a magic station-wide intercom: the further you are from the receiver, the more your transmission decays, with letters chewed into tildes and bursts of "--BZZT--" and "--KRSSH--" shoved between words, and past a hard range nobody hears you at all, so crews have to move toward relays or relay messages by hand. A hackable scrambler box smears that same static across every channel station-wide until someone spends fifteen seconds repairing it, and a scrambler examine tells you whether the processor is operational or malfunctioning. Alongside that, a wearable voice obfuscator makes you speak as "someone's muffled voice" (or as a monkey) instead of by name, a covert-message action pops a "Send Covert Message" dialog to push one line onto a chosen channel, a coughing affliction breaks your speech apart with hrgh/haughk/eugh interjections and cuts sentences short, and pain- and brain-damage-gated emotes like painslump and braingasp appear for hurt characters.

- **Take** — Distance-degraded radio plus a sabotageable station-wide scrambler turns comms into a resource crews fight over instead of a free omniscient channel, which is the single highest-leverage roleplay lever in this bundle.
- **Skip** — It is not the drop-in accent pack the label suggests: the falloff and distortion need hooks inside upstream's radio send/receive path, the channel gate reads a SenderWhitelist field the fork added to the upstream radio channel prototype, and nerfing global comms will make every department's coordination noticeably worse unless you also plan the relay placement and map work to compensate.
- **Touches** — all-players, command, security, antagonist, medical, engineering
- **Verified** — partly supported. Confirmed by direct fetch: Content.Shared/Radio/RadioChannelPrototype.cs (an upstream-path file, not under _ES/_Offbrand) has a `SenderWhitelist` EntityWhitelist field spliced in with literal `// ES START ... // ES END` markers, and Content.Server/Radio/EntitySystems/RadioSystem.cs (also upstream-path) has its `SendRadioMessage` body patched with ES-marked blocks that call the fork's `_esRadio.DistortMessage(...)` per receiver and add a `_esRadio` dependency. This is the fork editing files outside its own namespace directories in place, i.e. fork-patches-upstream — the opposite of the seed's drop-in guess, and exactly what the claim's skip_because already argued.
  - unsupported: A hackable scrambler box smears that same static across every channel station-wide until someone spends fifteen seconds repairing it" — the scrambler's malfunction state is set by `SetHacked(true)` inside `OnUndergoDegradationEvent` in ESSharedRadioSystem.cs, which fires from the generic `ESDegradationSystem`/`ESUndergoDegradationEvent` (Content.Shared/_ES/Degradation/ESDegradationSystem.cs), documented as equipment 'slowly breaking and degrading over the course of the round... in response to player events, not simply happening at will.' The in-game examine text only ever says 'malfunctioning', never 'hacked' or 'sabotaged'. There is no dedicated hacking/EMag/wire-panel minigame file for this component that I could find, so calling it a deliberately 'hackable' antagonist tool overstates what the evidence shows versus a passive wear-and-degrade mechanic (the 15s repair DoAfter itself is accurately described).

### Character traits and quirks

<a id="character-traits"></a>

Point-buy positive and negative traits picked at character setup that attach real components: disabilities, physical quirks, speech tics and skills.

**backmen, cosmatic-drift, einstein-engines, funky, goobstation, monolith, rmc14, serbia-strong, starlight, sunrise**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 8 forks carry it*

At character setup you spend a pool of points on quirks that follow you into the round: pick "Glass Jaw" or "Hemophiliac" and you crit sooner and bleed twice as long; pick "Wheelchair Bound" and you spawn already strapped into a chair with paralysed legs; pick "Oxygen Allergy" and you start with a nitrogen tank and mask because station air poisons you. Negative picks refund points you spend on good ones, so a doctor might take Deafness and Social Anxiety to afford Self-Aware (read your own wounds like a health analyzer) and Swift Feet. A large share of the additions are pure flavour instead: Boston, Russian, dwarven and bogan accents, Tourette outbursts that inject swears mid-sentence, felinid vocal cords, and starting languages your character speaks.

- **Take** — Every player touches it once per character, and it is the cheapest way to make crew feel individually distinct without adding a single new role or map — most entries are pure YAML on top of the trait system already in upstream.
- **Skip** — The interesting traits are not YAML: they reach into crit and death thresholds, bleeding rates, movement modifiers, vision and hearing, so each one you take is a small permanent balance and merge-conflict liability spread across upstream medical and movement code, and the accessibility-flavoured negatives (deaf, paralysed, anxiety) need a moderation stance before they go live.
- **Touches** — all-players, medical, security, service, roleplay
- **Needs** — [speech-accents-and-emotes](#speech-accents-and-emotes), [language-system](#language-system), [psionics-and-glimmer](#psionics-and-glimmer)
- **Verified** — partly supported. ### Player-authored documents and books

<a id="player-authored-text"></a>

Systems for player-written and printed text: form catalogs and document printers, signable paperwork, multi-page book readers and engraved inscriptions on items.

**sunrise**

*activity · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 11 forks carry it*

Adds a station copy machine that turns paperwork into a real activity. You walk up to it, browse a library of about 150 ready-made official forms sorted into department tabs (Central Command, Command, Security, Justice, Medical, Science, Engineering, Service, Supply, Prison, even a Syndicate set), pick one, and the machine spends a few seconds printing it while eating paper and ink from its own stocks. The printed sheet comes out already headed with the right departmental letterhead and auto-filled with the station name, the corporation name and the current time. There is also a slot you can feed an existing sheet into to run off duplicates of anything, including something you found or stole. Opening a printed form gives you a proper fillable document rather than a blank page: named blanks you click and type into, plus one-tap buttons that drop your character's real name into the signature line and your real job title into the position line, with a marked space at the bottom for stamps. So a lawyer can print a formal indictment, a doctor a death certificate or prescription authorization, the captain a certificate of commendation or a shuttle-call request, and the paper trail becomes something the crew actually passes around and stamps.

- **Take** — It gives every bureaucratic and command-adjacent role a concrete thing to do with a huge pre-written form library, turning "write a report" from a blank-page chore into a two-click printed document players will actually fill in and stamp.
- **Skip** — All 154 template documents are written in Russian markup and the visible strings live in Sunrise's own locale keys, so an English fork inherits a translation job roughly the size of the feature itself before a single form is readable; on top of that the fillable-field half hangs off upstream's PaperComponent/PaperWindow as partials, so it is not a clean drop-in, and the machine still needs map placement, a construction/lathe recipe and ink/paper resupply wired into your own station layouts.
- **Touches** — command, security, service, medical, cargo, science, engineering, all-players
- **Verified** — partly supported. Fetched all six cited files plus two more to settle the upstream question. categories.yml/templates.yml/pool_template.yml fully confirm the department tabs (Central Command x2, Command, Security, Justice, Medical, Science, Engineering, Service, Supply, Prison, Syndicate), the per-category letterhead header sprites, and ~140 real templates. CopyMachineComponent.cs confirms paper/ink consumption fields (PaperCost, IncCost), print duration, print sound, a CopySlot for feeding an existing sheet to duplicate it, and a Templates/TemplateCategoryGroupId filter list — matching the gameplay description closely. ApplicationEmployment.xml confirms {stationName}/{corpString}/{timeString} auto-fill tokens, [signature=]/[job=] fillable blanks, and a literal 'Место для печатей' (stamp placeholder) footer, and is written entirely in Russian, confirming the translation-burden claim. Crucially for graft_shape: PaperComponent.TemplateFields.cs lives under Content.Shared/_Sunrise/Paper/ but declares `namespace Content.Shared.Paper` and re-declares `PaperBoundUserInterfaceState` with extra fields (ImageContent, ImageScale, TemplateFieldsEnabled). I fetched Content.Shared/Paper/PaperComponent.cs (the plain, non-_Sunrise path) from this fork and compared it against the real upstream space-wizards file: upstream defines PaperBoundUserInterfaceState directly inside PaperComponent.cs, but this fork's copy of that same upstream-path file has had that nested class deleted so the _Sunrise partial can redefine it with more fields. That is a genuine in-place edit of an upstream-path file, not just a hypothetical integration cost — so upstream_reading is 'both': the fork's own tree already patches an upstream file, and any target codebase would need the equivalent edit to avoid a duplicate-type build break. This directly validates the seed's warning that path-only data can't see in-place upstream edits, and confirms (rather than overturns) the claim's own 'needs-upstream-edits' verdict.
  - unsupported: "you browse a library of about 150 ready-made official forms" — templates.yml contains 154 docTemplate blocks but 13 of those are abstract:true category-header templates (not user-selectable forms), leaving 141 actual templates; the pool actually wired into the copy machine (pool_template.yml, pool id "Sunrise") lists exactly 140 template IDs. The real number is ~140, not ~150.

### Ghost roles, respawn and game-master roles

<a id="gamemaster-and-ghost-roles"></a>

Ways for dead or waiting players to re-enter the round: extra ghost roles, respawn paths after a cooldown, and non-embodied roles that steer the shift from behind the scenes.

**ephemeral-space**

*loop-change · entangled · medium confidence · 9 forks carry it*

Players who are watching rather than playing can join a round as a "stagehand", a floating theatergoer with a randomised face who drifts through the station, sees in the dark, listens in on every radio channel, and can examine anyone but touch nothing. An Observe window lists everyone currently alive with their name, job and objectives, and a Warp button snaps you into following that person. Backstage you get a running feed of the shift's drama (who killed whom, who committed suicide, whose objective just succeeded or failed, who slipped into a new secret identity, who else joined the crew) with the bigger news printed in bigger text. Separately, dying puts you through a short cutscene and then ghosts you automatically after about fifteen seconds instead of leaving you lying in your body waiting.

- **Take** — It turns dead and waiting players from an idle ghost crowd into an engaged audience with a curated drama feed, a warp-to-anyone spectator menu, and a way to react audibly into the live round.
- **Skip** — The bundle is not just the spectator role: the auto-ghost half forcibly severs players from their bodies fifteen seconds after death with return-to-body disabled, which cuts across defib and cloning play, and the stagehand mob and join gate both reach into other Ephemeral Space systems (lobby theatergoer marker, voting, interaction blocking, death cutscene, viewcone) that would have to be stubbed or ported alongside it.

- **Needs** — [lobby-and-character-ui](ui.md#lobby-and-character-ui), [secret-identities](antag.md#secret-identities)
- **Verification** — none; read this as a claim, not a finding.

**sunrise**

*activity · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 9 forks carry it*

### Character records

**serbia-strong**

**sunrise**

*loop-change · entangled · medium confidence · 6 forks carry it*

Instead of upstream's simple "set status, add a crime line" security console, Sunrise gives security a full case-file workflow: an officer pulls up a person's record (name, job, age, species, fingerprints, DNA, current status), opens a numbered criminal case, ticks which corporate law articles were broken plus aggravating or mitigating circumstances, writes notes, and the console totals a recommended sentence in minutes with an itemised breakdown the warden can read line by line. Closed cases feed a separate prisoner management console showing who is queued for jail, who is currently serving time in which of ten cells (and whether that cell is occupied and properly kitted out), and who has finished or been paroled; starting an incarceration locks the cell door, starts a countdown on the cell's wall screen, and issues the prisoner a disposable access ID that opens only their own belongings locker. The warden can cut a sentence short with parole, and if someone gets out early the console flips them to Wanted, adds a ten-minute escape penalty to the sentence, and pushes a wanted alert into the security messenger channel. There is also a wearable visor with a click-a-person action that flags that person Wanted on the spot and broadcasts the reason.

- **Take** — It turns "brig them for a while" into an actual legal procedure with an auditable paper trail (laws cited, sentence arithmetic shown, cell timers, parole and escape handled by the console) which is exactly the structure security roleplay normally has to improvise by hand.

### Tape recorder and cassettes

### Psionics and the Glimmer

<a id="psionics-and-glimmer"></a>

Latent psychic powers (mind swap, pyrokinesis, telegnosis, dispel and more) gated behind a station-wide Glimmer meter that spawns hostile entities as it rises.

**einstein-engines**

*structural · entangled · high confidence · 4 forks carry it*

Some crew wake up psychic: at character setup you can pick a caster type (latent psychic, psycho-historian, elementalist, or the anti-psychic "insulated" option) and over the shift you roll powers from a pool that includes mind swap, mass sleep, telepathy, pyrokinesis, telekinetic pulse, telegnosis (send an invisible remote eye), psionic invisibility, healing word, revivify, xenoglossy (understand any language) and a detector pulse that finds other psychics nearby. Every power use pushes or pulls a station-wide "glimmer" meter, and as glimmer climbs the station gets weirder and more dangerous: machines visibly crackle, death messages change to souls leaving the body, noospheric storms are announced over the radio, and science can deliberately farm glimmer with a glimmer prober for research points while the talking statue Sophia broadcasts current readings over comms. Security gets a Psionics Registry console to register, suspect or flag crew as abusing psionics, chaplains and cargo get holy-water crates, and an Oracle entity trades divine gifts for whatever object it demands; some antagonist objectives are simply to drive glimmer past a threshold or to become psionic yourself.

- **Take** — It is a whole second layer of round pressure that no other system provides: a shared meter every player can push, which converts individual power use into escalating station-wide consequences and gives science, security and antagonists new reasons to care about each other.
- **Skip** — It is enormous (about 120 files here, 99 of them C#) and it assumes the fork's trait/point character setup, language system and Epistemics department restructure, so grafting it means importing several other large systems and then balancing a magic layer against every existing combat, medical and antag interaction forever.
- **Touches** — science, security, command, medical, service, antagonist, all-players

Going further than the cited evidence, I pulled the full repo tree and confirmed the entangled verdict directly: Resources/Prototypes/Roles/Jobs/departments.yml (outside any Psionics/Nyanotrasen namespace) is edited in place with a department literally named 'Epistemics' and the comment '# DeltaV - Epistemics Department replacing Science', folding Chaplain/Librarian into it; ForensicMantis' job prototype requires CharacterDepartmentTimeRequirement department: Epistemics; and the trait system (casterTypes.yml, feats.yml) leans on a points-based CharacterTraitRequirement/CharacterLogicOrRequirement framework that is its own large subsystem. ### Assigned roleplay imperatives

<a id="assigned-roleplay-moods"></a>

**impstation, starlight**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 6 forks carry it*

At the start of the shift you get a small set of secret personal rules you have to live by, opened from a button on your action bar: things like "You must always lie and can never admit it", "Swearing is the spice of any conversation", "Disagreements must be settled through a formal duel", "You must drink any puddle you see", or "Petty theft is morally neutral". Some rules are shared by everyone who has them that round (a station-wide belief like "the station is a living being", "music is sacred", or "the cargo hamster is a god and must be brought to the chapel for offerings"), so strangers discover they share the same weird conviction and start organising around it.
