**sunrise**

*activity · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 11 forks carry it*

The headline piece is a real playable tutorial, not a wall of text. A new player picks "Introduction" from a tutorial menu and is dropped alone onto a small purpose-built training map with a 15-minute timer, a progress bar and a path overlay pointing where to go. A speech bubble (with a matching chat line and spoken voice line) walks them through 31 numbered steps: walk, swap hands, find the locker room, buy a beret from the clothing vendor and wear it, drop what they were holding, sit on a bar stool, pick up and drink a juice, put the glass back, examine a locked airlock, rummage a bush to find the ID card that opens it, open a dresser, walk a dark hallway, grab and switch on a flashlight, bump a bolted door, press the signal button beside it, stow the flashlight, then pick up a practice pistol, enter combat mode, shoot targets and knife a clown dummy. Steps have preconditions and can skip themselves, and a completion window fires at the end. Separately, the station carries a real corporate lawset: numbered articles in 100-to-600 bands each with a base sentence in minutes, plus general provisions and aggravating/mitigating circumstance entries, and a threshold (default 50) above which the sentence becomes permanent. Security reads the sentence the station is actually running rather than a wiki page. The guidebook itself is a large fork-written library with its own server ruleset split into eleven numbered core rules plus roleplay sub-rules, each its own openable entry, and topic guides on antagonists, changelings, flesh cult, ammunition, expeditions, grenades and detonator triggers. Pressing help opens a chooser asking whether you want an admin or a mentor.

- **Take** — The scripted 31-step tutorial map is the most complete new-player onboarding in the catalogue: bubbles, voice lines, a guide path, per-step conditions and a completion screen, all data-driven so you can author your own sequences in YAML instead of C#.

- **Touches** — all-players, new-players, security, command, service

**sunrise**

*activity · needs upstream edits · high confidence · 11 forks carry it*

**ephemeral-space**

*loop-change · entangled · medium confidence · 9 forks carry it*

### Tape recorder and cassettes

<a id="tape-recorder"></a>

Handheld recorder that captures nearby speech onto swappable cassettes and plays it back, rewinds or prints a transcript. Cassettes and recorders only: playing cards, card decks and other tabletop props are `games-instruments-and-performance` even when a fork ships them in the same commit or directory.

**rmc14**

*activity · entangled · medium confidence · 5 forks carry it*

You slot a cassette into a handheld recorder and hit record; it picks up anything said within about ten metres and writes it onto the tape, keeping who spoke, whether they said/shouted/whispered it, and the exact timing. A tape holds twenty minutes per side, the recorder warns you when a minute is left, and it stops itself when the side fills. Flip the tape over for a fresh twenty minutes. Playback is the good part: the recorder speaks the recording back out loud in real time, in the original speakers' voices and speech verbs, and when the original conversation had a long gap it announces how many seconds of silence it is skipping, so bystanders hear the scene replayed rather than a wall of text. Examining the recorder shows its little readout: which tape is loaded, which side, how much is left. You can also print the whole thing as a paper transcript with timestamps, on a thirty-second cooldown, which makes a hearing or an accusation into a physical document you can hand over, plant or destroy. Tapes are sabotage targets too: a tape can be pulled apart into a tangle of unspooled ribbon that will not play until someone spends five seconds respooling it with a screwdriver. The same unit also carries a separate personal cassette player worn in the ear slot, with play/pause, skip and restart buttons and a visible sprite on your character, for listening to music tapes.

### Psionics and the Glimmer

<a id="psionics-and-glimmer"></a>

Latent psychic powers (mind swap, pyrokinesis, telegnosis, dispel and more) gated behind a station-wide Glimmer meter that spawns hostile entities as it rises.

**einstein-engines**

*structural · entangled · high confidence · 4 forks carry it*

Some crew wake up psychic: at character setup you can pick a caster type (latent psychic, psycho-historian, elementalist, or the anti-psychic "insulated" option) and over the shift you roll powers from a pool that includes mind swap, mass sleep, telepathy, pyrokinesis, telekinetic pulse, telegnosis (send an invisible remote eye), psionic invisibility, healing word, revivify, xenoglossy (understand any language) and a detector pulse that finds other psychics nearby. Every power use pushes or pulls a station-wide "glimmer" meter, and as glimmer climbs the station gets weirder and more dangerous: machines visibly crackle, death messages change to souls leaving the body, noospheric storms are announced over the radio, and science can deliberately farm glimmer with a glimmer prober for research points while the talking statue Sophia broadcasts current readings over comms. Security gets a Psionics Registry console to register, suspect or flag crew as abusing psionics, chaplains and cargo get holy-water crates, and an Oracle entity trades divine gifts for whatever object it demands; some antagonist objectives are simply to drive glimmer past a threshold or to become psionic yourself.

- **Take** — It is a whole second layer of round pressure that no other system provides: a shared meter every player can push, which converts individual power use into escalating station-wide consequences and gives science, security and antagonists new reasons to care about each other.
- **Skip** — It is enormous (about 120 files here, 99 of them C#) and it assumes the fork's trait/point character setup, language system and Epistemics department restructure, so grafting it means importing several other large systems and then balancing a magic layer against every existing combat, medical and antag interaction forever.
- **Touches** — science, security, command, medical, service, antagonist, all-players

**deltav**

Each psychic gets exactly one power out of a grab bag: mind-swapping into someone else's body, dropping everyone nearby into forced sleep, setting a target on fire with a thought, sending out a remote ghostly eye to scout, glimpsing a vision of a disaster that is about to hit the station, or dispelling other people's magic. ### Assigned roleplay imperatives

<a id="assigned-roleplay-moods"></a>

Players are handed randomly assigned behavioural rules or secret compulsions they must roleplay for the shift, surfaced in a dedicated panel and editable by admins.

**impstation, starlight**

*loop-change · needs upstream edits · medium confidence · 6 forks carry it*

At the start of the shift you get a small set of secret personal rules you have to live by, opened from a button on your action bar: things like "You must always lie and can never admit it", "Swearing is the spice of any conversation", "Disagreements must be settled through a formal duel", "You must drink any puddle you see", or "Petty theft is morally neutral". Some rules are shared by everyone who has them that round (a station-wide belief like "the station is a living being", "music is sacred", or "the cargo hamster is a god and must be brought to the chapel for offerings"), so strangers discover they share the same weird conviction and start organising around it. Most sets pair a "yes, and" compulsion with a "no, and" prohibition plus an occasional wildcard, one rule is usually "never reveal your rules", and admins can open the same panel to hand out, edit, or reroll someone's rules mid-round.

- **Take** — It is a huge amount of ready-written roleplay content (well over a hundred authored compulsions across four themed pools) sitting on a small, generic framework, and it changes how the whole shift feels without touching any existing mechanic.
- **Skip** — Almost every shipped mood is written in-fiction for Impstation's Thaven species ("Thaven pay close attention to appearances", "congregate with your fellow Thaven"), so grafting it means either importing that species too or rewriting the entire content pack; and it only pays off on a server whose players will actually roleplay a rule they can hide, since nothing in the code enforces compliance.
